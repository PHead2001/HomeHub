# Notifications, Reminders, and Push Delivery

## Purpose and Scope

This module owns household notification records, bell/history presentation, per-user read/dismiss state, FCM subscription and delivery, foreground/background handling, and deep-link navigation. Feature modules own the events and due calculations that create records.

## User-Facing Capabilities

- View recent active notifications from the header bell, grouped into Today, This week and Earlier. The bell is a popover with a real list (NO-20: every link and dismiss button is reachable with Tab; unread items carry a dot and the words "Unread"; new unread notifications are announced through a polite live region), sized to the screen (at most 26rem wide and 80% of the viewport tall; NO-16), with Mark all read (NO-14) and Dismiss all in its header and "N more in the notification center" when more than 10 are active. Its badge is 20 px with 12 px text. The bell is named "Notifications" (with the unread count), rings briefly and pops its badge when a new unread notification arrives (the badge's number ticks to the new count with `NumberTicker`; "9+" above nine), and shows an "All caught up" illustration when empty.
- Open seven-day history at `/notifications` (a `max-w-3xl` column; NO-16), grouped into Today, This week and Earlier, with Mark all read (every unread notification the member can see, whatever the filter; NO-14). It reads 100 notifications at a time (`CENTER_PAGE_SIZE`) with Load more (NO-21). Cards show the title, message, age, a plain-language source ("Home maintenance", "Warranty", "Chore assignment", "New member"...) and who dismissed or completed it; internal source names and expiry stamps are never shown.
- An empty Unread filter shows the shared `EmptyState` (notifications illustration, "All caught up" with a friendly line that stays the same all day); other empty filters offer "Show all".
- In the center, on a hovering pointer, a card's dismiss button appears on hover or when the card has keyboard focus; in the bell it is always shown (40 px, 48 px on touch). On touch screens (touch, or a pen on a coarse-pointer device) the dismiss button is always shown (48 × 48, like the filter buttons, through the shared `Button` sizing; on top of the card link, which never takes its taps) and swiping a card left dismisses it; a mouse drag does nothing. Only the card's visual layer slides; the card and its link stay in place, so taps land where they were aimed. With reduced motion nothing slides: the dismiss hint fades in over the card and a completed swipe dismisses it in place.
- Every dismissal (swipe, button, Dismiss visible, Dismiss all) shows a toast with Undo for 10 seconds (the shared `UNDO_WINDOW_MS`); Undo removes the member's own dismissal. Only one dismissal toast shows at a time (a newer dismissal replaces the older toast at once through the toast `group` `notification-dismiss`; other toasts may show beside it), so dismissals made within 10 seconds of each other accumulate: the newest toast counts them all ("2 notifications dismissed") and its Undo restores them all. The window runs from the latest dismissal (`src/app/notifications/undo-batch.ts`, one batch per household and member shared by the bell and the center).
- Pushes show the app icon (`/icons/icon-192.png`), a monochrome house badge for the Android status bar (`/icons/badge-96.png`), and, when the notification is about a pet or a product with a photo stored in the household, that photo as the large image.
- Filter unread, all, Household (addressed to everyone, `isHouseholdWide`; NO-13), dismissed, and the feature categories the member may see (`notificationFilterOptions`; NO-18: a child gets no Shopping, Maintenance or Automation chip). The chips stay at the top while scrolling and fade at the edge on phones to show there are more (NO-16). Filtering is `matchesNotificationFilter`. The Unread filter shows the unread count (a `NumberTicker`, same visibility rules as the list). Each group's cards enter staggered (`Stagger`, replayed when the filter changes, instant under reduced motion).
- On a hovering pointer, resting on a Notification Center card (or focusing its link from the keyboard) for 400 ms shows a peek card (`PeekCard`, H26) with the exact time, where the link opens (`notificationDestination`) and who completed it. The peek wraps the whole card, so the card and its link keep their own structure and hit targets; touch never shows it.
- Mark a notification read by opening it, in the app or from a push (NO-9: the push link carries `?n=<id>` and the page marks it read).
- Dismiss one or all active notifications without deleting history.
- Follow supported deep links to source pages.
- Turn browser push on or off for this device from Profile (NO-5, NO-17): the card shows the device's real state (registered, needs re-connecting, turned off, blocked, unsupported), explains before the browser's permission prompt, shows one outcome toast per action, and on an iPhone or iPad Safari tab explains adding HomeHub to the Home Screen first.
- See the unread count as a badge on the installed app's home-screen or taskbar icon where the browser supports the Badging API; while the app is closed, the service worker sets it to the number of HomeHub notifications in the tray (NO-27).

## Architecture and Data Flow

Feature code creates household notification documents with `buildNotificationDocument`. Bell and center queries parse legacy/current shapes, merge duplicate semantic identities for presentation, filter per-user target/read/dismiss state, and write only the current UID's map entries. Swipe handling lives in `src/app/notifications/swipe-dismiss.ts` (`useSwipeDismiss` plus pure helpers, shared by the bell and the center). Its pointer handlers sit on the card wrapper, which never moves; only the `data-swipe-visual` layer inside the link is translated. Cards keep vertical pan (`touch-pan-y`), capture the pointer only after 6 px of horizontal travel (so a tap still opens the link), swallow the click that ends a swipe (so a swipe that starts on the dismiss button, which sits where a left swipe naturally starts, does not also press it; a tap still does), and dismiss when released at least 70 px left (200 ms slide-out). Action entries (`readBy.<uid>`, `dismissedBy.<uid>`, maintenance `resolvedBy`) are built by `createNotificationAction` and hold `at`, `uid` and `displayName` only: every member who can read the notification sees them, so they no longer carry the email. Older entries may still have one; the center never shows it (who completed or dismissed something is the display name, else "Household member"). Dismissals go through `useDismissNotifications` (`src/app/notifications/use-dismiss-notifications.tsx`): one batch sets `dismissedBy.<own uid>`, wrapped in `awaitWriteUnlessOffline`, then a 10-second toast offers Undo, which deletes the same key with `deleteField()`. Both writes change only the caller's own key, which the existing rules allow.

Audience queries: every client list query of notifications carries `where('audience', 'array-contains-any', [uid, '*'])` (`audienceConstraint` in `src/app/notifications/notification-query.ts`): the bell, center and dashboard tile through `notificationWindowQuery`, and Maintenance Center's two reminder resolvers through `notificationsBySourceQuery`. A document whose `audience` names neither the member nor everyone, or that has no `audience` at all, is not returned, and the read rule refuses any list query that lacks this filter (see Authentication, Roles, and Security).

Category queries: for a member who cannot see every module, the bell, center and dashboard tile also put the category filter in the Firestore query (`where('category', 'in', viewable)` with `system` and `general` always included; `src/app/notifications/notification-query.ts`), so the bell's 30-item cap counts only notifications they can see and a busy hidden category cannot push theirs out. Full-access members keep the unfiltered query. Stored categories are always one of the seven known values. Composite indexes in `firestore.indexes.json`: (`audience` array-contains, `createdAt` DESC) for the full-access window, (`audience` array-contains, `category` ASC, `createdAt` DESC) for the category-filtered one, (`audience` array-contains, `sourceType` ASC) for a deleted asset's or vehicle's reminders and (`audience` array-contains, `sourceType` ASC, `sourceId` ASC) for one schedule's reminders. The older (`category` ASC, `createdAt` DESC) index is no longer used by the app.

Category visibility (`functions/src/shared/push-audience.ts`, shared by the push sender, bell, center and dashboard tile): notifications in a module category need that module's view permission (`chores` → `chores.view`, `pets` → `pets.view`, `shopping` → `shopping.view`, `maintenance` → `maintenance.view`, `automation` → `automation.view`); `system` and `general` need none. In the app this uses the member's effective permissions; on the server `memberCanView` mirrors the rules (owner always, explicit override wins, then the role default; a unit test checks it against `getEffectivePermissions` for every role). A member who lacks the permission sees none of that category in the bell, center or unread tile and gets no push for it, not even text.

The active notifications are `activeNotifications` (not expired, visible to the member, allowed by category permission when the caller passes `canView`, unresolved, not dismissed by them), applied after `deduplicateNotifications`; the bell lists the newest 10 (`activeBellNotifications`, `BELL_LIMIT`) but its badge, the dashboard's unread tile (`unreadBellCount`) and the center's Unread filter and count all count every active unread one before any cap, and the bell's Dismiss all dismisses every active one (NO-7; resolved notifications are never unread); the dashboard's unread tile uses the same steps, so duplicate legacy copies are counted once and a read or dismissal on either copy applies to both. Presentation helpers in `src/lib/dashboard/notification-display.ts` also group items by local day (`today` since local midnight, `week` for the six days before, `earlier`) and map `sourceType` values to plain labels (unknown values show nothing rather than the raw name). Bell ring and badge pop are CSS animations from `src/components/dashboard/dashboard.module.css`, keyed so they replay only when the unread count increases after the first load; both are removed under reduced motion. Load failures in the center show `friendlyError` text; dismiss failures use `toastError`.

A Firestore create trigger resolves recipients, sends FCM, and removes invalid registration tokens. Foreground messages become toasts. `/api/sw` serves HomeHub's single service worker: Firebase Messaging background display and deep-link clicks, plus the offline app shell owned by [Application Shell and Dashboard](../application-shell/README.md). Production builds register it on every page load through `PushNotificationManager`; `PushNotificationSettings` registers the same URL and scope (`src/lib/service-worker.ts`) on push opt-in and so receives the same registration for `getToken`. Background display: when a payload carries a `notification` block, the Messaging SDK shows it itself (with the server's icon, badge, image and tag); the worker's `onBackgroundMessage` then shows the same notification with the same `tag`, which replaces the SDK's copy instead of stacking a second one, and routes clicks to the worker's own handler. That display uses `icon` and `image` from `payload.notification` and `badge` and `tag` from `payload.data`, re-checked in the worker: icons and badges only from this origin's `/icons/`, images only as https Firebase Storage download URLs under `households/` in the configured bucket or image paths on this origin, falling back to `/icons/icon-192.png` and `/icons/badge-96.png`. The worker's own display adds the notification's ID to the link (`pushOpenLink` in `functions/src/shared/push-link.ts`, embedded like `safePushLink` and re-checked by it: `?n=<id>`, or `&n=<id>` before any `#`, only for IDs of letters, digits, `_` and `-`). `notificationclick` opens only `/`-relative in-app paths; anything else opens `/`. It reuses an open HomeHub window (NO-9, SL-16): the first same-origin window (`includeUncontrolled`) is focused and, unless it already shows that exact link, `navigate()`d to it; when there is none, or navigating fails (an uncontrolled window rejects it), a new window opens. `PushForegroundListener` reads `?n=` on load, removes it from the address (`history.replaceState`, other parameters kept) and writes the member's own `readBy.<uid>` entry; a notification not addressed to them is refused by the rules and only logged. The check is `safePushLink` in `functions/src/shared/push-link.ts`, the same function the push function uses as `safeDeepLink` for `fcmOptions.link`; the route imports it through `src/app/api/sw/push-link.ts` and embeds its source in the worker (`safePushLink.toString()`). It is self-contained and ASCII-only with no named inner functions, because build tools may wrap those in helpers the worker lacks (tsx's `__name`); the production build's minified copy was checked to run correctly. Beyond the old rule (one leading slash, no `//`) it refuses control characters (URL parsing drops tabs and newlines, so `/<tab>/evil.com` would open as `//evil.com`), backslashes and `.`/`..` segments, raw or percent-encoded, malformed percent-encoding, and anything that does not resolve to the same origin. `tests/unit/notifications-push-link.test.ts` runs one hostile-input table against `safeDeepLink`, the module the route imports and the function embedded in the served worker. If the Firebase scripts cannot be imported (CDN blocked or offline at install), the worker still installs without push and retries on its next update.

`NotificationBell` passes its unread count to `useAppBadge`, which calls `navigator.setAppBadge(count)` (or `clearAppBadge()` at zero) when the Badging API exists. The badge is left unchanged while notifications are loading or failed to load, and cleared when the bell unmounts (sign-out, or loss of `notifications.view`). While HomeHub is closed, the service worker sets the badge after each background push, again after a notification click, and when a notification is dismissed from the tray (`notificationclose`, kept alive with `waitUntil`), to the number of HomeHub notifications shown in the tray (`registration.getNotifications()`), clearing it at zero (NO-27); the open app replaces that with the exact unread count. Failures (no badge permission) are ignored.

## Data Model and Persistence

- Notifications: `households/{householdId}/notifications/{notificationId}`.
- Push tokens: `users/{email}/private/push` `{ fcmTokens: string[], updatedAt }`, owner-only. `registerPushOnThisDevice` (`src/lib/push-registration.ts`, called by `PushNotificationSettings` on opt-in and by the silent registration below) gets the device token with `getToken`, checks that the account is still the signed-in one, reads that document, computes the new list with `nextPushTokens` (stored tokens oldest first, then legacy profile tokens, then this device last, de-duplicated; when that would exceed the rules' cap of 20 the oldest tokens are dropped, so subscribing never fails on the cap), and writes it in one batch (through `awaitWriteUnlessOffline`) that also deletes the legacy profile field. Two devices of the same person subscribing at the same instant can overwrite each other's token; that device re-registers on its next subscribe. A failed subscribe from the Profile card shows the component's own wording for an unsupported browser, a missing VAPID key ("not set up yet", the variable name only in the console) or no token; any other error goes through `friendlyError`, never raw text.
- This device's registration: `localStorage["homehub.push-device"]` `{ v: 1, token, email, at }`, written after every successful registration (`email` is the account whose private document holds `token`, `at` the time it was last confirmed with FCM). It exists so sign-out can remove the token without asking FCM for it. It is never sent anywhere and holds nothing the browser's own FCM storage does not hold. An empty `email` marks a sign-out that could not finish removing the registration; when no token was remembered at that point the placeholder token `"unknown"` is stored with it.
- Sign-out (`logout`, see [Identity and Profile](../identity-profile/README.md)): before anything else `logout` closes the sign-out gate (`pushSignOutGate.begin()`), then runs `runPushSignOut` while still signed in. The whole push cleanup, including downloading `src/lib/push-registration.ts` and Firebase Messaging, shares one 2 s budget (`settleWithin`); it never throws and never holds sign-out up longer, and logs a console warning on failure or timeout.
  - Nothing is loaded when this device has no device record, no registration in flight and no browser push subscription (`hasPushSubscription`): never subscribed, unsupported browser such as an iOS Safari tab, or no service worker.
  - Otherwise `unregisterPushOnThisDevice` first waits for registrations in flight (`pushSignOutGate.drain()`), then removes the remembered token from the signed-in account's document with `arrayRemove` (only if the record names that account) and, in parallel, revokes the device registration: Firebase Messaging `deleteToken`, then the service worker's push subscription is unsubscribed directly if it is still there, so the old token cannot deliver to this browser even when FCM could not be told. This also runs when a subscription exists without a device record (a device subscribed before the record existed, or an interrupted registration); that token stays in the account's document until a push to it fails and the push function removes it. `deleteToken` is pointed at HomeHub's existing worker registration (`navigator.serviceWorker.getRegistration('/')`), because without one the SDK would try to register its default worker file, which HomeHub does not serve; no `getToken` runs at sign-out.
  - When everything succeeded the device record is deleted. Otherwise (failure, the budget ran out, or the browser is offline, in which case the network work is skipped) the record is kept with an empty `email`, or the `"unknown"` placeholder is written when there was no record, so the next sign-in revokes the device registration before registering.
  - Registrations racing sign-out: every registration (Profile opt-in or silent) takes a pass from the gate when it starts and checks it after `getToken`, after reading the token document and after the write (`pushRegistrationStep`). Once sign-out has begun, a pass taken earlier is never valid again and no new registration starts until sign-out is over. A token that arrives after sign-out began is not stored and the device registration is revoked; if the token was already written, it is removed from the account again (`arrayRemove`, possible only while still signed in) and revoked, and if either step fails the token is kept in the device record with an empty `email`. The gate reopens when a sign-out ends without a page reload (emulator mode, or Firestore could not be stopped); after a reload the module state is new. The gate is per tab: a registration running in another tab of the same browser is not stopped by this tab's sign-out.
- Silent registration after sign-in (`syncPushForSignedInUser`, run by `PushForegroundListener` once per signed-in account after the page is idle): browser permission stays "granted" after sign-out, so `pushSignInAction` decides without prompting. Permission not granted, or nobody signed in: nothing. Permission granted and no device record (after a sign-out, or a device that subscribed before the record existed): register a token for the signed-in account. Record for this account older than 7 days: register again, which confirms the token and, if FCM changed it, replaces the old one in the list. Record for this account and recent: the account's push document is read and, when it no longer holds this device's token (the push function removed it after FCM reported it invalid), the device registers again (NO-5, `pushTokenMissing`). Push turned off on this device by this account (`localStorage["homehub.push-off"]`, a JSON list of the accounts that turned it off here, one entry each, so on a shared browser each person's choice is kept; an older single-email value is read as a one-entry list; `readPushOff`): nothing is registered, and when that account's "Turn off" did not finish (`homehub.push-off-pending`, same format, `readPushOffPending`) the removal runs again (`pushSignInAction` gives `cleanup`, whatever the permission) until it succeeds. `PushForegroundListener` runs this sync on each signed-in load and again when the browser comes back online. Record for another account or with an empty `email`: revoke the device registration first (as on sign-out), then register, so the new account gets a token the previous account never held; if the old push subscription cannot be removed, nothing is registered and the next page load tries again. A token is only ever written for the account that is signed in at the moment of the write. Failures log a console warning; offline, the step is skipped.
- Legacy push tokens: `users/{email}.fcmTokens`. Approved housemates can read the whole profile, so tokens no longer go there; the rules let the field only shrink or be removed. `scripts/ops/migrate-push-tokens.mjs --project <id>` (dry run, counts only) and `--apply` move the remaining ones, one transaction per profile. Until it has run, the push function reads both places.
- `users/{email}/notifications/{notificationId}` is a legacy inbox: its owner may read, update and delete it, and rules deny creation by anyone.

`Notification`, `NotificationCategory`, `NotificationUserAction`, and `NotificationUserActionMap` define categories, source/deep-link fields, optional `stateKey`, targeting, UID-keyed `readBy`/`dismissedBy`, and optional resolution metadata. `expiresAt` defaults to seven days after `createdAt`; parsing supplies that fallback for older records.

Targeting: the web app's `buildNotificationDocument` takes `targetUser: { uid }` and writes `targetUserUid` only, and upkeep's chore reminders no longer write `targetUserEmail` either: the field named the target's email to every member who can read the document. Every reader (bell, center, dashboard, AI overview, push sender, audience derivation) prefers `targetUserUid`; older documents that carry only `targetUserEmail` were backfilled with an `audience` and keep working until they expire (within seven days). The rules refuse `targetUserEmail` on every client create, whatever the `audience` (the create rule requires `targetUserUid` when there is a target).

Stored email copies (member privacy release C): `scripts/ops/scrub-shared-emails.mjs` (dry run by default, `--apply`; counts only; update-time preconditions; re-runnable) removes `targetUserEmail` from live notifications and the `email` inside `readBy.<uid>`, `dismissedBy.<uid>` and `resolvedBy` entries written before release A. A notification that also names its member by `targetUserUid` just loses the email (and gets `audience: [uid]` when it has none). An email-only one is first given `targetUserUid` (and `audience: [uid]` when missing or empty), taken from its one-entry `audience` or the household's members (contact documents first), so it never turns household-wide; one whose target is no longer a member keeps its `targetUserEmail` (removing it would address everyone) and expires within seven days. Pure decisions in `scripts/ops/shared-email-scrub.mjs` (`notificationEmailScrubPlan`), covered by `tests/unit/shared-email-scrub.test.ts`.

`audience` (list of strings) names who a notification is for: `[targetUserUid]` when it targets one member, `['*']` when it is household-wide. It is written at creation by every producer, the web app builder included, each through `notificationAudience` in `functions/src/shared/notification-audience.ts` (shared by the Functions code and the app, like `push-audience.ts`):

- the web app's `buildNotificationDocument` (chore assignment in the Chores page and quick add);
- the upkeep function (`functions/src/reminders.ts`): maintenance and pet medication reminders are `['*']`, chore reminders `[uid]`. A refresh leaves the stored value alone and a reopen writes it again; both add it to a reminder written before the field existed;
- the household server actions (`src/lib/server/household-membership.ts`): the new-member and member-left notices and the daily pending-member reminder, one per approver, `[approver uid]`; "You're in" and "Your role changed", `[uid]` of the person concerned; the ownership-transfer notice, `['*']`;
- the e2e seed (`scripts/e2e/seed.mjs`).

Every current producer knows the target's uid. For a legacy email-only target the derivation gives the member's uid when the caller can look it up and `[]` (nobody) when it cannot; it never falls back to `['*']`.

Consumers decide from `audience`. The client queries filter on it and the read rule enforces it (see audience queries above). Behind them, one check, `isAddressedToMember` in `functions/src/shared/notification-audience.ts`, decides who a document is for in the bell and center (`isNotificationVisibleToUser`, kept as defence in depth), the dashboard tile, the AI overview and the push sender: a stored `audience` decides on its own (it names the member's uid or `'*'`; `[]` is nobody); a document without it falls back to `targetUserUid`, then `targetUserEmail` (case-insensitive), then everyone. Documents written before the field existed (they expire within seven days) get it from `scripts/ops/backfill-notification-audience.mjs --project <id>` (dry run, counts only) and `--apply`: `[targetUserUid]`, else the uid of the household member with the legacy target email, else `['*']` for an untargeted document, else `[]`. It updates only that field in batches of at most 400, skips documents that already have it or were deleted meanwhile, and is safe to re-run. Documents it has not reached are missing from the client queries and unreadable by clients; server code (Admin SDK: upkeep, push, AI overview, household actions) still reads them.


## Authentication, Roles, and Security

A member with `notifications.view` may get or list a household notification only when its `audience` names them or everyone (`resource.data.audience.hasAny([request.auth.uid, '*'])`); owners and admins are no exception. A single-document read of a notification aimed at someone else, of one with an empty `audience`, or of one without the field is refused, and a list query is accepted only when it is constrained by `audience` `array-contains-any [uid, '*']` (or `array-contains` of either value). Updates need the same audience: only a member the document is addressed to may change it, and only the caller UID key in `readBy` (with `notifications.view`: guests can mark read, NO-8; `updatesOnlyOwnReadState`), the caller UID key in `readBy` and `dismissedBy` together (with `notifications.dismiss`), or, for a maintenance document, `resolvedAt`/`resolvedBy` (with `maintenance.edit`). Deletion is denied. The rules do not inspect the action values; the app stopped writing emails into them without a rules change. Admin SDK code is not subject to rules.

A registered wall tablet (device principal, no membership) reads only household-wide (`['*']`) notices, through one query per source (`where('audience', 'array-contains', '*')`, `where('category', '==', c)`): `system` and `general` always, `chores`, `pets`, `shopping` and `maintenance` while that section is on for the tablet; any other query shape, a targeted notice and `automation` are refused (see [Registered Wall Tablets](../devices/README.md)). Tablets write no notification state. Device management sends targeted `system` notices: "Wall tablet paired" / "Wall tablet set up again" to the code's creator (`device-paired-<deviceId>-<generation>`, `sourceType` `device-paired`) and "Wall tablet removed" to each active owner and admin (`device-removed-<deviceId>-<generation>-<uid>`, `sourceType` `device-removed`), both linking to `/household?tab=devices`.

`notifications.view` controls navigation, reads and marking read; `notifications.dismiss` controls dismissing (NO-8). The UI mirrors the rules: without `notifications.dismiss` (the Guest role by default) the bell and center show no dismiss buttons, no swipe, no Dismiss all and no Dismiss visible, while opening a notification still marks it read. Action-map keys are UID-restricted and action values may not carry an email.

Approved household members can read household notification documents. Rule updates permit only the caller UID key in `readBy` (view) and `dismissedBy` (dismiss); deletion is denied. When an update sets the caller's own entry it must be a map with only `at`, `uid` and `displayName` (`validNotificationAction`); removing it (Undo) is allowed, and other people's older entries do not block the update. Maintenance records may also update `resolvedAt`/`resolvedBy`, where `resolvedBy` has the same shape and names the caller's uid.

Creation rules require an approved sender, the household's own `householdId`, Timestamp `createdAt`/`expiresAt`, a known category, bounded title/message, an allow-listed set of fields, a `/`-relative `deepLink` (no scheme, no `//`), and a target that belongs to the household (a `targetUserUid` with a membership record). `targetUserEmail` is not an allowed field. The push function independently re-validates targets and links.

`audience` is required on every client create and must be exactly `[targetUserUid]` on a document with a `targetUserUid` (a member of the household) and `['*']` on an untargeted one, the derivation of `notificationAudience`. A client may not create a notification with only a `targetUserEmail` (it could name nobody), and anything else (missing, another uid, extra entries, an empty or non-list value) is denied, so a client cached from before the field existed can no longer create notifications. Clients cannot add, change or remove it afterwards: the two permitted updates touch only `readBy`/`dismissedBy` or `resolvedAt`/`resolvedBy`. There is no client departure-notice create rule: the member-left notices are written by the leave server action (see [Household Governance](../household-governance/README.md)).


## Integrations and Background Processing

- Home Assistant notices (AU-16, AU-17; [Home Automation](../home-automation/README.md)): the server writes household-wide (`audience ['*']`) notices with deterministic ids when it reads fresh states: `automation` for leaks, smoke and alert rules (`ha-leak-*`, `ha-safety-*`, `ha-rule-*`) and `maintenance` for low batteries and filters (`ha-battery-*`, `ha-filter-*`, at most monthly per device). During household quiet hours they carry `silent: true` except leaks and smoke. The "Automation" category in the center and the push audience (`automation.view`) now has a sender.

- FCM web config uses `NEXT_PUBLIC_FIREBASE_*` and `NEXT_PUBLIC_FIREBASE_VAPID_KEY`.
- Held pushes (NO-3, audit 16.3; pure rules in `functions/src/shared/push-hold.ts`): a notification is always written at once and shows in the bell; only its push may wait, per recipient, in the household's time zone (D-6). `pushHoldUntil` holds a push when (a) the document has `holdUntilMorning: true` and it is before 07:00 (`MORNING_PUSH_MINUTES`): upkeep sets it on maintenance reminders and on medication reminders without a set dose time (`timed: false` from `buildMedicationReminders`), whose state changes at local midnight; a dose with a set time pushes at that time; (b) the document is household-wide (`['*']`) and the household's `quietHours` (the wall-tablet setting) are on now: held until they end; holds that follow one another add up (quiet hours ending 06:30 then the 07:00 morning hour). When clocks go back and the end time happens twice, a hold made during the repeated hour ends at the next occurrence still ahead that day (`nextLocalTime`), not the day after. `deliverPush` records each held recipient as `pushHeld.<uid>` (when) and the earliest as `pushHeldNext`, and sends to the others. Hourly upkeep (`releaseHeldPushes` in `functions/src/held-pushes.ts`, after each household's reminders) finds documents with `pushHeldNext` due, and in a transaction (`releaseHeld`) drops the holds of anyone who has read or dismissed it and every hold of a resolved or expired document, and every hold of a medication reminder in its "due" state once the "overdue" reminder for the same dose exists (`supersedingStateKey`; both stay pending for one dose, so it is pushed once, not twice at 07:00), keeps the later ones, and writes `pushRelease: { at, uids }` for the due ones; `sendPushOnRenotify` then sends to exactly those members (`releasedUids` in `functions/src/push-policy.ts`; `pushRelease.at` must move, and never for a resolved document), re-checking holds at that moment. So a reminder resolved before the morning is never pushed, and a release is sent once. Clients cannot create or change `holdUntilMorning`, `pushHeld`, `pushHeldNext` or `pushRelease` (create allow-list; updates touch only read/dismiss entries), checked in `tests/rules/notifications-section7.rules.test.ts`. Tests: `tests/unit/notifications-push-hold.test.ts`, `tests/server/held-pushes.test.ts`.
- Cloud Function `sendPushOnNewNotification` runs on notification creation, except for a document created with `silent: true` (written by upkeep, for the overdue-chores notification, and by the Home Assistant alerts during household quiet hours; the rules' create allow-list does not accept the field from clients). `sendPushOnRenotify` runs on notification updates and pushes only when `renotifyAt` changed to a new value on an unresolved document (`pushOnCreate` / `pushOnUpdate` in `functions/src/push-policy.ts`); read, dismiss, refresh and resolve updates return at once, and clients cannot write `renotifyAt` (they may change only their own read and dismiss entries). Both send through the same `deliverPush`, which reads each recipient's tokens with the Admin SDK from `users/{email}/private/push` and from the legacy `users/{email}.fcmTokens` field, de-duplicated, and removes an invalid or unregistered token from every document that holds it.
- The push function re-validates every document: it ignores documents whose `householdId` does not match their path, loads the household's membership records, and pushes only to approved active members. An `audience` uid or `targetUserUid`/`targetUserEmail` that is not an approved member receives nothing; untargeted notifications go to approved members only (never pending joiners). Recipients are then limited to members who hold the category's view permission (see category visibility above). Links are reduced to `/`-relative in-app paths by the shared `safePushLink` check (see the service worker above), and title/body are bounded (`functions/src/push-policy.ts`, tested in `tests/server/push-policy.test.ts`).
- Push artwork (`functions/src/shared/push-media.ts`): every push sets `webpush.notification.icon` `/icons/icon-192.png`, `badge` `/icons/badge-96.png` (the house glyph from the app icon on transparency) and `tag` (the notification ID); `webpush.data` repeats `tag` and `badge` for the worker. A rich `image` is added only when the notification names a household record with a photo, read with the Admin SDK only under `households/{householdId}` from the trigger path (and only when the document's `householdId` matches it):
  - category `pets`, `sourceType` `pet` or starting `pet-`/`pet_` (for feeding and medication reminders, for example `pet_feeding`): `sourceId` is the pet ID, optionally followed by `:` or `|` and a state key; uses the pet's `photoUrl`.
  - category `shopping`, `sourceType` `shopping_item`: `sourceId` is `listId:itemId`; uses the item's `imageUrl`, else its barcode's library `imageUrl`.
  - category `shopping`, `sourceType` `barcode_library` or `product`: `sourceId` is the barcode; uses the library item's `imageUrl`.
  - The URL is used only if it is an https `firebasestorage.googleapis.com` media download URL for this project's default bucket whose object path is `households/{householdId}/pets/...` (pets) or `households/{householdId}/barcode-library/...` (shopping), with no credentials, port or path tricks, or an image path on the app origin. Placeholders, Open Food Facts and any other host, other buckets, other households and other folders are dropped, as are malformed IDs; any read failure means no image.
  - Recipients who could not open the record in the app (no `pets.view` / `shopping.view`) already get no push for that category; the image check is kept as a second guard and would send such a recipient the push without the image, as a separate multicast. Title and body are unchanged, so a push never reveals more than the notification text plus a picture the recipient can already see.
  - Pet medication reminders (below) use this contract; no generator writes shopping-item notifications yet. Tested in `tests/unit/notifications-push-media.test.ts` and against the emulator in `tests/server/push-media.test.ts`.
- Reminders are never written by browser code: there is no per-minute timer on Chores and no synchronization when Maintenance opens. Opening Chores does call the server-side `syncHouseholdUpkeep` action, which runs the full upkeep step (recurring chores, retention, maintenance and chore reminders) on demand. Direct Temporary Task creation writes one deterministic targeted assignment notification in the same batch as its chore.
- Pending-member reminders: when someone with `household.manageMembers` opens Household Manager and a pending person asked to join 24 hours ago or more and has not been reminded about for 24 hours, the page calls the `remindPendingMembersAction` server action, which stamps `lastNewUserReminderAt` and writes one `system` notification per person, day and approver (deterministic ID, targeted to each holder of `household.manageMembers`). Approving or denying the request resolves the open join-request and reminder notices about that person. See [Household Governance](../household-governance/README.md).
- Medication reminders: hourly upkeep (`householdUpkeep`, and `syncHouseholdUpkeep` when Chores opens) reads every pet with its `medication-courses` and all of its `medication-logs` (`functions/src/shared/medication-reminders.ts`). **Courses** (P3, see [Pets](../pets/README.md), "Medication courses"): one reminder per scheduled occurrence, for the course's next due occurrence (the shared schedule model, `functions/src/shared/medication-schedule.ts`) once its time, or with no set time its household day, has come: `sourceId` `<petId>:course:<courseId>`, `stateKey` `due|<occurrence key>` on its household day or `overdue|<occurrence key>` after it, title "Maple's Apoquel 8:00 PM dose is due" / "Maple's Apoquel dose is overdue", message "Planned dose: 1 tablet. Tap to record it once it's given.", deep link `/pets/<petId>?tab=medication&dose=<courseId>` (opens Give dose). A finished or ended course has none; its open reminder resolves on the next run. **Legacy medications** (entries without a course whose name no course has taken over, D-P8) keep the countdown rule of `currentMedications` / `useMedicationCountdownLogs`: entries are grouped by trimmed, lower-cased medication name, the most recent entry with a readable `date` decides, and a later dose without a valid `nextDueDate` cancels the countdown; message "Open Maple's Medication tab to record the dose once it's given, or to confirm its schedule." An open medication reminder whose title, message or link no longer match (a pet or course renamed) is updated in place, without a new push (PE-30d). When the due day is today or past in the household time zone, it writes one household-wide (untargeted, like maintenance) `pets` notification, which only members with `pets.view` see or are pushed per countdown state: `sourceType` `pet_medication`, `sourceId` `<petId>:<medication key>`, `stateKey` `due|<day>` or `overdue|<day>`, deterministic ID from `deterministicNotificationId`, title "Maple's flea prevention is due today" / "... is overdue", deep link `/pets/<petId>?tab=medication`. Re-runs create nothing; still-open reminders get the usual refresh before expiry. An open reminder is resolved (`resolvedAt`, no `resolvedBy`) once its countdown (medication and due day, or course and occurrence) is no longer pending: a newer dose was logged, the course was finished, ended or rescheduled past it, a course took the legacy name over, or the pet was deleted (the Pets delete action, D-P12, also resolves a deleted pet's open reminders at once). The resolve transaction re-reads the pet, all its medication entries and its courses and resolves only if the countdown is still not pending, so a run working from stale entries never hides a reminder that an overlapping run reopened. If the countdown becomes pending again (the newer dose is deleted), the same reminder is reopened (see reopening below). The push carries the pet's photo when it is stored in the household's Storage folder (see push artwork above).
- Reopening: when upkeep finds a reminder actionable again whose deterministic document is resolved (medication: the dose that resolved it was deleted; maintenance: resolved in the app but the schedule is still due with the same state, for example the schedule write failed), it reopens that document: `resolvedAt` and `resolvedBy` become `null`, title, message and link are rewritten, and `createdAt`/`expiresAt` move as on a refresh, so it is listed again. Being actionable again, it is new again for everyone (NO-11): `readBy` and `dismissedBy` are emptied and `firstCreatedAt` restarts. It reopens only if the document's last write was already visible to the run's source reads: its Firestore `updateTime` is at or before the earliest server `readTime` of the run's first source queries (members, chores, templates, assets, vehicles), compared exactly as server timestamps (`shouldReopen`, `earliestReadTime`), never against the Functions clock. So a resolution written while a run is in flight is never undone by that run's older reads, and a resolution committed just before the run is reopened regardless of millisecond order or clock skew. A reopen is an update, so it sends no push: members already got one for this same reminder. Daily chore reminders share this path but are never resolved; the overdue-chores notification below has its own resolve and reopen with the same `shouldReopen` check.
- House messages (NO-25): an active owner or admin (by role) writes a short message on Household > Overview ("Message the house", `src/components/household/house-message-card.tsx`), choosing how long it stays up (2, 6, 12, 24 or 72 hours) and whether to push it to phones. `postHouseAnnouncementAction` (`src/app/household/announcement-actions.ts`, personal sessions only) calls `postHouseAnnouncement` (`src/lib/server/house-announcements.ts`), which re-reads the household and the caller's membership in a transaction and writes one `general` notification (`sourceType: 'house-announcement'`, `audience: ['*']`, title "House message from <public name>" or "House message", the trimmed text, at most 200 characters, no control characters except newlines, `validateHouseAnnouncement` in `src/lib/house-announcements.ts`; `expiresAt` the chosen time; `createdByUid` the poster; `silent: true` unless a push was asked for) plus an activity entry `household.message_posted` (`hours`, `push`; no email). Members see it in the bell and under the center's Household filter until it expires; a push to phones waits for household quiet hours like any household push. Registered wall tablets pin the newest unexpired one in their top bar (`pinnedAnnouncement`), read-only: tapping it opens Household notices. Tests: `tests/unit/house-announcements.test.ts`, `tests/server/house-announcements.test.ts`.
- Daily digest (NO-22): each person's daily chore reminder (`${uid}-reminder-${day}`, created at their reminder time, pushed once) is their digest for the day: chores due that day and overdue, plus pet doses due or overdue that day (the household's open medication reminders, counted only for members with `pets.view`; `dailyDigestFor` in `functions/src/shared/chore-reminders.ts`, title for example "2 chores due today" or "1 pet dose due today", message "2 chores due today and 1 overdue. 1 pet dose due today."). After it is sent, every upkeep run rewrites today's digest in place when what is left changes (title and message only: no push, read and dismissed state kept), resolves it when nothing is left, and makes it active again (`resolvedAt: null`) if something comes back, only when the document's last write was visible to the run's reads (`shouldReopen`). A digest for yesterday (a reminder time after the day's last run) carries chores only.
- Overdue chores (NO-1): one notification per person, document `${uid}-overdue` (`sourceType` `chore-reminder`, `sourceId` `overdue`, category `chores`, `targetUserUid` and `audience: [uid]`, title naming the chore or the count (NO-19: "Sweep floor is overdue", "3 overdue chores"; `overdueTitle`), message `"Sweep floor" is overdue.` or `You have 3 overdue chores.`, deep link `/chores?due=overdue`), updated in place by hourly upkeep and `syncHouseholdUpkeep` (`syncOverdueNotification` in `functions/src/overdue-chores.ts`, deciding with the pure `decideOverdue`). Overdue means the person's incomplete chores due before their local today (`overdueChoresFor`, newest due day first). The document stores `overdueChoreIds` (what it lists: at most 500, the newest due days), `overdueTotal` (how many are overdue, the count in the message; a change in it alone, such as completing a chore past the 500, updates the message in place without a push and keeps read and dismissed state), `overflowFrom` (when the list is capped, the due day of its oldest listed chore), `notifiedChoreIds` (every listed chore plus, oldest first, up to 200 chores the person was told about that have since left the list; IDs of chores that no longer exist are dropped, and listed chores are never dropped) and `lastPushDay` (their local day of the last push, or of the daily reminder that carried it). Per run:
  - Times and days use the household's time zone for everyone (D-6; the `timeZone` a member's device wrote to their profile is ignored by upkeep).
  - A person who turned overdue alerts off in Profile (`choreSettings.overdueAlerts: false`, NO-4) is never pushed about overdue chores: news is still written (unread, listed, `lastPushDay` set), created `silent` or updated without `renotifyAt`.
  - Before 7:00 household time nothing new is announced; the list may only shrink. Exception: a daily reminder created in the run (even before 7:00) has just announced the overdue count, so the document records those chores at once, silently.
  - A chore not in `notifiedChoreIds` joined the list: the document is created, or updated with the new list and message, `readBy` and `dismissedBy` emptied, and `createdAt`/`expiresAt` moved to now, so it is unread and listed again. It is pushed (on creation by the create trigger, on an update by writing `renotifyAt`) unless the person already had a push that local day or their daily reminder covers it: sent in this run, or still to come later today (its message states the overdue count, for example "1 chore due today and 2 overdue."). Then a new document is created with `silent: true`, and `lastPushDay` still becomes today. A daily reminder sent in a run sets `lastPushDay` to today even when the list is unchanged. So a push happens only when the set grows, at most once a day, and the daily reminder counts as that day's push: a chore that becomes overdue after it went out (for example after a 06:00 reminder) is listed as new, unread, but is not pushed until a later day brings another new chore.
  - Past the cap: a chore that is counted but not listed is never news on its own. A newly overdue chore is due most recently, so it is always listed; one that joins with an old due day (for example reassigned to the person) only raises the count, quietly, and is not announced again on later days. When listed chores are completed and an unlisted one moves into the list, it is due on or before the stored `overflowFrom`, so it is not news either.
  - A chore counts as new only under an ID the person was not told about: each recurring occurrence has its own ID (template, room, day and assignee), so the next occurrence of a chore is news, while undoing a completion is not.
  - The list is unchanged: nothing is written (a re-date before expiry as for other reminders, without a push). The list shrank: message and list are updated, read and dismissed state kept, no push. So a dismissal holds until a chore the person has not been told about becomes overdue.
  - Nothing is overdue: `resolvedAt` is set and the list emptied; the bell and dashboard stop showing it. When something is overdue again the same document reopens (`resolvedAt`/`resolvedBy` `null`, re-dated); a chore already told about (for example a completion undone, even days later and after other chores were announced) reopens it quietly with read and dismissed state kept, a new one as above.
  - Every write, resolution and reopen happens only when the document's last write was visible to the run's source reads (`shouldReopen`), so a run with older reads never overwrites a newer run's result or a dismissal made while it ran; the next run picks it up.
  - Dated `${uid}-overdue-YYYY-MM-DD` documents written by earlier versions are deleted in the same transaction, for that person only (ID and `targetUserUid` checked), over the last 9 local days: whenever the overdue notification is written, and in the first hour of the person's day. No separate script is needed. When the stable document does not exist yet, the most recent dated one is taken over (`legacyOverdueState`): on its day the person was pushed about every chore due before that day, so those chores count as told about and `lastPushDay` is that day. A dated alert from today therefore gives a silent creation that keeps the person's own `readBy`/`dismissedBy` entries; one from an earlier day leaves chores that became overdue since as news (pushed, unread). Earlier dated documents add nothing beyond the most recent one.
- Maintenance and chore reminders are scheduled server-side. The hourly `householdUpkeep` Cloud Function (`functions/src/index.ts` → `functions/src/reminders.ts`) runs the same idempotent step for every household; the Chores page also runs it on demand through `syncHouseholdUpkeep` (`src/app/chores/actions.ts`, authorized by `chores.view`) on each load so a new recurring assignment appears immediately. Maintenance Center computes its due/overdue list locally from the same shared rules (`functions/src/shared/maintenance-reminders.ts`) for display only and writes no reminder notifications.
- Chore reminders read each approved member's reminder settings and time zone from their `users/{email}` profile, where the email is the one in the member's protected contact document only (`readMemberContacts` and `withContactEmails` in `functions/src/reminders.ts`, which also feed assignee resolution for recurring generation); a member without one gets no chore reminder, whatever their record holds; a failed contact read skips email-keyed lookups for that run, and generation from templates that still carry an email waits for the next run (see [Chores](../chores/README.md), "Assignee identity"). `tests/server/household-upkeep.test.ts` covers both, and a failed contact read deferring email-credited purges.
- Snoozed maintenance reminders are not notified: a schedule snoozed in the Maintenance Center carries `snoozedUntil` equal to its new due date, and `actionableReminders` skips the reminder while it is upcoming or due soon. It notifies again on the due day, or at once if a vehicle service becomes due by mileage (see [Maintenance Center](../maintenance-center/README.md)).
- Physical expiration: a Firestore TTL policy on collection group `notifications`, field `expiresAt`, deletes documents after they expire (Firestore usually acts within a day). It is versioned in `firestore.indexes.json` (`fieldOverrides`, `ttl: true`, with the field's default single-field indexes) and deployed by the release workflow. Nothing visible is lost: `expiresAt` is seven days after `createdAt`, and the bell and center only list the last seven days by `createdAt`. Still-overdue maintenance reminders are re-surfaced by hourly upkeep before they expire. Legacy `users/{email}/notifications` documents have no `expiresAt` and are not affected.

## Invariants and Failure Behavior

- Notification UI never displays `sourceType`, `stateKey` or `expiresAt` values directly.
- A notification without a link is a button (it marks the notification read), never a link to `#` (NO-11, NO-20). The grab cursor shows only on touch screens, where swiping works, and while a swipe is in progress only "grabbing" applies (`swipeCursorClass`; NO-20). A dismissed card is shown with muted token colours, not reduced opacity, and says "You dismissed this" only for the viewer's own dismissal (NO-11: dismissal is per person). Ages and the Today / This week groups use `useNow` (`src/hooks/use-now.ts`, every minute), so they keep up on an always-on screen (NO-11).

- Bell queries the last seven days, reads at most 30 records (for a member who cannot see every module, the 30 are counted after the category filter, which is part of the query), displays up to 10 active unresolved/undismissed items, and counts and dismisses all active ones among those it read (NO-7).
- Age: upkeep writes `firstCreatedAt` when it creates a reminder (and on a reopen, or when the overdue notification has news) and leaves it on a refresh, which moves `createdAt` to keep the reminder in the 7-day window; parsing exposes it as `firstCreatedAt` (only when earlier than `createdAt`) and `notificationSince` is the age the bell and center show (NO-11).
- Chore-assignment notifications written by the app use fixed IDs (`assignmentNotificationId`: source and recipient, or the quick-add temporary chore's `getDeterministicNotificationId`), so a retried write is refused as an update instead of adding a duplicate (NO-11).
- Center hides expired records; household-wide records (system and general included) are under the Household filter.
- Dismissal is per user and does not delete the record.
- Deterministic maintenance identity is `(sourceType, sourceId, stateKey)`; `stateKey` changes only for a meaningful due-state/date/mileage cycle change.
- Deleting a maintenance asset or vehicle resolves its active schedule and expiration reminders from a fresh notification snapshot; unrelated notification history remains intact.
- Completing or snoozing a maintenance schedule resolves that schedule's reminders on the server after the commit (Admin SDK, `resolvedBy` the person; they need no `notifications.view`), and hourly upkeep resolves any open maintenance reminder that is no longer actionable (see [Maintenance Center](../maintenance-center/README.md), "Server operations").
- Bell and center presentation deduplicate legacy documents with the same semantic source while merging read, dismiss, and resolution history.
- Reminders appear without anyone opening a page; IDs are deterministic, so overlapping runs never duplicate. Each person has at most one overdue-chores notification, whatever the number of days or chores.
- The member who caused a notification is not pushed about it (NO-11): the app writes `createdByUid` (the signed-in member; the create rule requires it to be the caller) on chore-assignment notices, and the server on the ownership-transfer notice, the pending-member reminder and the wall-tablet removal notice; `deliverPush` drops that member from the recipients. It still shows in their bell.
- Pushes carry an FCM `TTL` header (`pushTtlSeconds`): a day-based reminder (`holdUntilMorning`) expires at the household's next midnight (at least an hour), anything else after a day, so a phone that was off for two days never gets an old "due today" (NO-11).
- Push recipients come from the household's membership records: a `[uid]` audience goes only to that approved member, `['*']` to every approved member, `[]` to nobody; a document without `audience` falls back to `targetUserUid` (or `targetUserEmail`) for one approved member, else every approved member. Never pending or former members. A member's email comes only from their protected contact document (`members/{uid}/private/contact`, read for every approved member with the Admin SDK, `readMemberContacts`; `withContactEmails`, `functions/src/shared/member-contact.ts`, drops any email left on a record). A member without one gets no push; a failed contact read is tried again (three reads, 250 ms and 1 s apart: `readMemberContacts` in `functions/src/reminders.ts`) and, if every read fails, the invocation fails with an error and sends nothing rather than guessing (the trigger has no retries configured, so a notification is never pushed twice; Error Reporting shows the failure); the recipient selection, the category filter and the image check (`canSeeImage`) all use that email. Each recipient's profile is then read directly by email (`users/{email}`) for its FCM tokens; no `users.uid` query is involved, so profiles without a stored `uid` still receive pushes.
- New-member approval notifications are targeted at each active owner/admin.
- Signing out stops pushes to that browser for the account that signed out; the next account to sign in there (or the same one) is registered again without a prompt when browser permission is still granted. If sign-out could not reach the server, the account's document may still list the old token until the next push to it fails and the push function removes it, or the next sign-in on that device replaces it; if the browser was offline, the push subscription itself also stays until that next sign-in.
- The Profile card (NO-5) says "This device is registered for alerts" only when this device's remembered token is in the account's push document (`pushRegisteredHere`); with permission granted but the token missing it offers "Re-connect this device". "Turn off on this device" stores the opt-out for this account (`homehub.push-off`), marks the removal pending (`homehub.push-off-pending`) and removes the registration as sign-out does (`turnOffPushOnThisDevice`); the pending mark is cleared only once both the account token and the browser subscription are gone, so a "Turn off" made offline finishes on reconnect or the next load. "Turn on for this device" clears only this account's entries and registers. States come from `pushCardState`; on an iPhone or iPad outside the installed app (no Notification or Push API, not standalone) the card gives the Home Screen steps instead of "unsupported" (NO-17). Enabling explains the browser prompt first and shows one toast for the outcome.

- Kid profiles (`kid_…` members, docs/modules/devices/README.md) are never targeted: `buildNotificationDocument` throws for a `kid_` target, chore reminders skip them, and push delivery finds no contact document for them (no recipient, no lookup error). A household-wide notice still reaches everyone with an account. "Tablet PIN locked" (`sourceType: 'pin-locked'`, category `system`) is one targeted notice per active owner and admin when someone's tablet PIN is hard-locked. "Tablet PIN keeps locking" (`sourceType: 'pin-tablet-locks'`) is one targeted notice per active owner and admin when one person's PIN locks 3 different tablets within 24 hours, at most once a day per person.
