# Maintenance Center

## Purpose and Scope

This module owns home assets, vehicles, maintenance logs, embedded service schedules, maintenance attachments, due-state calculation, and maintenance notification creation. It does not own general notification presentation, shared AI configuration, or background push delivery.

## User-Facing Capabilities

- Use Overview, Home Assets, Vehicles, Reminders, and Logs tabs at `/maintenance`.
- Create, view, edit, and delete assets; manage multiple schedules and linked logs.
- Create, view, edit, and delete vehicles; separate sold/retired vehicles; manage multiple service schedules.
- Create general, asset, or vehicle logs; view, edit, delete, attach files (a receipt or photo can be picked in the log form itself and read with AI), and request an AI summary (stored on the log; see "Forms, receipts, deletes and mileage").
- Complete an asset/vehicle schedule by creating a routine log and advancing due values (on the server, once per cycle, with a 10 s Undo; see "Server operations").
- Upload, open/download, categorize, and delete attachments.
- Review date/mileage reminders and act on them in place: **Mark done**, **Log service**, **Snooze 1 week**, or **Open** the asset/vehicle (see "Reminder actions").
- **Overview** (MA-12, MA-14, M4): **Needs doing** comes first: overdue (and expired registrations or inspections) and due-today reminders, then due soon, then snoozed ones (muted, "Snoozed until …"), each with Mark done, Log service and Snooze in place, and below them the assets and vehicles flagged "Needs attention", labelled "Flagged" (`needsDoing` in `src/lib/maintenance/needs-doing.ts`; an ended warranty has nothing to do and is left out). Its badge is the one "attention" number: reminders that need doing now plus flags (snoozed ones not counted); the old "Needs Attention" count card that counted only flags is gone. With `?location=` (a **Room** chip row; MA-26) the list keeps only home assets in that room (`matchesRoom`) and leaves vehicles out; `NeedsDoingPanel` takes the room as a prop, so a registered tablet's room can drive it later (M8). Beside it: the home health panel (or "Not enough tracked yet" with **Add a schedule**, which opens Home Assets, and the add form when there is no asset) and **Coming up** for the next 30 days (narrowed to the same room). Below: Home assets, Vehicles, "Logged, last 30 days" and "Spent, last 30 days" counts, and **Recent logs** (the five newest by date, undated last).
- **Reminders tab** (MA-13): one list grouped by urgency (Overdue or expired, Due today, Due soon, Snoozed, Upcoming; `reminderGroups`), each reminder exactly once, each group with its count and a one-line meaning (the due-soon line quotes `REMINDER_DUE_SOON_DAYS`, `MILEAGE_DUE_SOON_THRESHOLD` and `EXPIRY_WARNING_DAYS`), kind chips with counts (All, Home schedules, Vehicle service, Warranties, Registration and inspection; `kinds`, a chip is listed only while it has reminders), and the 90-day Coming up beside it. The page builds its reminders with `expiryLookaheadDays: COMING_UP_DAYS`, so warranties, registrations and inspections expiring within 90 days are in the Upcoming group and in Coming up alike (never notified). Vehicle services with a due mileage show a mileage estimate line when there is one (MA-29, below).
- **Coming up** (MA-27a): a month-by-month timeline of schedules, services, warranties, registrations and inspections (30 days on Overview, 90 on Reminders), then services due only by mileage, by miles left; a service with a mileage estimate is placed on its estimated day ("Expected …") instead (see "Coming up").
- **Warranty claim checklist** (MA-33c): in the asset detail of an asset with a warranty end, lifetime warranty or provider, what a claim needs and what HomeHub already has (provider, serial number, model, purchase date and price, a receipt/invoice/warranty document attached, warranty end), with the gaps flagged.
- **Search and filters** (MA-21): on Home Assets, Vehicles, Reminders and Logs a search box and Location and Category chips (`MaintenanceFilterBar`, `FilterChips`), kept in the URL as `?q=`, `?location=` and `?category=` next to `?tab=` and the record links (see "Insights, filters and QR labels"). Choosing a tab keeps them; **Clear filters** removes all three. Typing is written to the URL after a 250 ms pause (no history entry). Logs are paged, 20 per page (Newer/Older), and a `?log=` link opens the page that log is on.
- **Layout** (MA-17, MA-18): Home Assets and Vehicles fill the width with as many 15rem card columns as fit (`repeat(auto-fill, minmax(min(100%,15rem),1fr))`: 2-3 beside the detail) and, from `lg` (1024 px, `WIDE_LAYOUT_QUERY`), a sticky detail column (1rem below the 3.5rem site header: `top-[4.5rem]`, `max-h: 100dvh - 5.5rem`, scrolling on its own when taller than the window). Below `lg` there is no detail column: tapping a card (or a link to a record: a deep link, Open, a health reason) opens the detail in a full-height bottom sheet (`DetailSheet`: a top bar with the record's name and Close that never scrolls away, the detail scrolling under it), which Back closes (`useBackCloses`) except while a dialog opened from it is still open. The sheet remembers the record it was opened for and closes if that record goes away (deleted here or on another device), rather than showing the record the selection falls back to. The detail renders in exactly one place (column or sheet). The tab row is one row at every width; on a phone it scrolls sideways and keeps the chosen tab in view (only the row scrolls, never the page). Retired assets leave the main grid for a collapsed **Retired (N)** section below it, like sold and retired vehicles (MA-11j).
- **Detail** (M4): the service history line under the name ("Summer HVAC inspection last done 6 weeks ago by Sam", MA-32), fields, for vehicles **Registration and inspection** with **Renewed (+1 year)** (MA-33a, with a visible note that it moves the date a year and can be undone for 10 s), the action row, **Print label** (MA-31: shows the QR label, loaded on first open, with its own Print button), the schedules with their own history lines, **Costs** (MA-28: lifetime, by year, cost per mile for vehicles, the repair-versus-replace hint), the warranty claim checklist (assets), files and the linked logs. A checklist item (no due date) says "No due date yet. Add one to get reminders." with **Add a due date**, which opens the record's edit form. Long names wrap anywhere instead of overflowing (cards, detail, reminders, logs; MA-11i).
- **Logs** (MA-11e, MA-20): every log is kept: a log without a readable date shows "Undated" and sorts last (logs are read unordered and sorted on the page, because a Firestore `orderBy('date')` drops documents without the field); an older log's `receiptUrl` is a link "Receipt (older upload)" (http and https only); a log written by the server shows "Done by Sam" (`doneByName` through `publicDisplayName`; legacy logs show nothing; logs record no device yet, so there is no "on <tablet>"). Files are not rendered per row: **Files (N)** opens the log's attachment panel (a linked `?log=` opens by itself).
- See a 0-100 **home health score** on Overview (dial, band label and the deductions behind it, each linking to its item from a row at least `--target-min` tall: 44 px, 48 px on touch screens), or "Not enough tracked yet" with **Add a schedule** when nothing is tracked, and a small health ring on every tracked asset and vehicle card and detail panel (see "Home health score").
- Asset and vehicle cards are photo-first (H30, `PhotoBand`): the newest image attachment of that asset or vehicle (`pickTargetPhoto`, preferring the `photo` category) fills a fixed 80 px band over a blurred copy of itself; without one, the category icon (a car for vehicles) on a token gradient. Vehicles upload photos through the same attachment panel and Storage path as assets (`households/{householdId}/maintenance/vehicle/{vehicleId}/…`, already allowed by the existing `storage.rules` match), so no rule changed.
- **Brand logos** (H30): `BrandLogo` (`src/components/brand/brand-logo.tsx`) matches an asset's `brand` or a vehicle's `make` against a catalog of 223 household brands (`src/lib/maintenance/brands.ts`: cars and motorcycles, appliances, small kitchen, cleaning, HVAC and water, smart home and electronics, tools and lawn, plumbing, pool). 68 brands have a monochrome Simple Icons mark (CC0 path data in `src/components/brand/brand-marks.ts`, loaded once as its own lazy chunk by dynamic import, never in a first-paint bundle and never fetched from the network); the other 155 get one consistent monogram badge (short name or initials in the app's headline type). Both draw in `currentColor`, so they follow every theme. Matching ignores case, accents, punctuation, spaces, "&"/"+"/"and" and trailing company words (Inc, Appliances, Motor Company, Electronics…), knows aliases (Chevy→Chevrolet, VW→Volkswagen, Mercedes/Benz→Mercedes-Benz, GE Appliances→GE, SharkNinja→Ninja, Roomba→iRobot, Kitchen Aid→KitchenAid, Honda Power→Honda…), accepts leading words ("Whirlpool Gold"→Whirlpool), and tolerates typos with optimal-string-alignment Damerau-Levenshtein distance 1 for 5–7 letters and 2 for 8 or more, choosing the single closest brand and refusing ties; names of 4 letters or fewer (GE, LG, Kia) match only exactly, and placeholders ("Generic", "N/A") never match. Logos show once on each asset and vehicle card (beside the title), in the detail panel header and on flagged items in Needs doing. Where the brand is not printed as text (asset cards, flagged assets) the mark is an image named after the brand ("Bosch"); elsewhere it is `aria-hidden`.
- Upload, open/download, categorize, and delete attachments; delete an asset, vehicle or log together with its own files in one confirmed operation.
- Update a vehicle's mileage from a quick "Update mileage" dialog with a 10 s Undo, and see when it was last updated (component built; its place on the vehicle card and detail panel is pending page wiring).
- **Schedule presets** (MA-27b): common schedules per asset category and for vehicles, offered as "Suggested schedules"/"Suggested services" buttons in the add/edit forms; each adds an editable schedule row (see "Schedule presets").

- Asset and vehicle cards are photo-first (H30, `PhotoBand`): the newest image attachment of that asset or vehicle (`pickTargetPhoto`, preferring the `photo` category, never a receipt or invoice) fills a fixed 80 px band over a blurred copy of itself; without one, the category icon (a car for vehicles) on a token gradient. Vehicles upload photos through the same attachment panel and Storage path as assets (`households/{householdId}/maintenance/vehicle/{vehicleId}/…`, already allowed by the existing `storage.rules` match), so no rule changed.
- **Warranty countdown ring** (H10): an asset with a warranty end date shows a small ring beside its health ring, filled with the share of the warranty term still left and the days left inside ("9d"; years from 1,000 days, "0d" once ended). Its accessible name reads e.g. "Main HVAC System: Warranty: 9 days left of 1,551". The ring turns amber within 30 days and grey once ended. A **lifetime warranty** (asset `warrantyLifetime: true`) gets no expiry reminders and no countdown: in place of the ring a 40 px tall pill with a success-colored border reads "Lifetime" (same height, so the card does not shift); its accessible name and tooltip read "Lifetime warranty" (`warrantyCountdown` returns `LIFETIME_WARRANTY` even without an end date, `warrantyRingLabel` "Lifetime"). HomeHub stores no warranty start date, so the term runs from the asset's **purchase date** when it is set and earlier than the end date; otherwise the term is assumed to be **one year** ending on the warranty end date (`warrantyCountdown()` in `src/lib/maintenance/warranty.ts`, `WARRANTY_FALLBACK_DAYS = 365`). Days are counted on the household's local day. The ring sweeps from full on load (550 ms, value only; instant under reduced motion).
- **Odometer** (H10): a vehicle card shows its mileage as odometer wheels (at least six, leading zeros), each rolling to its digit (transform only, 550 ms, wheels a few ms apart; final digits at once under reduced motion). Screen readers get the plain value ("48,750 miles"), which is also part of the card's accessible name; the wheels are `aria-hidden`.

- Controls follow the member's maintenance permissions: Add/Edit/Complete/Snooze/Upload/Update mileage need `maintenance.edit`, Add Log needs `maintenance.createLogs` (which also lets the member attach receipts and photos to the log they just created, within 30 minutes, and read a receipt with AI), Mark done and Complete need both, Delete (records, logs, attachments) needs `maintenance.delete`. ## Architecture and Data Flow

`MaintenanceLogClient` keeps four live `onSnapshot` listeners on the household's `home-assets` (by name), `vehicles` (by nickname), `maintenance` logs (read unordered, so a log without a `date` is not dropped, and sorted on the page with `sortLogs`: newest date first, undated last, MA-11e) and `maintenance-attachments` (newest first) (MA-2, `useMaintenanceLiveData` in `src/components/maintenance/use-maintenance-data.ts`, state in `src/lib/maintenance/live-data.ts`). Each is the shared `resilientListener` (`src/lib/dashboard/resilient-listener.ts`): a failed listener resubscribes with bounded backoff (1 s doubling to 60 s, with jitter) and at once on `online` or when the page becomes visible; only a server-confirmed snapshot (`includeMetadataChanges`, `!fromCache`) resets the schedule, and metadata-only snapshots change no state. The listeners are keyed by household and member (`{householdId}|{uid}`; none while `maintenance.view` is missing) and stop when the key changes or the page unmounts; their state is keyed the same way, so a record of the previous household is never shown in the next one, not even for a frame. The page is ready once all four have answered for the key: until then it shows the loading skeleton (only on that first load), and a failure before that is the error card, whose **Try again** resubscribes at once (`retryNow`). After the first load a failing listener keeps the records it has and retries quietly. A listener refused with `permission-denied` (retried twice, about 3 s, since a token refresh can race a read) settles on `unauthorized`: every collection is cleared and the page shows the "not shared with you" state, never stale protected records; it stays so until the listeners are recreated (another household or member, or `maintenance.view` revoked and granted again; each subscription has its own session, so the new one starts from an empty state). Changes from this page's server actions, from quick add (`homehub:data-changed` is not needed) and from other devices arrive through the listeners, so no action re-reads the collections after a write (MA-25c). It does not query notifications on load; it reads them only when a registry record is deleted (the record's source types; the id prefix is matched in the browser), to resolve that record's reminders. Completion and snooze resolve reminders on the server (see "Server operations"). The deletion query is audience-constrained (`notificationsBySourceQuery`, `array-contains-any [uid, '*']`); maintenance reminders are household-wide (`['*']`), so every member finds all of them, while a reminder without `audience` (not backfilled) is not found and stays open until it expires. Assets and vehicles embed schedule arrays. Reminder cards and server notifications share one builder (`functions/src/shared/maintenance-reminders.ts`) evaluated in the household timezone. Retired assets and retired/sold vehicles produce no reminders; warranties and vehicle documents "expire" rather than become "overdue". Notification documents are created by the server, not by opening this page.

### Server operations (M1: the completion contract)

Completing, snoozing and saving a log, asset or vehicle are server actions (`src/app/maintenance/actions.ts`, `'use server'`), never client writes. Each verifies the ID token (`authorizeHouseholdAction`) and checks the member's permissions: complete and its Undo need `maintenance.edit` **and** `maintenance.createLogs`; snooze, its Undo, and saving an asset or vehicle need `maintenance.edit`; creating a log needs `maintenance.createLogs` and editing one `maintenance.edit`. Requests are shape- and size-checked (96 KB) and fail closed with a typed, user-safe result (`{ ok: false, code, message }`). Who did it and when never come from the client. Each operation in `src/lib/server/maintenance-operations.ts` has the shape `(db, householdId, input, ctx: OperationContext)` and is one Admin SDK transaction in the chore-completion order (device hooks' `precheck` first, then reads, writes, `finalize` last), so tablet device actions can call them later; none exist yet. The plans are pure functions in `src/lib/maintenance/operations.ts`.

- **Op id and ledger.** Every request carries a client op id (a UUID taken once per press; only Retry reuses it). `households/{h}/maintenance-ops/{opId}` records `kind` (`complete`, `snooze`, `renew`, `save_asset`, `save_vehicle`, `save_log`, `update_mileage`), `actorUid`, `actorName`, `ownerType`, `ownerId`, `scheduleId`, `logId`, `appliedAt` (server time), `undoneAt`, the stored result, the completion record (completions) or `before`/`after` (snoozes), and the notification cleanup target. The same op id from the same person and kind replays the stored result and writes nothing; reused by someone else or for another kind it is refused (`conflict`). Entries are kept (no TTL).
- **Completion.** The request names the schedule by its stable id (or legacy key, below) and the cycle the person saw (`seen`: its `nextDueDate`/`nextDueMileage`). In one transaction: if the schedule's `lastCompletion.cycle` equals `seen` the answer is `already_done` with who and when (checked first, so a completion that left the due values unchanged still counts once); if the current cycle differs from `seen` it is `changed`; a missing record or schedule is `gone`. Otherwise the values are validated (`completedDate` a real `yyyy-MM-dd` not after the household's today; mileage an integer 0 to 2,000,000; a vehicle service with `intervalMiles` or a `nextDueMileage` needs a reading, `mileage_required`; next due mileage above the reading) and a completion that would leave neither a next due date nor mileage is refused (`next_due_required`). Then, together: the log (id `{slug}-{opId}`) with `doneByUid`, `doneByName` (the member's display name through `publicDisplayName`, never an email), `createdAtServer`, `opId` and `completion: { scheduleId, ownerType, ownerId, cycle, before, after }` holding exactly the fields this completion changed (`nextDueDate`, `nextDueMileage`, `lastCompletedDate`, `lastCompletedMileage`, `snoozedUntil`, `snoozedFrom`, `lastCompletion`, and the vehicle's `currentMileage`/`mileageUpdatedAt`); the schedule patched by id (snooze ended, `lastCompletion` set); the vehicle's mileage; and the ledger entry. Two sessions, a double tap or a retry with different op ids therefore make one log.
- **Mileage (MA-6).** `currentMileage` becomes `max(current, reading)`: a backdated or lower reading never lowers it (the log keeps its own reading). `mileageUpdatedAt` (household day) is set when the reading is at least the current one. Vehicle logs saved through the log operation follow the same rule, applied to the vehicle the log points at after the save is merged onto the freshly read log (so a log moved to another vehicle by someone else meanwhile never raises the old vehicle's odometer); a reading counts when the save set it or pointed the log at a vehicle. An explicit change in the vehicle form is written as entered (a correction). The page asks once before saving a reading lower than the recorded one or more than 20,000 above it (`mileageWarning`), with separate copy: a log or completion reading says the vehicle keeps the higher reading, the vehicle form says the odometer will be corrected down (or set) to the value entered. The server accepts either. A log's reading raises the vehicle only when the person also holds `maintenance.edit` (the update is a vehicle edit; `applyVehicleReading`); with `maintenance.createLogs` alone the reading stays on the log.
- **Notification cleanup.** After the commit (never inside it, and never with the person's own notification access) the schedule's open reminder notifications whose `sourceId` is the schedule's id, its legacy key or a pre-key legacy id (`{owner}-{index}:{status}`) and whose state is no longer actionable are resolved (`resolvedBy` the actor). Only `sourceType ==` is queried. A failure returns `cleanup: 'pending'`; the completion stays saved and the hourly upkeep pass resolves the rest.
- **Undo** (completion and snooze; only the original actor; `UNDO_WINDOW_MS` = 10 s from the ledger's `appliedAt` plus a 5 s server grace for latency) is compare-and-revert: each field the operation changed goes back to its `before` value only while it still holds the `after` value; a field someone changed since keeps its value and is returned in `kept` ("The due date was changed after you marked it done, so it stays Mar 3."). The completion's log is deleted; if files are attached to that log the Undo is refused with `has_attachments` ("Remove the attached files first, or keep it done.") and nothing changes. New attachment metadata needs its target to exist (rules), and the Undo transaction holds the log, so no file can be attached while it runs. A schedule completed again since is `superseded`: nothing changes and the log stays (the decision for "completion, another completion, Undo of the first"). A schedule or record deleted meanwhile is never recreated. A vehicle mileage raised by a later reading stays. Because values are compared, an edit back to exactly the value the operation wrote is indistinguishable from no edit. After an Undo the next upkeep run reopens the resolved reminder (same identity, still actionable). The completion and snooze answers carry `undoMs`, what is left of the 10 s window when the answer leaves the server (measured from `appliedAt`, so a replay of an older press or a slow cleanup leaves less); the page offers Undo only for that long less half a second, and not at all below 1.5 s (`undoOfferMs`). A snooze is taken back only while it still stands (the schedule still carries its `snoozedUntil`); once a completion ended it or it was snoozed again, its Undo changes nothing (`superseded`), so a completion whose next due happens to equal the snoozed date is never rolled back.
- **Snooze** (MA-3b) patches only `nextDueDate`, `snoozedUntil` and `snoozedFrom` (the due date before the snooze, for M3: a completion while the snooze applies steps from it; a snooze still in effect keeps the date it was first snoozed from) of the schedule named by id, if its cycle is still `seen`, under the card's rules (`snoozedDueDate`, `canSnoozeReminder`: never for a service due by mileage), stores `before`/`after` in the ledger and resolves the old cycle's reminder after the commit.
- **Edits** (MA-3c). Asset, vehicle and log saves send `{ id?, base, values }`; `base` is what the form showed when it opened. For an existing record only fields whose value differs from `base` are written onto the freshly read document (`null` removes). Schedules merge by id: changed fields of a schedule the form had are patched, a schedule the form removed is removed, one someone added meanwhile is kept, and a new one is appended, so a completion or snooze that happened while the form was open survives and a removed schedule is never resurrected. Formats (real days, required names, ranges, options) are checked only for changed fields, so an untouched legacy value never blocks an unrelated edit. A record deleted meanwhile is `not_found`, never recreated. A log's target type and its linked id (`targetType`, `assetId`, `vehicleId`) are one choice: when a save changes any of them, all three are written from that form (with `mileage` cleared off a vehicle), never mixed field by field with someone else's concurrent move. The 100-schedule limit is checked again on the merged list inside the transaction, so two editors adding at once cannot write 101 (`too_many_schedules`: "…at most 100 maintenance items, counting ones someone else added meanwhile…"; nothing is written). New records get `{slug}-{10 characters of the op id}`, which cannot collide and is the same on a retry. Log edits can never change `doneByUid`, `doneByName`, `opId`, `completion` or `createdAtServer`. Edits carry both snapshots, so their request budget is twice a record's plus room for the rest (any record that could be created stays editable).
- **Schedule identity.** A schedule without an id is named by its legacy key `legacy-{index}-{slug}` (the reminder builder's compatibility key), and the first operation that touches its record stores that key as its id (`-2`, `-3` only if taken), so reminder `sourceId`s, deep links and open notifications do not change. `scripts/ops/backfill-maintenance-schedule-ids.mjs` does the same for records nobody touches (dry run by default, `--apply` to write, `--project <id>`; idempotent; only the schedule array of a record with id-less schedules is written, in a transaction; a malformed list is skipped; counts only). Its pure twin `scripts/ops/maintenance-schedule-ids.mjs` is checked against the server's `withScheduleIds`.

In the page, every save, complete and snooze button is disabled with a spinner while its request is in flight, and a dialog cannot be closed until the outcome is known. Offline (`navigator.onLine` false) the page says "Needs a connection. Your entries are kept." and keeps the dialog and its input. With no answer after 20 s (the deadline starts before the ID token is fetched and covers the whole call) it says "No answer from HomeHub. It may have been saved." with **Retry**, which resends the same request with the same op id (a card action shows this as a toast). Until a definite answer, pressing Save again in that dialog (or the card action again) also sends that same request, never a new op id, so an uncertain save is never duplicated; opening the dialog afresh discards it (`createPendingRequests`, `callWithDeadline` in `src/lib/maintenance/action-call.ts`). `already_done` shows "Already done by Sam on Mar 1." and `changed` "This changed since you opened it.", the listeners already show the current state. "Needs a connection" is only said before a request leaves; a request that fails after it left (even because the connection dropped) is an unknown outcome and keeps its op id.
The held requests and the dialog notices live in `useMaintenanceOps` (`src/components/maintenance/use-maintenance-ops.tsx`); each dialog's opening discards its key's held request.

### Completion and anchoring

Schedules carry an optional `anchor` (MA-11g): `'completion'` (the default, and every schedule saved before this field) or `'calendar'`, with optional `anchorMonth` (1-12) and `anchorDay` (1-31). `nextDueAfterCompletion(schedule, completedDate)` in `schedules.ts` (used by the server completion planner `planCompletion`, by Mark done, Log service and Confirm mileage through `serviceCompletionValues`, and by the completion dialog's prefill; the planner passes the schedule as stored, snooze included, before the completion removes `snoozedUntil`/`snoozedFrom`) (asset `frequencyType`/`intervalValue` or vehicle `intervalMonths`) calls the shared pure `nextDueDay` in `functions/src/shared/maintenance-schedule.ts`, so the page, the server operation and the hourly job use one implementation. All math is on household-day strings (`yyyy-MM-dd`), never `Date` in a local zone, so DST and the server clock cannot move a day.

- Completion-anchored: completion day + interval (month steps clamp to the month's end: Jan 31 + 1 month = Feb 28; Feb 29 + 1 year = Feb 28). This is exactly what `calculateNextAssetDueDate`/`calculateNextVehicleDueDate` return (parity test over 800 days).
- Calendar-anchored: the next due is the previous due day plus k intervals, the smallest k ≥ 1 that lands after the completion day. Gutters due Oct 1 2026: done Nov 5 2026 or early on Sep 20 2026, next Oct 1 2027. Each step is computed from the previous due day, never from a clamped step, and `anchorDay` keeps month/year steps on that day (monthly on the 31st returns to Mar 31 after Feb 28).
- A snoozed due day (`snoozedUntil` equal to `nextDueDate`) is not the schedule's real cycle. Invariant: the cycle a snooze moved away from is recovered exactly from `snoozedFrom` (the due day before the snooze, to be written by the server snooze operation with `snoozedUntil`; completion removes both), so completing a snoozed schedule gives the same day as completing it unsnoozed (gutters due Oct 1, snoozed to Oct 8, done Sep 30: Oct 1 2027; every 2 months on the 31st, due Jan 31, snoozed to Feb 8, done Feb 2: Mar 31). A stale `snoozedFrom` (the snooze no longer applies) is ignored. Fallback for snoozes written before `snoozedFrom`: the cycle is inferred as the last anchor day on or before the snoozed day minus `SNOOZE_SHIFT_DAYS` (7, equal to `SNOOZE_DAYS`, tested); this is exact only when the anchors fix the phase (an anchor month with an interval that divides a year, or a monthly day-only anchor) and otherwise may move to a neighbouring cycle; without anchor fields the snoozed day is used.
- Anchor lattice: month/year steps pass through the anchor month of the reference year, or with a day-only anchor through the reference's own month (`anchorLattice`). With no due day at all, the anchors place the cycle; with neither, completion + interval. Day and week intervals have no anchor fields.
- Property test (`tests/unit/maintenance-schedule-anchor.test.ts`): 6,000 seeded random month/year schedules (intervals 1-12 months or 1-3 years; day-only, month+day and month-only anchors), first due days created on random days and on the first or last day of months (on or after today, on the anchor day, today itself when today is one), snoozes from 14 days before to 120 days after the due day (into other months), and completions: the result is always after the completion and, with `snoozedFrom`, equal to the unsnoozed result (also without it where the phase is fixed); plus 4,000 arbitrary schedules that always give a day after the completion.
- `firstCalendarDue` gives a new calendar schedule its first due day: the first anchor day on or after today, with an implicit anchor month taken from today (gutters created Nov 24 2026: Oct 1 2027; monthly on the 31st created Nov 24 2026: Nov 30 2026; yearly on the 1st created Nov 1 2026: Nov 1 2026).
- Anchor fields never change a reminder or its notification identity.

### Renewing registrations and inspections

`renewedExpiration(current, today)` (shared file): one year after the current expiration when it is in the future or no more than `RENEWAL_GRACE_DAYS` (60) past, so renewing a little late keeps the cycle; otherwise (long expired, or no valid date) one year after today. Feb 29 renews to Feb 28.

**Renewed (+1 year)** (MA-33a, M4) in the vehicle detail calls `renewVehicleDocument` (`src/app/maintenance/actions.ts`, needs `maintenance.edit`) → `renewVehicleDocumentAs` (`src/lib/server/maintenance-operations.ts`, plan `planRenewal` in `operations.ts`): one transaction reads the household (its day), the member, the ledger and the vehicle, and writes only that field (`registrationExpiration` or `inspectionExpiration`) to `renewedExpiration(current, today)`, plus `updatedAt`. The request carries an op id and `seen`, the expiration the card showed (null for none): a stored value that differs answers `changed` ("This changed since you opened it…"), so a double press with another op id or a second session never adds two years; the same op id replays (`replay: true`); a missing vehicle is `gone` and is never created. The ledger entry has `kind: 'renew'`, `field`, `before`/`after` and the result. The toast ("Registration renewed", "Family SUV's registration now expires Aug 12, 2027.") offers **Undo** for 10 s: `undoVehicleRenewal` → `undoVehicleRenewalAs` (same actor and window rules as the other Undos) compares and reverts that one field (`planUndoRenewal`): a date someone changed since stays and is reported ("The registration date was changed after you renewed it, so it stays Jun 1."); a renewal from no date removes the field again; a deleted vehicle is not recreated. The old "expires soon" notification is resolved by the hourly upkeep (its state is no longer actionable); there is no immediate cleanup. The button is disabled with a spinner while its request is in flight (`renewing` in `useMaintenanceOps`).

Motion (H12): asset and vehicle grids enter with `Stagger`; selecting a card morphs it into the detail panel with a View Transition (`useInPageMorph` from `src/components/shopping/use-in-page-morph.ts`: the shared `view-transition-name` sits on the clicked card before the change and on the detail card after it, and is removed when the transition ends). Reduced motion, a browser without View Transitions, or a modified click (Shift/Ctrl/Cmd/Alt) just selects. The Overview's counts and 30-day spend tick up with `NumberTicker` (`src/components/motion/number-ticker.tsx`): its only text node is always the final value, and the moving digits are painted by CSS, so the text and accessible name never carry an in-between number. Flagged items in Needs doing have peek cards (brand/model, health, location or mileage). On a phone (below `lg`) there is no morph: the card opens the detail sheet.

Only the first load shows the loading skeleton, and it is the same component as the route's `loading.tsx` (`MaintenanceSkeleton`: header with Add Log, the tab row, the Overview cards), so on a cold load the header and tabs never disappear and come back (MA-25a). After a save, delete, completion or snooze the page stays on screen (so reminder actions stay in place) and the change arrives through the listeners.

**The household's today (MA-1, MA-11f).** `today` is the household's calendar day (`useHouseholdToday(householdDayZone(household.timeZone))` from `src/lib/household-day.ts`; America/Chicago when the household has no valid zone), kept in state and recomputed by a timer to the next household midnight (capped at an hour, so a throttled timer or a clock change cannot strand it) and on `visibilitychange` to visible, `pageshow`, window `focus` and `online` (a tablet or laptop waking from sleep). The next midnight is computed from the household zone's calendar (`msUntilHouseholdMidnight`), so a 23- or 25-hour DST day is neither skipped nor doubled. Reminders, home health, warranty rings, Mark done's and Confirm mileage's completed date, the completion dialog's and a new log's default date, and the Overview's 30-day counts all use it. "Logged, last 30 days" and "Spent, last 30 days" count logs dated today or in the 29 days before in household days (`recentLogSummary` in `src/lib/maintenance/recent.ts`; a legacy log date stored as an instant is read in the household zone; a log dated after today still counts, as before; one without a readable date does not).

**Rendering (MA-25b).** The page only wires state: each tab panel and the delete confirmations are memoized components, the dialogs own their forms (react-hook-form) and schedule rows, initialised once per opening from the record as it was then (that is also the edit's `base`, so a snapshot arriving while a form is open never resets it), and the page passes stable handlers (`useStableHandler`) and memoized derived lists and props (reminder groups, selected record logs, health, 30-day counts). Typing in a form re-renders only its dialog; nothing queries Firestore per keystroke.

### Forms, receipts, deletes and mileage (M5/M6)

**Shorter forms (MA-15).** Add Home Asset shows the essentials (name, location/room, category, warranty end or **Lifetime warranty**, and schedules, with the category's presets as "Suggested schedules" buttons); Add Vehicle shows nickname, make, model, year, current mileage and "Suggested services"; Add Maintenance Log shows title, what it is for (general, asset or vehicle and which one), date and cost, and a vehicle log's mileage (always shown: the max rule and Log service need it, and the page puts refusals under it; a refusal for a hidden detail field opens the details). The other fields sit behind **More details** on a new record; an edit shows every detail that already has a value and **Add details (N empty)** opens the empty ones (`ESSENTIAL_FIELDS`/`DETAIL_FIELDS`, `visibleDetailFields`, `emptyDetailFields` in `src/lib/maintenance/form-sections.ts`; an untouched status or log type counts as empty). A preset becomes an ordinary editable row (`presetSchedulesFor` with the household's today, and for vehicles the current mileage); a month or year schedule has **Keep it on a calendar day** (anchor `calendar`, a month for intervals that divide a year, and a day of the month), and a new calendar schedule without a due day gets `firstCalendarDue`. A lifetime warranty saves `warrantyLifetime: true` and no end date; unchecking it removes the field. The forms use the household's day (`useHouseholdToday`), never the device's.

**Phones and the unsaved-changes guard (MA-24f).** The three forms share `MaintenanceFormDialog` (`form-shell.tsx`): under 640 px it is a full-screen sheet sized with dynamic viewport units, sitting above the on-screen keyboard (`--keyboard-inset`), with Cancel and Save in a footer outside the scrolling body (safe-area padding), so both stay reachable while typing. Cancel, Escape, the close button, a click outside and Back (`useBackCloses`, the shared back stack) go through one guard: a form whose values, schedule rows or staged receipts differ from what it opened with (`formIsDirty`: blank, absent, false and outer spaces count as equal) asks **Discard your changes?** (Keep editing / Discard); a clean form closes at once; nothing closes while a save is in flight. The in-flight spinner, offline notice and Retry of M1 are unchanged.

**Receipts and photos in the log form (MA-16, MA-30).** "Receipt / photos" offers **Take photo** (`accept="image/*" capture="environment"`: the camera on phones) and **Choose file** (images and PDFs, several at once; up to 5). Files are checked in the browser with the limits of storage.rules (`checkMaintenanceFile`: under 15 MiB, exactly the rules' types, the type inferred from the extension when the browser gives none, as phones do for HEIC) and staged (`receipt-staging.ts`). They are uploaded to the log right after the server answers the save with its id (`commitStagedReceipts`, called by `runOp` for a 'log' save or a Log service completion), as receipts, with "Receipt attached" or a failure toast with **Try again** (the log stays saved). An offline or unanswered save keeps the staged files with the form. A receipt attached to a completion's log makes its Undo answer "Remove the attached files first" (as before for any file). For the log-saved toast, `useMaintenanceRecords` returns `addPhotoAction(logId)`, an **Add photo** toast action that opens the same picker and attaches what is picked to that log (pending page wiring: the page's "Maintenance log added" toast passes `action: addPhotoAction(result.id)`).

**Reading a receipt with AI (MA-30).** With a photo or PDF staged, **Read receipt** sends one downsized copy (longer edge 1600 px, at most 512 KB; a PDF must already be that small) to `readMaintenanceReceipt` (`maintenance.createLogs`, AI quota; see [AI and OpenAI](../ai-genkit/README.md)) and shows "Reading receipt…" (with Stop). The whole reading is bounded at 30 s (`withReceiptReadDeadline`); past that the form says "No answer from the receipt reader…" and stays usable, and a later answer is ignored. The answer is untrusted data: `sanitizeReceiptFields` keeps a finite cost from 0 to 1,000,000 (rounded to cents) and plain provider and parts text without control or bidirectional characters, bounded to 120 and 500 characters, rendered as text only. It is shown for checking, field by field, and fills the form only on **Use these**, and never a field the person changed since the form opened (`receiptFillPatch`; such a field shows "yours stays"). A later answer after the receipt was removed or replaced, Stop, or the form closed is ignored (`createReceiptReadGuard`). A failure (offline, HEIC the browser cannot decode, too large, nothing legible, AI errors) is a plain note; the form and the staged receipt are unchanged.

**Attachments (MA-11b, MA-11c, MA-25d, MA-11k).** Every upload (`uploadMaintenanceFile`, `attachment-upload.ts`) is checked first, refused at once offline ("Needs a connection. Your entries are kept."), and cancelled when no bytes move for 30 s or the browser goes offline; URL lookups and the metadata write wait at most 20 s (no answer there keeps the files, since a queued write may still land). A picture gets a 1024 px display copy and a 256 px thumbnail (the shared `downscaleImage`, JPEG, siblings `{attachmentId}-display.jpg`/`-thumb.jpg` in the record's folder, `displayPath`/`displayUrl`/`thumbnailPath`/`thumbnailUrl` on the metadata) when the browser can decode it; a HEIC photo most browsers cannot decode is stored as it is and listed with a "HEIC preview not available" tile. Every object carries the uploader's uid in its Storage metadata. When the metadata write is refused or a later step fails, every object just uploaded is removed through `cleanupMaintenanceUpload` (any uploader may ask; the server deletes only objects in this household's maintenance paths whose metadata names the caller and that no attachment points at), so members without `maintenance.delete` no longer leave orphans. Until the person picks a category, a log's file is a receipt and an asset's or vehicle's picture a photo and document a manual (`defaultAttachmentCategory`). A receipt or invoice is never a card picture (`pickTargetPhoto`), and a HEIC file is one only through its display copy. The attachment list shows thumbnails; one that fails to load falls back to the icon.

**Delete with files (MA-22, MA-4c, MA-11a).** Deleting an asset, vehicle or log is one confirmed server operation (`deleteMaintenanceRecord`, `maintenance.delete`) instead of a client batch, and no longer requires removing attachments first. `deleteMaintenanceCascade` (`functions/src/shared/maintenance-delete.ts`): (1) one transaction writes a tombstone `households/{h}/maintenance-deletions/{targetType}~{targetId}` (name, who, when; server-only) and deletes the record, so the rules refuse new attachments for it; (2) an asset's or vehicle's logs become general history with `formerTargetType`/`formerTargetName` (legacy logs linked by id included; re-checked until none are left) and keep their own files; (3) the record's own attachments: for each, its Storage objects (only paths inside `households/{h}/maintenance/{targetType}/{targetId}/`, so `asset-1` never touches `asset-10` and never a log's folder) and then its metadata, then any object left in that folder; (4) open reminders of the asset or vehicle are resolved; (5) the tombstone goes last. A Storage failure stops at that file: metadata stays only for files that may still exist, the record is already gone everywhere, and the action answers `pending` ("Some files are still being removed; HomeHub finishes within the hour"). A retry finishes it, and so does the hourly `householdUpkeep` (`resumeMaintenanceDeletions`, tombstones older than 10 minutes; a record recreated under the same id is left alone and the stale tombstone removed). Without a Storage bucket nothing is deleted past the record and the delete stays pending. The confirmations (`delete-dialogs.tsx`) keep their dialog open with a spinner until the answer and show a failure inside it; with the live `attachments` passed (pending page wiring) the action reads "Delete Fridge and its 3 attachments" or "Delete log and its 2 files".

**Update mileage (MA-19, MA-6e).** `UpdateMileageButton`/`UpdateMileageDialog` (`update-mileage.tsx`, `maintenance.edit`; pending page wiring on the vehicle card and detail panel) open a numeric field (`inputMode="numeric"`) prefilled with the recorded mileage ("Add mileage" when there is none, so mileage reminders can start). `updateVehicleMileageAs` writes a reading at or above the recorded one with `mileageUpdatedAt` (household day); a lower reading first shows the correction copy ("Saving corrects the vehicle's odometer down to …") and is then sent with `correction: true` (without it the server refuses, `lower_needs_correction`); a jump of more than 20,000 asks once. The op ledger (`update_mileage`) keeps before/after; the toast's **Undo** (10 s, `undoVehicleMileageAs`) is compare-and-revert (a later, higher reading stays and is named). `MileageFreshnessNote` reads "Mileage updated 12 days ago", with a nudge when older than 30 days, unknown (older vehicles without `mileageUpdatedAt`) or missing (`mileageFreshness`).

**Stored AI summaries (MA-11d).** "Summarize with AI" calls `summarizeStoredMaintenanceLog` (`maintenance.view`): the server reads the log's own notes (never text from the client), and returns the stored summary without an AI call or quota while the notes still hash to `summaryNotesHash` (`notesHash`, `src/lib/maintenance/summary.ts`: line endings and outer spaces ignored); otherwise, or with `regenerate`, it summarizes and writes `summary`, `summaryNotesHash`, `summaryModel` (`maintenance-summary@2/{model}`) and `summaryAt` with the Admin SDK, but only if the notes did not change while the model ran. Server-written was chosen over a client write within the rules: it lets a view-only member keep a summary for everyone, the hash and model are always the server's, and the AI action itself cannot be used to store text the client sent. `summary` itself stays in the client log allowlist for older clients, so a member with `maintenance.edit` can still overwrite it directly; the hash is not client-writable, and such a summary shows only as long as the notes are unchanged (it is not an integrity guarantee). `storedSummaryOf` tells the page whether a stored summary is current or stale (showing it is pending page wiring in the log list).

### Reminder actions

Reminder cards show the title, a status badge, the related asset/vehicle and one "when" line (for example "Due Jul 1, 2026 · 4 weeks ago" or "At 49,000 mi · 250 mi to go"); the builder's sentence ("... is overdue") is no longer repeated under the badge. Warranties and vehicle documents read "Expired" / "Expires today" / "Expires soon", never "Overdue".

- **Mark done** (schedules and vehicle services only; needs `maintenance.edit` and `maintenance.createLogs`): the H05 completion check (`CompletionCheck` and `useConfetti`, imported from Chores) at the start of the card. One tap fills the box and draws the check at once (150 ms), fires a small confetti burst from it (once per reminder, skippable by any input, none under reduced motion), plays the `complete` feedback, and completes the schedule today with the next due date advanced by the schedule's interval, through the same server completion as the dialog. The "Marked done" toast offers **Undo** for 10 s (`UNDO_WINDOW_MS`). A vehicle service with a mileage interval (`intervalMiles`) instead opens a small **Confirm mileage** dialog (numeric input prefilled with the vehicle's current mileage, the lower/jump warning) and is completed at the confirmed reading, never silently at the stored odometer (MA-6a); the check is not drawn before. A service with only a set due mileage and no interval opens the completion dialog, where the next due is entered. After an Undo the reminder lists start over, so the reopened card is unchecked with its actions. When the next due values cannot be worked out (no interval) the completion dialog opens instead and the check is not drawn; a failed or refused completion unchecks it. The drawn check is keyed by the reminder's cycle (`reminderCycleKey`: id, due date and due mileage), so when a schedule completed early shows its next cycle under the same reminder id, that card starts unchecked with its actions enabled.
- **Log service** (schedules and vehicle services only; needs `maintenance.createLogs`): opens the Add Maintenance Log form filled in for that asset or vehicle and schedule (target, schedule name as title, routine type, today, the vehicle's current mileage). When the member can also complete schedules (`maintenance.edit`), the dialog says "Saving marks … done …" and saving writes the log with its details (cost, provider, parts, notes) through the same server completion as Mark done (with Undo), advancing the schedule from the log's date and mileage and resolving the reminder. Switching the form to another asset, vehicle or General makes it an ordinary log. Like Mark done, Log service never writes a schedule without a next due: a schedule whose next due cannot be advanced from its interval (an explicit date or mileage with no repeat) opens the completion dialog instead of the log form, and a mileage-interval service saved without a mileage gets an inline "Enter the mileage" error (`serviceCompletionValues` in `schedules.ts`, shared with Mark done).
- **Snooze 1 week** (date-driven schedule and service reminders only; needs `maintenance.edit`): the server sets that schedule's `nextDueDate` to 7 days after the later of today and the current due date and records `snoozedUntil` (the same date), only while the card's cycle is still current, then resolves the old cycle's notifications (best effort). The toast offers **Undo** for 10 s. Vehicle services that are due by mileage, warranties and documents cannot be snoozed, because moving a date would not change them. A snoozed reminder (MA-9) carries `snoozed: true` and `snoozedUntil`; `ReminderList` shows it muted (`data-snoozed`, muted surface, outline badge "Snoozed", `reminderBadgeLabel`) with a "Snoozed until Nov 12, 2026" line (`reminderSnoozeLabel`); `reminderGroups` puts it in its own Snoozed group, never Due soon (the Reminders tab's Snoozed group, and last in Overview's Needs doing), and it costs no health. A snooze only applies while `snoozedUntil` equals `nextDueDate` and only to an upcoming or due-soon date, so overdue or due-today work is never hidden, and a vehicle service due or overdue by mileage stays due while its date is snoozed.
- **Explanations on touch** (MA-23): the buttons carry no hover-only `title`. One visible line above each list of reminder cards (`ReminderActionsHint`, Needs doing and Reminders) says what the actions do: "Log service records the cost, provider and parts, and marks it done. Snooze moves a due date 7 days later." The asset card prints the warranty ring's meaning under the name in 14 px text ("Warranty · 198 days", "Warranty ends today", "Warranty ended"; `warrantyCaption`; nothing extra for the Lifetime pill). A "Due soon" badge is amber (warning border and tint), stronger than the neutral Upcoming badge (MA-24).
- **Open**: selects the asset or vehicle and highlights the schedule. Hovering (fine pointer) or keyboard-focusing it for 400 ms shows a peek card (H26) with the item, reminder and due line.
- Cards enter with the shared stagger (≤ 30 ms apart, ≤ 400 ms total). A card that leaves a list fades out in 220 ms, after the check has drawn (instant under reduced motion).

Compatibility with the hourly `householdUpkeep` job: both actions change the schedule's due date, which changes the reminder's `stateKey`, so the next run creates a notification only if the new state is still actionable. A snooze is quiet: the shared builder (`functions/src/shared/maintenance-reminders.ts`) marks a reminder `snoozed` while the schedule's `snoozedUntil` equals its `nextDueDate` and the status is upcoming or due soon, and `actionableReminders` skips snoozed reminders, so no "due soon" push follows a snooze. On the due day the reminder becomes "due today" and notifies as usual. Completing the schedule removes `snoozedUntil` and `snoozedFrom` explicitly (`completedAssetSchedule` / `completedVehicleSchedule`), even when the new cycle lands on the snoozed date. Editing the due date changes `nextDueDate`, so a stale `snoozedUntil` never applies. Other edits to the asset or vehicle keep it: the edit form never sends an unchanged field, and schedules merge by id. A vehicle service that becomes due by mileage still notifies. The resolved old document is never refreshed because upkeep skips documents with `resolvedAt`. A snoozed item appears in the Maintenance Center's Snoozed group (and last in Needs doing), muted, with its new date. Tests: `tests/server/reminders-shared.test.ts` (snoozed maintenance reminders).

### Reminder groups, warning windows and Coming up

- **Statuses**: Overdue (schedules/services) or Expired (warranties, documents), Due today / Expires today, Due soon / Expires soon, Upcoming; plus Snoozed (a muted state of an upcoming or due-soon schedule/service date).
- **Warning windows**: schedules and services are "due soon" within 14 days (`REMINDER_DUE_SOON_DAYS`) or 500 miles; warranties, registrations and inspections "expire soon" within 30 days (`EXPIRY_WARNING_DAYS`, MA-11h), because renewing takes longer. Both are in `functions/src/shared/maintenance-reminders.ts` and apply to the page, the dashboard and the hourly notifications alike.
- **Groups** (`reminderGroups(reminders, kind?)`, `src/lib/maintenance/reminder-groups.ts`): one urgency-ordered list, Overdue or expired, Due today, Due soon, Snoozed, Upcoming, each reminder in exactly one group (earliest date, then fewest miles left, first). `kinds` counts every reminder per kind for the filter chips (`schedule`, `vehicle_service`, `warranty`, `document`); `counts` (`reminderCounts`) gives overdue, due today, due soon, snoozed, upcoming and `dueNow` (today + soon), the one "Due Soon" number.
- **Coming up** (`comingUp(reminders, today, days = 90, { timeZone, predictedDates })`: pass the household time zone the reminders were built in, so a legacy ISO due date lands on its household-local day exactly as the builder reads it (`toDay`; default America/Chicago), `src/lib/maintenance/coming-up.ts`; `ComingUp` component): everything due or expiring from today to today + 90 days, earliest first, grouped by month (`comingUpByMonth`), plus vehicle services with no date in the window listed by miles left (`byMileage`); a caller-supplied predicted day (keyed by `scheduleKey`) places a mileage service on the timeline instead. Overdue items are not "coming up". Upcoming warranties, registrations and inspections are only in the reminders when the builder is asked to look ahead (`buildMaintenanceReminders(..., { expiryLookaheadDays: COMING_UP_DAYS })`); by default the builder reports expiries only inside the warning window, so other lists and the dashboard are unchanged, and upcoming reminders are never notified. Rows are full-width buttons at least `--target-min` tall (48 px on touch screens) that open the item; the component has no motion.

### Schedule presets

`presetSchedulesFor(category, { today?, currentMileage?, newId? })` (`src/lib/maintenance/presets.ts`, MA-27b; offered in the asset and vehicle forms, see "Forms, receipts, deletes and mileage") returns editable schedule drafts for an asset category or `'Vehicle'`; `schedulePresetsFor(category)` returns the catalog entries (label and plain summary) in the same order. Each draft has a new id (`{presetKey}-{random}`, stable once saved), `mode: 'scheduled'`, the interval and, with `today`, a suggested first due day (calendar presets: the next anchor day; others: today + interval); vehicle drafts get the next due mileage when the current mileage is given. Catalog: HVAC filter every 3 months, HVAC tune-up every April (calendar); dryer vent and refrigerator coils yearly, dishwasher filter monthly; water-heater flush yearly, softener salt monthly; smoke-alarm batteries yearly (Electrical and Other), GFCI test monthly; router firmware every 3 months; smart-home sensor batteries yearly; gutters every October and sprinkler winterizing every November (calendar); mower blade every March (calendar); vehicles: oil change 5,000 mi or 6 months, tire rotation 7,500 mi, engine and cabin air filters 15,000 mi or 12 months, brake inspection and wiper blades every 12 months.

### Home health score

`computeHomeHealth` (`src/lib/maintenance/health.ts`, MA-10) uses the same reminder builder as the Reminders tab and the server, evaluated in the household timezone, over active assets and vehicles (retired assets and retired/sold vehicles are ignored; with none left `overall` is null and Overview shows the assets empty state).

Only **tracked** items are scored. An item is tracked when it is an asset with at least one scheduled (non-checklist) schedule, a vehicle with a scheduled service or a registration or inspection date, or any item flagged "Needs attention". This depends only on stored fields, never on today, so the set being averaged cannot shift as time passes. A warranty alone does not make an item tracked. With active items but none tracked, `overall` is `{ score: null, reason: 'not_enough_tracked' }`: the panel says "Not enough tracked yet" with an **Add a schedule** button (`onAddSchedule`, or a link to `/maintenance?tab=assets`), and untracked items have no entry in `targets` (no ring).

Deductions per reminder (`HEALTH_RULES`, `reminderDeduction`):

| Reminder | Points |
|---|---|
| Schedule/service overdue | 10, plus 2 per full 30 days late or per full 500 miles over (whichever is larger), at most 20 |
| Registration/inspection expired | Same as an overdue schedule: 10, plus 2 per full 30 days expired, at most 20 |
| Schedule/service due today | 5 |
| Registration/inspection expires today | 5 |
| Due soon, upcoming, expires soon | 0 |
| Snoozed (an upcoming or due-soon date) | 0 |
| Warranty (ending, ended or lifetime) | 0: warranties never affect health; they appear in reminders and Coming up only |
| Asset or vehicle flagged "Needs attention" | 10 |

- Item score = 100 - its deductions, clamped to 0-100.
- Home score = the average of the tracked items' scores, rounded (not 100 minus every deduction, so one neglected item among many does not zero the home). The panel says "The average of N tracked items".
- There is no bonus for recent logs (`logs` is still accepted by `computeHomeHealth` and ignored).
- Monotonic: with no member action a score never rises as days pass (due soon 0 → due today 5 → overdue 10 rising to 20; expiry can never improve a score because warranties do not count and an expired document costs the same as overdue work). Renewing a registration or inspection (a later date) makes it healthy at once. `tests/unit/maintenance-health.test.ts` walks 60 random households day by day for 420 days across both 2026 DST changes and asserts no item or home score ever rises.
- Bands: 85 and up "In good shape" (success color), 60-84 "Needs some care" (warning), below 60 "Needs attention" (destructive).
- The dashboard's house badge uses the same result (`assembleDashboardFacts` → `facts.health`, null when nothing is tracked) and no longer reads maintenance logs.

The Overview dial uses `NumberTicker` and a ring that sweeps to the value in 550 ms (value only, never blocking); under reduced motion both show the final value at once. Every ring is `role="img"` with a full text equivalent ("Home health 79 out of 100, needs some care."). The "Why this score" list shows the five largest deductions ("−12 Main HVAC System: Replace air filter is 3 months overdue"), with "Show N more", and each row opens its item.

### Insights, filters and QR labels

Status: on the page since M4. The modules below are pure and unit tested, and the components take props only. Where they show: costs, the history lines, the warranty claim checklist and Print label in the asset and vehicle detail; mileage estimates on vehicle reminder cards and in Coming up; filters and search in the bar under the tabs (Home Assets, Vehicles, Reminders, Logs) and the Room chips on Overview; pagination on the Logs tab. Derived values (estimates, filtered lists, Needs doing, Coming up) are memoized once per page render inputs; cost summaries and history lines are memoized in the detail, never per log row (MA-25).

**Which logs count.** `links.ts` (`isLinkedTo`, `linkedLogs`) uses the same rule as the health score: a log's `targetType`, or for legacy logs the one implied by `assetId`/`vehicleId`, and the matching id. Logs of a deleted record are general logs (only `formerTargetName` remains) and are never counted for any item.

**Costs (MA-28, `costs.ts`, `CostSummary`).** Costs are read as US dollars. HomeHub stores no currency per log, so multi-currency households are not supported and nothing is converted. A cost counts when it is a finite number above zero. A missing or non-numeric cost is left out and reported ("2 logs without a cost"). Zero and negative costs are left out silently.
- **Lifetime cost** is the sum of the live-linked logs.
- **Spend per year** groups costs by calendar year in the household timezone (`toDay`), newest first. Logs with a missing or unreadable date go under "Undated", last; they are never dropped. The years always add up to the lifetime total.
- **Cost per mile** (vehicles only) is the lifetime cost divided by the miles between the lowest and highest readings (linked logs' `mileage` plus `currentMileage`). It needs at least two readings spanning more than 500 miles, otherwise "Not enough mileage data"; with no costs it reads "No costs recorded yet".
- **Repair versus replace:** when the counted costs of `repair` and `replacement` logs pass 50% of `purchasePrice`, the summary reads "Repairs have cost about N% of the purchase price" (N rounded). When there are repair costs but the price is missing, zero, negative or not a number, it reads "Add the purchase price to compare".
- **Rounding:** totals show in whole dollars (`formatWholeDollars`); cost per mile shows in cents ("$0.12 per mile"), because whole dollars would read $0.

**Mileage estimate (MA-29, `mileage-prediction.ts`).** This is an estimate shown beside a mileage reminder. It never replaces the due date or mileage trigger in `maintenance-reminders.ts`.
- **Readings** (`vehicleReadings`) are linked logs with both a mileage and a readable date, plus `currentMileage` when the vehicle has a `mileageUpdatedAt` (written by the server's completion and log operations, MA-6). A current mileage without that date is not a dated reading.
- **Cleaning** (`cleanReadings`) keeps readings from the last 365 days and none after today. It then keeps the longest date-ordered chain in which each reading is at or above the previous one and rises no faster than 1,000 mi/day; between equally long chains, the one whose fastest step is slower wins. A backdated reading or a typo, too high or too low, drops out without taking its neighbours with it.
- **Fit:** after cleaning, an estimate needs at least 3 readings spanning at least 30 days. The rate is the Theil-Sen median of the pairwise slopes, and the range uses the 25th to 75th percentile slopes (the slow end is never below half the median).
- **Result:** `predictDueDate(nextDueMileage, readings, today)` counts from the latest cleaned reading and returns `{date, low, high, basis, milesPerDay}`. Otherwise it returns `{insufficient}` with one of `no-due-mileage`, `too-few-readings`, `too-short-span`, `no-increase`, `already-reached` (the reminder already says it is due) or `too-far` (more than 730 days away, decided on the day count before any date is built, so a very slow rate cannot overflow the four-digit-year calendar helpers). An estimate earlier than today is moved to today, and the late end of the range is capped at 730 days.
- **Copy:** "Estimated around Mar 12, based on 6 readings", with the year added when it is not this year. On a reminder card (vehicle services with a due mileage; `reminderMileageEstimates`, keyed by reminder id) it is an extra line with a gauge icon, followed by "An estimate from past readings; the mileage above is what counts." The same estimated day places the service in Coming up (keyed by `scheduleKey`).

**Service history line (MA-32, `history-line.ts`).** "Replace air filter last done 3 months ago by Sam". The source is the newest live-linked log dated on or before today in the household zone; on the same day, the later `createdAt` wins, then the id. Relative time reads "today", "yesterday", "N days ago", then weeks, months and years, using the reminder cards' `spanLabel`.
- **Per schedule** (`logMatchesSchedule`): a completion's log (server-written) names its schedule in `completion.scheduleId`; when the caller passes the schedule's ids (its `id` and `scheduleRef`, the legacy key a legacy schedule is stored under), only that id counts, whatever the log's title (an edited Log service title still matches; a log titled like another schedule does not). Other logs, and calls with only a name, match by title (or legacy `item`), ignoring case and spacing. The detail shows one line per schedule row and one for the whole record under its name.
- **"by …"** comes only from `doneByName`, which the server writes on new logs (M1). It goes through `publicDisplayName(name, '')`, so an email or a blank name leaves "by …" out. Legacy logs never get a guessed name.

**Filters and search (MA-21, `filters.ts`, `FilterChips`).**
- **URL:** `parseFilters` and `serializeFilters` read and write `?location=`, `?category=` and `?q=` (search capped at 100 characters). Serializing copies the current query and changes only those three keys, so `tab`, `asset`, `vehicle`, `log` and `schedule` are kept.
- **Location** matching ignores case and extra spaces. "Unassigned" matches assets with no location. `matchesRoom(location, roomName)` is the same comparison and is exported for MA-26 (a registered tablet's room).
- **Categories** are the asset categories. Vehicles have neither field: they form the category "Vehicle" and are hidden while a location filter is on.
- **Logs and reminders** follow their asset or vehicle. General logs, including logs of deleted records, are hidden while a location or category filter is on.
- **Search** requires every word to appear in one field of the item: assets by name, brand, model, location and notes; vehicles by nickname, make, model and plate; logs by title (or `item`), provider, parts and notes; reminders by title and the item's name.
- **Chips:** `locationChips` and `categoryChips` list the distinct values with counts. The label is the first spelling seen; "Unassigned" and "Vehicle" come last.
- **Pagination:** `paginateLogs(logs, page)` gives 20 logs per page in a stable order: date newest first (unreadable dates last), then `createdAt` newest first, then id. Out-of-range pages are clamped. `pageOfLog(logs, id)` is the page a log is on (the Logs tab opens it for `?log=`); the list goes back to page 1 when the filters change.
- **On the page:** the filters are read from the URL in `useMaintenanceNavigation` (`filters`, `setFilters`); every write replaces the URL in place and is marked applied, so it never re-runs the deep-link plan. `changeTab` keeps the three keys and drops record links; an in-page link to a record (`openTarget`) keeps them too, and the record asked for is shown even when the filters would hide its card. The Overview shows only the Room chips (`?location=`).
- **`FilterChips`** is one horizontally scrollable row ("All" plus each chip) of `aria-pressed` toggle buttons, each at least `--target-min` (44 px, 48 px on touch). Choosing the selected chip clears it.

**QR labels (MA-31, `qr-label.ts`, `PrintLabel`).**
- **Link:** `maintenanceDeepLink(origin, target)` returns `<origin>/maintenance?asset=<id>` or `?vehicle=<id>` with the id URL-encoded. Only the scheme, host and port of `origin` are kept. A non-http(s) origin or an empty id gives null. The link carries no token, household id or credentials. Opening it still needs a signed-in member with `maintenance.view` (a signed-out visitor gets the in-place sign-in prompt on the same URL; a member without the permission sees "Maintenance isn't shared with you"). An id that is not in the member's household (deleted, or never theirs) selects the list tab and shows "The requested home asset is no longer available." (or the vehicle version), the existing deep-link behavior in `MaintenanceLogClient`.
- **Drawing:** the QR is drawn as an inline SVG path from `@zxing/library`'s encoder, the library already used for the device setup QR, so no dependency was added. It uses error correction M, UTF-8 and a 4-module quiet zone, dark on white in every theme.
- **`PrintLabel`** (in the detail behind **Print label**, loaded with `next/dynamic`, `ssr: false`, on first open; the link is `maintenanceDeepLink(window.location.origin, …)`; vehicles print no location line) shows the name, the location, the QR (`role="img"`, "QR code that opens … in HomeHub") and "Scan to open in HomeHub", plus a "Print label" button.
- **Printing:** "Print label" copies the label into a sheet at the end of `<body>`, marks `<html>` (`data-print-label`) and calls `window.print()` (`beginLabelPrint`, `PRINT_LABEL_CSS`). The `@media print` rules apply only while that mark is set: every other child of `<body>` is removed from the layout, so the label prints alone on one page even from a dialog, black on white in a dark theme. The sheet never shows on screen. The sheet and mark are removed on `afterprint` and on unmount, so other printing is unchanged. This was checked once in Chromium (a 4-page page printed as 1 page with black text on white); there is no automated browser test yet.

## Data Model and Persistence

- Assets: `households/{householdId}/home-assets/{assetId}`. Optional `warrantyLifetime: true` marks a lifetime warranty.
- Vehicles: `households/{householdId}/vehicles/{vehicleId}`.
- Logs: `households/{householdId}/maintenance/{logId}`.
- Attachment metadata: `households/{householdId}/maintenance-attachments/{attachmentId}`; an image may add `displayPath`/`displayUrl` and `thumbnailPath`/`thumbnailUrl` (its derived copies, siblings in the same folder).
- Delete tombstones: `households/{householdId}/maintenance-deletions/{targetType}~{targetId}` (server only; present while a delete with files is unfinished).
- Op ledger: `households/{householdId}/maintenance-ops/{opId}` (server only; see "Server operations"). `uploadedByUid` and `uploadedByName` record the uploader; the name is the profile's display name through `publicDisplayName` ("Member" when it is missing or an email), never the email. `scripts/ops/scrub-remaining-emails.mjs` rewrites older names that are an email (see [Household Governance](../household-governance/README.md), "Email copies").
- Reminder notifications: `households/{householdId}/notifications/{notificationId}`.
- Attachment objects: `households/{householdId}/maintenance/{targetType}/{targetId}/{attachmentId}-{sanitizedFileName}`.

Shared types include `HomeAsset`, `HomeAssetSchedule`, `MaintenanceScheduleMode`, `MaintenanceLastCompletion`, `Vehicle`, `VehicleServiceSchedule`, `MaintenanceLog`, and `MaintenanceAttachment`. Schedules carry a stable `id` (assigned on first server touch or by the backfill; see "Schedule identity") and `mode` of `scheduled` or `checklist`, and optionally `snoozedUntil`, `snoozedFrom`, `anchor` (`completion`/`calendar`), `anchorMonth` and `anchorDay`; the server stores `lastCompletion: { opId, byUid, byName, completedDate, at, cycle }` on a completed schedule. Vehicles gain `mileageUpdatedAt` (household day of the reading that set `currentMileage`). Logs may carry the server-written stored summary fields `summaryNotesHash`, `summaryModel` and `summaryAt` with `summary`. Logs written by the server add `doneByUid`, `doneByName`, `createdAtServer` (Timestamp) and `opId`, and a completion's log adds `completion` (see "Server operations"); older logs have none of these. Legacy name-only schedules normalize to checklist presentation without a destructive migration. Legacy logs fall back from missing `title` to `item`, infer target type from IDs, and default missing log type to `other`.

When an asset or vehicle is deleted, linked historical logs are preserved as general maintenance records. The original relationship is retained in optional `formerTargetType` and `formerTargetName` fields while the live `assetId` or `vehicleId` is removed.

Deep links use `/maintenance?asset={id}`, `?vehicle={id}`, `?log={id}`, or `?tab={overview|assets|vehicles|reminders|logs}`, optionally with `?location=`, `?category=` and `?q=` (MA-21). Choosing a tab replaces the URL with `?tab=` (Overview has no parameter) and the filters, so refresh and Back keep the tab. On a phone a record link opens that record's detail sheet. A deep link is applied once per URL after every listener has answered once (`deepLinkPlan` in `src/lib/maintenance/deep-link.ts`), so a collection arriving late never yields a false "no longer available", and "no longer available" also waits until every listener has answered from the server, not only from the persistent cache (a record created on another device may be missing from the cache); later changes (Mark done, another device's edit) do not pull the page back to the linked record. Schedule reminders add a URL-encoded `schedule={ownerId}:{scheduleId}`. Valid links select the matching tab/detail and highlight schedules/logs; missing IDs select the relevant list tab and show a non-destructive status message. Legacy `targetType`/`targetId` links remain accepted.

## Authentication, Roles, and Security

Clients derive household paths from the authenticated user. Pending `newuser` members are denied and legacy members remain supported.

Attachment metadata creation validates matching `householdId`, target/category values, path prefix, `uploadedByUid == request.auth.uid`, and that the target exists (`maintenance_log` → `maintenance/{id}`, `home_asset` → `home-assets/{id}`, `vehicle` → `vehicles/{id}`). It needs `maintenance.edit`, or (MA-16/MA-30, the scoped receipt rule) `maintenance.createLogs` for a `receipt`, `invoice` or `photo` on a log whose server-written `doneByUid` is the caller and whose `createdAtServer` is less than 30 minutes old (`ownNewLogAttachment`); quick logs from the command palette carry neither field and stay excluded. Storage uploads mirror it (`ownNewLogFile`: a `maintenance_log` path, the same log test, and the object's `uploadedByUid` metadata equal to the caller; at most two Firestore reads, the member and the log); Storage allows approved members, limits files to under 15 MiB, and accepts images, PDF, plain text, Word, and OpenXML documents. Storage deletes still need `maintenance.delete`; a member's orphaned upload is removed by the server (`cleanupMaintenanceUpload`). Record deletes with files are a server action (`maintenance.delete`); `maintenance-deletions` is denied to every client.

Firestore rules enforce the granular permissions (with per-member overrides): reads need `maintenance.view`; asset/vehicle create and update and attachment metadata create need `maintenance.edit`; log create needs `maintenance.createLogs` and log update `maintenance.edit`; deletes need `maintenance.delete`; resolving a maintenance notification needs `maintenance.edit` and may only change `resolvedAt`/`resolvedBy` (the page's record deletion still uses it; completion and snooze resolve on the server). The page hides controls a member cannot use (see capabilities); the rules remain the authority for client writes. Completion, snooze and log/asset/vehicle saves are server actions that check the same permissions (see "Server operations"); the client write rules stay for record deletion and older clients. `maintenance-ops` is denied to every client (`allow read, write: if false`).

Shape validation (QA-8): logs, home assets and vehicles may only set the fields of `MaintenanceLog`, `HomeAsset` and `Vehicle` in `src/lib/types.ts` (logs include the legacy `item`, `summary`, `receiptUrl`, `formerTargetType` and `formerTargetName`). A `householdId` field, when present, must equal the household in the path, on create and update. Enums are checked (`targetType`, log `type`, asset and vehicle `status`, `formerTargetType`), numbers must be numbers (no range: the forms accept any finite value), `warrantyLifetime` must be a boolean (assets only), short text is bounded at 1000 characters and notes, parts and summaries at 20000; `schedules` and `serviceSchedules` must be lists of at most 100 entries (their entries are not inspected). On update only added or changed keys are checked, so a record with legacy fields stays updatable. The server-owned fields (`doneByUid`, `doneByName`, `opId`, `completion`, `createdAtServer` on logs, `mileageUpdatedAt` on vehicles) are not in the client allowlists, so a client can neither forge nor change them. Schedule entries remain an unvalidated list in the rules (`lastCompletion` inside one could be written by a client with `maintenance.edit`); the server treats it as data and never trusts it for authorization.

## Integrations and Background Processing

- AI summaries use the authenticated `summarizeStoredMaintenanceLog` action (stored on the log by the server with its notes hash, see "Forms, receipts, deletes and mileage"); original notes are unchanged. The receipt reader is `readMaintenanceReceipt`; its answer is never stored. Both are in [AI and OpenAI](../ai-genkit/README.md).
- The hourly `householdUpkeep` also finishes maintenance deletes that stopped part-way (`resumeMaintenanceDeletions`, a collection-group read of `maintenance-deletions`).
- The homepage overview uses `maintenance.view` to calculate status, schedule/checklist, date, mileage, warranty, registration, and inspection signals server-side, with the dashboard's own `computeMaintenanceFacts` (`summarizeOverviewMaintenance` in `src/ai/overview-fact-helpers.ts`), so its overdue and due-soon counts match the dashboard and this module. Only bounded labels/dates/counts reach the optional narrative; serial numbers, VINs, plates, costs, notes, and attachment paths are excluded.
- Date due-soon threshold: 14 days for schedules and services; 30 days for warranties, registrations and inspections (`EXPIRY_WARNING_DAYS`). A lifetime warranty never notifies (an already open warranty notice for it stays until it expires or is resolved).
- Mileage due-soon threshold: 500 miles.
- The hourly `householdUpkeep` Cloud Function (`functions/src/index.ts` → `functions/src/reminders.ts`) runs the same idempotent step for every household; the Chores page also runs it on demand through `syncHouseholdUpkeep` (`src/app/chores/actions.ts`, authorized by `chores.view`) so a new recurring assignment appears immediately. A notification for something still overdue is re-surfaced within two days of expiry rather than allowed to disappear (so the Firestore TTL policy on `expiresAt` never deletes a still-actionable reminder): its `expiresAt` is extended and its `createdAt` moves to the refresh time, so the 7-day notification lists include it again, while an earlier dismissal still stands. A resolved one is reopened only if upkeep still finds it actionable with the same state (for example the completion never reached the schedule) and it was resolved before that run read its data; it sends no new push. Upkeep writes nothing for a household being deleted.
- The same upkeep run resolves open maintenance reminder notifications (all five maintenance source types, with a string `stateKey`) whose `sourceType`/`sourceId`/`stateKey` is no longer in the current actionable set: a completed, snoozed, edited or removed schedule, or a retired or deleted record. Each is re-checked in a transaction that re-reads the asset or vehicle collection, like the medication pass. This is the durable retry of the server's post-commit cleanup. Tests: `tests/server/household-upkeep.test.ts`.
- New notification documents can trigger FCM through the shared Cloud Function.
- Physical notification expiration requires Firestore TTL on `expiresAt`.

## Invariants and Failure Behavior

- Invalid/missing dates are ignored; mileage reminders require current and next-due mileage.
- Scheduled entries require a date/frequency or mileage due mechanism appropriate to their owner. Explicit checklist entries may contain only a name and never show a Complete action.
- Notification IDs hash stable source type, persisted owner/schedule identity, and a `stateKey` containing due status plus meaningful due date/mileage. Transactions make overlapping synchronization idempotent.
- Read/dismiss history remains on an existing reminder document. A changed due status/date/mileage produces a new identity; completing a schedule resolves the current cycle and advances the embedded schedule.
- Asset and vehicle photos are plain `<img>` elements on the attachment's `downloadUrl` (`alt=""`, the name is printed next to them) inside a fixed 80 px band, so loading never shifts layout; an image that fails to load falls back to the category art. A brand logo reserves its box while the artwork chunk loads, so a badge never flashes before a logo. Only image content types (PNG, JPEG, WebP, GIF, AVIF) are used, or a HEIC/HEIF photo through its JPEG display copy, preferring the `photo` category; receipts and invoices are never used (MA-11b); SVG and PDFs are ignored.
- Field problems are shown inline: schedule rows use `FieldError` (focus moves to the first bad schedule name), and the log dialog's missing asset/vehicle uses the form field's message. Failures use `toastError`/`friendlyError`, except AI summary errors, whose messages are already user-safe from the AI module.
- "Summarize with AI" is offered only for notes with at least two statements (split on `. ! ? ;` or line breaks, each two words or more) or at least 40 words.
- Overview labels: "Logged, last 30 days" counts logs dated in the last 30 household days (today and the 29 before); "Recent logs" shows the five newest logs by date (undated last).
- Home Assets layout: the asset card grid uses `content-start`, so cards keep their content height instead of stretching to the selected asset's detail panel, and the page never scrolls sideways (regression-tested at 1009 px in `tests/e2e/navigation-layout.spec.ts`, where the layout is the phone one: no detail column). From `lg` the detail column is sticky with its own scroll (`max-h: 100dvh - 5.5rem`), so its bottom is reachable on short landscape screens (`tests/e2e/navigation-layout.spec.ts` sweeps every tab at 1263x818, 1263x722, 1264x843, the WT-25 sizes, 1024x600, 1440x900 and 390x844: no sideways scroll, one tab row, the detail's bottom reachable; `tests/e2e/tablet-pages.spec.ts` adds `?tab=assets`, `?tab=vehicles` and `?tab=reminders` to the 48 px / no-overlap check). The attachment Upload control is a label around a plain visually hidden `<input type="file">` with `relative overflow-hidden`; the shared `Input` component is not used there because its `w-full` overrides `sr-only` and widened the page.
- Asset, vehicle and log deletion require confirmation and delete the record's own attachments with it in one server operation (MA-22); embedded schedules/checklists are deleted with the owning registry document. The confirmation stays open until the answer and shows a failure inline (MA-11a).
- Maintenance summary requests require verified household membership plus `maintenance.view`; provider failures are sanitized and non-destructive.
- Registry deletion runs on the server (`deleteMaintenanceCascade`): linked logs become general history and keep their files, active schedule/warranty/registration/inspection notifications for the deleted record are resolved, and only the record's own folder and attachments are deleted. It is idempotent and resumable (tombstone; retry or hourly upkeep). Unrelated records and other records' files are unchanged.
- Log, attachment, and embedded schedule/checklist removal use target-specific confirmation dialogs; attachment Storage cleanup remains best effort under the existing metadata workflow.
- Storage is deleted before metadata; a missing object is tolerated. On upload, if the metadata write is refused (for example the record or a completion's log was deleted meanwhile, which the rules check, or the 30-minute receipt window passed) or a later upload step fails, every object just uploaded is removed by the server cleanup (MA-11k); an upload never hangs (30 s stall watchdog, 20 s step deadlines, offline refused up front).
- Loading uses four live listeners; the page waits for all four before its first render of data. A listener failing before that shows the error card (Try again resubscribes); after it, records stay and the listener retries. A refused listener clears every collection and shows the "not shared with you" state.
- A completion writes its log, the schedule, the vehicle's mileage and the ledger in one transaction; a cycle is completed at most once (identified by its due values, so a cycle recreated with the same values by an edit is a new one). Next due is never lost: a completion that would leave no next due is refused.
- New asset, vehicle and log ids are `{slug}-{part of the op id}` and cannot collide; the command palette's quick log keeps its own `quickCreateId`.
- Notification cleanup after a completion or snooze is best effort (`cleanup: 'pending'`); upkeep finishes it within the hour.
- Deleted asset/vehicle deep links remain on the relevant list tab and report that the requested record is unavailable. New schedule notification source IDs use persisted schedule IDs; legacy schedules receive a deterministic compatibility key.
