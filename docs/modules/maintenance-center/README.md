# Maintenance Center

## Purpose and Scope

This module owns home assets, vehicles, maintenance logs, embedded service schedules, maintenance attachments, due-state calculation, and maintenance notification creation. It does not own general notification presentation, shared AI configuration, or background push delivery.

## User-Facing Capabilities

- Use Overview, Home Assets, Vehicles, Reminders, and Logs tabs at `/maintenance`.
- Create, view, edit, and delete assets; manage multiple schedules and linked logs.
- Create, view, edit, and delete vehicles; separate sold/retired vehicles; manage multiple service schedules.
- Create general, asset, or vehicle logs; view, edit, delete, attach files, and request an AI summary.
- Complete an asset/vehicle schedule by creating a routine log and advancing due values (on the server, once per cycle, with a 10 s Undo; see "Server operations").
- Upload, open/download, categorize, and delete attachments.
- Review date/mileage reminders and act on them in place: **Mark done**, **Log service**, **Snooze 1 week**, or **Open** the asset/vehicle (see "Reminder actions").
- See a 0-100 **home health score** on Overview (dial, band label and the deductions behind it, each linking to its item from a row at least `--target-min` tall: 44 px, 48 px on touch screens) and a small health ring on every active asset and vehicle card and detail panel (see "Home health score").
- Asset and vehicle cards are photo-first (H30, `PhotoBand`): the newest image attachment of that asset or vehicle (`pickTargetPhoto`, preferring the `photo` category) fills a fixed 80 px band over a blurred copy of itself; without one, the category icon (a car for vehicles) on a token gradient. Vehicles upload photos through the same attachment panel and Storage path as assets (`households/{householdId}/maintenance/vehicle/{vehicleId}/…`, already allowed by the existing `storage.rules` match), so no rule changed.
- **Brand logos** (H30): `BrandLogo` (`src/components/brand/brand-logo.tsx`) matches an asset's `brand` or a vehicle's `make` against a catalog of 223 household brands (`src/lib/maintenance/brands.ts`: cars and motorcycles, appliances, small kitchen, cleaning, HVAC and water, smart home and electronics, tools and lawn, plumbing, pool). 68 brands have a monochrome Simple Icons mark (CC0 path data in `src/components/brand/brand-marks.ts`, loaded once as its own lazy chunk by dynamic import, never in a first-paint bundle and never fetched from the network); the other 155 get one consistent monogram badge (short name or initials in the app's headline type). Both draw in `currentColor`, so they follow every theme. Matching ignores case, accents, punctuation, spaces, "&"/"+"/"and" and trailing company words (Inc, Appliances, Motor Company, Electronics…), knows aliases (Chevy→Chevrolet, VW→Volkswagen, Mercedes/Benz→Mercedes-Benz, GE Appliances→GE, SharkNinja→Ninja, Roomba→iRobot, Kitchen Aid→KitchenAid, Honda Power→Honda…), accepts leading words ("Whirlpool Gold"→Whirlpool), and tolerates typos with optimal-string-alignment Damerau-Levenshtein distance 1 for 5–7 letters and 2 for 8 or more, choosing the single closest brand and refusing ties; names of 4 letters or fewer (GE, LG, Kia) match only exactly, and placeholders ("Generic", "N/A") never match. Logos show once on each asset and vehicle card (beside the title), in the detail panel header and in the Attention Queue. Where the brand is not printed as text (asset cards, Attention Queue assets) the mark is an image named after the brand ("Bosch"); elsewhere it is `aria-hidden`.
- **Warranty countdown ring** (H10): an asset with a warranty end date shows a small ring beside its health ring, filled with the share of the warranty term still left and the days left inside ("9d"; years from 1,000 days, "0d" once ended). Its accessible name reads e.g. "Main HVAC System: Warranty: 9 days left of 1,551". The ring turns amber within 30 days and grey once ended. HomeHub stores no warranty start date, so the term runs from the asset's **purchase date** when it is set and earlier than the end date; otherwise the term is assumed to be **one year** ending on the warranty end date (`warrantyCountdown()` in `src/lib/maintenance/warranty.ts`, `WARRANTY_FALLBACK_DAYS = 365`). Days are counted on the household's local day. The ring sweeps from full on load (550 ms, value only; instant under reduced motion).
- **Odometer** (H10): a vehicle card shows its mileage as odometer wheels (at least six, leading zeros), each rolling to its digit (transform only, 550 ms, wheels a few ms apart; final digits at once under reduced motion). Screen readers get the plain value ("48,750 miles"), which is also part of the card's accessible name; the wheels are `aria-hidden`.
- Detail panels list only the fields that have a value (no "Not recorded"); Edit, "Add details" (its accessible name adds "(N empty)") and Delete share one action row that fits the desktop detail column, with Delete set apart on the right; it wraps only on very narrow screens. The attachment header (category, Upload) wraps by its own width, so it never overflows the narrow detail column.
- Controls follow the member's maintenance permissions: Add/Edit/Complete/Snooze/Upload need `maintenance.edit`, Add Log needs `maintenance.createLogs`, Mark done and Complete need both, Delete (records, logs, attachments) needs `maintenance.delete`. A member without `maintenance.view` sees a "not shared with you" state and no queries run. The server actions check the same permissions from the verified ID token (see "Server operations"); completing never needs `notifications.view`.

## Architecture and Data Flow

`MaintenanceLogClient` keeps four live `onSnapshot` listeners on the household's `home-assets` (by name), `vehicles` (by nickname), `maintenance` logs (newest date first) and `maintenance-attachments` (newest first) (MA-2, `useMaintenanceLiveData` in `src/components/maintenance/use-maintenance-data.ts`, state in `src/lib/maintenance/live-data.ts`). Each is the shared `resilientListener` (`src/lib/dashboard/resilient-listener.ts`): a failed listener resubscribes with bounded backoff (1 s doubling to 60 s, with jitter) and at once on `online` or when the page becomes visible; only a server-confirmed snapshot (`includeMetadataChanges`, `!fromCache`) resets the schedule, and metadata-only snapshots change no state. The listeners are keyed by household and member (`{householdId}|{uid}`; none while `maintenance.view` is missing) and stop when the key changes or the page unmounts; their state is keyed the same way, so a record of the previous household is never shown in the next one, not even for a frame. The page is ready once all four have answered for the key: until then it shows the loading skeleton (only on that first load), and a failure before that is the error card, whose **Try again** resubscribes at once (`retryNow`). After the first load a failing listener keeps the records it has and retries quietly. A listener refused with `permission-denied` (retried twice, about 3 s, since a token refresh can race a read) settles on `unauthorized`: every collection is cleared and the page shows the "not shared with you" state, never stale protected records; it stays so until the key changes (another household or member, or `maintenance.view` revoked and granted again). Changes from this page's server actions, from quick add (`homehub:data-changed` is not needed) and from other devices arrive through the listeners, so no action re-reads the collections after a write (MA-25c). It does not query notifications on load; it reads them only when a registry record is deleted (the record's source types; the id prefix is matched in the browser), to resolve that record's reminders. Completion and snooze resolve reminders on the server (see "Server operations"). The deletion query is audience-constrained (`notificationsBySourceQuery`, `array-contains-any [uid, '*']`); maintenance reminders are household-wide (`['*']`), so every member finds all of them, while a reminder without `audience` (not backfilled) is not found and stays open until it expires. Assets and vehicles embed schedule arrays. Reminder cards and server notifications share one builder (`functions/src/shared/maintenance-reminders.ts`) evaluated in the household timezone. Retired assets and retired/sold vehicles produce no reminders; warranties and vehicle documents "expire" rather than become "overdue". Notification documents are created by the server, not by opening this page.

### Server operations (M1: the completion contract)

Completing, snoozing and saving a log, asset or vehicle are server actions (`src/app/maintenance/actions.ts`, `'use server'`), never client writes. Each verifies the ID token (`authorizeHouseholdAction`) and checks the member's permissions: complete and its Undo need `maintenance.edit` **and** `maintenance.createLogs`; snooze, its Undo, and saving an asset or vehicle need `maintenance.edit`; creating a log needs `maintenance.createLogs` and editing one `maintenance.edit`. Requests are shape- and size-checked (96 KB) and fail closed with a typed, user-safe result (`{ ok: false, code, message }`). Who did it and when never come from the client. Each operation in `src/lib/server/maintenance-operations.ts` has the shape `(db, householdId, input, ctx: OperationContext)` and is one Admin SDK transaction in the chore-completion order (device hooks' `precheck` first, then reads, writes, `finalize` last), so tablet device actions can call them later; none exist yet. The plans are pure functions in `src/lib/maintenance/operations.ts`.

- **Op id and ledger.** Every request carries a client op id (a UUID taken once per press; only Retry reuses it). `households/{h}/maintenance-ops/{opId}` records `kind` (`complete`, `snooze`, `save_asset`, `save_vehicle`, `save_log`), `actorUid`, `actorName`, `ownerType`, `ownerId`, `scheduleId`, `logId`, `appliedAt` (server time), `undoneAt`, the stored result, the completion record (completions) or `before`/`after` (snoozes), and the notification cleanup target. The same op id from the same person and kind replays the stored result and writes nothing; reused by someone else or for another kind it is refused (`conflict`). Entries are kept (no TTL).
- **Completion.** The request names the schedule by its stable id (or legacy key, below) and the cycle the person saw (`seen`: its `nextDueDate`/`nextDueMileage`). In one transaction: if the schedule's `lastCompletion.cycle` equals `seen` the answer is `already_done` with who and when (checked first, so a completion that left the due values unchanged still counts once); if the current cycle differs from `seen` it is `changed`; a missing record or schedule is `gone`. Otherwise the values are validated (`completedDate` a real `yyyy-MM-dd` not after the household's today; mileage an integer 0 to 2,000,000; a vehicle service with `intervalMiles` or a `nextDueMileage` needs a reading, `mileage_required`; next due mileage above the reading) and a completion that would leave neither a next due date nor mileage is refused (`next_due_required`). Then, together: the log (id `{slug}-{opId}`) with `doneByUid`, `doneByName` (the member's display name through `publicDisplayName`, never an email), `createdAtServer`, `opId` and `completion: { scheduleId, ownerType, ownerId, cycle, before, after }` holding exactly the fields this completion changed (`nextDueDate`, `nextDueMileage`, `lastCompletedDate`, `lastCompletedMileage`, `snoozedUntil`, `lastCompletion`, and the vehicle's `currentMileage`/`mileageUpdatedAt`); the schedule patched by id (snooze ended, `lastCompletion` set); the vehicle's mileage; and the ledger entry. Two sessions, a double tap or a retry with different op ids therefore make one log.
- **Mileage (MA-6).** `currentMileage` becomes `max(current, reading)`: a backdated or lower reading never lowers it (the log keeps its own reading). `mileageUpdatedAt` (household day) is set when the reading is at least the current one. Vehicle logs saved through the log operation follow the same rule, applied to the vehicle the log points at after the save is merged onto the freshly read log (so a log moved to another vehicle by someone else meanwhile never raises the old vehicle's odometer); a reading counts when the save set it or pointed the log at a vehicle. An explicit change in the vehicle form is written as entered (a correction). The page asks once before saving a reading lower than the recorded one or more than 20,000 above it (`mileageWarning`), with separate copy: a log or completion reading says the vehicle keeps the higher reading, the vehicle form says the odometer will be corrected down (or set) to the value entered. The server accepts either.
- **Notification cleanup.** After the commit (never inside it, and never with the person's own notification access) the schedule's open reminder notifications whose `sourceId` is the schedule's id, its legacy key or a pre-key legacy id (`{owner}-{index}:{status}`) and whose state is no longer actionable are resolved (`resolvedBy` the actor). Only `sourceType ==` is queried. A failure returns `cleanup: 'pending'`; the completion stays saved and the hourly upkeep pass resolves the rest.
- **Undo** (completion and snooze; only the original actor; `UNDO_WINDOW_MS` = 10 s from the ledger's `appliedAt` plus a 5 s server grace for latency) is compare-and-revert: each field the operation changed goes back to its `before` value only while it still holds the `after` value; a field someone changed since keeps its value and is returned in `kept` ("The due date was changed after you marked it done, so it stays Mar 3."). The completion's log is deleted; if files are attached to that log the Undo is refused with `has_attachments` ("Remove the attached files first, or keep it done.") and nothing changes. New attachment metadata needs its target to exist (rules), and the Undo transaction holds the log, so no file can be attached while it runs. A schedule completed again since is `superseded`: nothing changes and the log stays (the decision for "completion, another completion, Undo of the first"). A schedule or record deleted meanwhile is never recreated. A vehicle mileage raised by a later reading stays. Because values are compared, an edit back to exactly the value the operation wrote is indistinguishable from no edit. After an Undo the next upkeep run reopens the resolved reminder (same identity, still actionable).
- **Snooze** (MA-3b) patches only `nextDueDate` and `snoozedUntil` of the schedule named by id, if its cycle is still `seen`, under the card's rules (`snoozedDueDate`, `canSnoozeReminder`: never for a service due by mileage), stores `before`/`after` in the ledger and resolves the old cycle's reminder after the commit.
- **Edits** (MA-3c). Asset, vehicle and log saves send `{ id?, base, values }`; `base` is what the form showed when it opened. For an existing record only fields whose value differs from `base` are written onto the freshly read document (`null` removes). Schedules merge by id: changed fields of a schedule the form had are patched, a schedule the form removed is removed, one someone added meanwhile is kept, and a new one is appended, so a completion or snooze that happened while the form was open survives and a removed schedule is never resurrected. Formats (real days, required names, ranges, options) are checked only for changed fields, so an untouched legacy value never blocks an unrelated edit. A record deleted meanwhile is `not_found`, never recreated. New records get `{slug}-{10 characters of the op id}`, which cannot collide and is the same on a retry. Log edits can never change `doneByUid`, `doneByName`, `opId`, `completion` or `createdAtServer`.
- **Schedule identity.** A schedule without an id is named by its legacy key `legacy-{index}-{slug}` (the reminder builder's compatibility key), and the first operation that touches its record stores that key as its id (`-2`, `-3` only if taken), so reminder `sourceId`s, deep links and open notifications do not change. `scripts/ops/backfill-maintenance-schedule-ids.mjs` does the same for records nobody touches (dry run by default, `--apply` to write, `--project <id>`; idempotent; only the schedule array of a record with id-less schedules is written, in a transaction; a malformed list is skipped; counts only). Its pure twin `scripts/ops/maintenance-schedule-ids.mjs` is checked against the server's `withScheduleIds`.

In the page, every save, complete and snooze button is disabled with a spinner while its request is in flight, and a dialog cannot be closed until the outcome is known. Offline (`navigator.onLine` false) the page says "Needs a connection. Your entries are kept." and keeps the dialog and its input. With no answer after 20 s (the deadline starts before the ID token is fetched and covers the whole call) it says "No answer from HomeHub. It may have been saved." with **Retry**, which resends the same request with the same op id (a card action shows this as a toast). Until a definite answer, pressing Save again in that dialog (or the card action again) also sends that same request, never a new op id, so an uncertain save is never duplicated; opening the dialog afresh discards it (`createPendingRequests`, `callWithDeadline` in `src/lib/maintenance/action-call.ts`). `already_done` shows "Already done by Sam on Mar 1." and `changed` "This changed since you opened it.", the listeners already show the current state.
The held requests and the dialog notices live in `useMaintenanceOps` (`src/components/maintenance/use-maintenance-ops.tsx`); each dialog's opening discards its key's held request.

Motion (H12): asset and vehicle grids enter with `Stagger`; selecting a card morphs it into the detail panel with a View Transition (`useInPageMorph` from `src/components/shopping/use-in-page-morph.ts`: the shared `view-transition-name` sits on the clicked card before the change and on the detail card after it, and is removed when the transition ends). Reduced motion, a browser without View Transitions, or a modified click (Shift/Ctrl/Cmd/Alt) just selects. The Overview and Reminders summary counts and the 30-day spend tick up with `NumberTicker` (`src/components/motion/number-ticker.tsx`): its only text node is always the final value, and the moving digits are painted by CSS, so the text and accessible name never carry an in-between number. Attention Queue links have peek cards (brand/model, health, location or mileage).

Only the first load shows the loading skeleton, and it is the same component as the route's `loading.tsx` (`MaintenanceSkeleton`: header with Add Log, the tab row, the Overview cards), so on a cold load the header and tabs never disappear and come back (MA-25a). After a save, delete, completion or snooze the page stays on screen (so reminder actions stay in place) and the change arrives through the listeners.

**The household's today (MA-1, MA-11f).** `today` is the household's calendar day (`useHouseholdToday(householdDayZone(household.timeZone))` from `src/lib/household-day.ts`; America/Chicago when the household has no valid zone), kept in state and recomputed by a timer to the next household midnight (capped at an hour, so a throttled timer or a clock change cannot strand it) and on `visibilitychange` to visible, `pageshow`, window `focus` and `online` (a tablet or laptop waking from sleep). The next midnight is computed from the household zone's calendar (`msUntilHouseholdMidnight`), so a 23- or 25-hour DST day is neither skipped nor doubled. Reminders, home health, warranty rings, Mark done's and Confirm mileage's completed date, the completion dialog's and a new log's default date, and the Overview's 30-day counts all use it. "Logged, Last 30 Days" and "Spent, Last 30 Days" count logs dated today or in the 29 days before in household days (`recentLogSummary` in `src/lib/maintenance/recent.ts`; a legacy log date stored as an instant is read in the household zone; a log dated after today still counts, as before; one without a readable date does not).

**Rendering (MA-25b).** The page only wires state: each tab panel and the delete confirmations are memoized components, the dialogs own their forms (react-hook-form) and schedule rows, initialised once per opening from the record as it was then (that is also the edit's `base`, so a snapshot arriving while a form is open never resets it), and the page passes stable handlers (`useStableHandler`) and memoized derived lists and props (reminder groups, selected record logs, health, 30-day counts). Typing in a form re-renders only its dialog; nothing queries Firestore per keystroke.

### Reminder actions

Reminder cards show the title, a status badge, the related asset/vehicle and one "when" line (for example "Due Jul 1, 2026 · 4 weeks ago" or "At 49,000 mi · 250 mi to go"); the builder's sentence ("... is overdue") is no longer repeated under the badge. Warranties and vehicle documents read "Expired" / "Expires today" / "Expires soon", never "Overdue".

- **Mark done** (schedules and vehicle services only; needs `maintenance.edit` and `maintenance.createLogs`): the H05 completion check (`CompletionCheck` and `useConfetti`, imported from Chores) at the start of the card. One tap fills the box and draws the check at once (150 ms), fires a small confetti burst from it (once per reminder, skippable by any input, none under reduced motion), plays the `complete` feedback, and completes the schedule today with the next due date advanced by the schedule's interval, through the same server completion as the dialog. The "Marked done" toast offers **Undo** for 10 s (`UNDO_WINDOW_MS`). A vehicle service with a mileage interval (`intervalMiles`) instead opens a small **Confirm mileage** dialog (numeric input prefilled with the vehicle's current mileage, the lower/jump warning) and is completed at the confirmed reading, never silently at the stored odometer (MA-6a); the check is not drawn before. A service with only a set due mileage and no interval opens the completion dialog, where the next due is entered. After an Undo the reminder lists start over, so the reopened card is unchecked with its actions. When the next due values cannot be worked out (no interval) the completion dialog opens instead and the check is not drawn; a failed or refused completion unchecks it. The drawn check is keyed by the reminder's cycle (`reminderCycleKey`: id, due date and due mileage), so when a schedule completed early shows its next cycle under the same reminder id, that card starts unchecked with its actions enabled.
- **Log service** (schedules and vehicle services only; needs `maintenance.createLogs`): opens the Add Maintenance Log form filled in for that asset or vehicle and schedule (target, schedule name as title, routine type, today, the vehicle's current mileage). When the member can also complete schedules (`maintenance.edit`), the dialog says "Saving marks … done …" and saving writes the log with its details (cost, provider, parts, notes) through the same server completion as Mark done (with Undo), advancing the schedule from the log's date and mileage and resolving the reminder. Switching the form to another asset, vehicle or General makes it an ordinary log. Like Mark done, Log service never writes a schedule without a next due: a schedule whose next due cannot be advanced from its interval (an explicit date or mileage with no repeat) opens the completion dialog instead of the log form, and a mileage-interval service saved without a mileage gets an inline "Enter the mileage" error (`serviceCompletionValues` in `schedules.ts`, shared with Mark done).
- **Snooze 1 week** (date-driven schedule and service reminders only; needs `maintenance.edit`): the server sets that schedule's `nextDueDate` to 7 days after the later of today and the current due date and records `snoozedUntil` (the same date), only while the card's cycle is still current, then resolves the old cycle's notifications (best effort). The toast offers **Undo** for 10 s. Vehicle services that are due by mileage, warranties and documents cannot be snoozed, because moving a date would not change them.
- **Open**: selects the asset or vehicle and highlights the schedule. Hovering (fine pointer) or keyboard-focusing it for 400 ms shows a peek card (H26) with the item, reminder and due line.
- Cards enter with the shared stagger (≤ 30 ms apart, ≤ 400 ms total). A card that leaves a list fades out in 220 ms, after the check has drawn (instant under reduced motion).

Compatibility with the hourly `householdUpkeep` job: both actions change the schedule's due date, which changes the reminder's `stateKey`, so the next run creates a notification only if the new state is still actionable. A snooze is quiet: the shared builder (`functions/src/shared/maintenance-reminders.ts`) marks a reminder `snoozed` while the schedule's `snoozedUntil` equals its `nextDueDate` and the status is upcoming or due soon, and `actionableReminders` skips snoozed reminders, so no "due soon" push follows a snooze. On the due day the reminder becomes "due today" and notifies as usual. Completing the schedule removes `snoozedUntil` explicitly (`completedAssetSchedule` / `completedVehicleSchedule`), even when the new cycle lands on the snoozed date. Editing the due date changes `nextDueDate`, so a stale `snoozedUntil` never applies. Other edits to the asset or vehicle keep it: the edit form never sends an unchanged field, and schedules merge by id. A vehicle service that becomes due by mileage still notifies. The resolved old document is never refreshed because upkeep skips documents with `resolvedAt`. A snoozed item still appears under Due Soon in the Maintenance Center, with its new date. Tests: `tests/server/reminders-shared.test.ts` (snoozed maintenance reminders).

### Home health score

`computeHomeHealth` (`src/lib/maintenance/health.ts`) uses the same reminder builder as the Reminders tab and the server, evaluated in the household timezone, over active assets and vehicles (retired assets and retired/sold vehicles are ignored; with none left there is no score and Overview shows the assets empty state).

Deductions per reminder:

| Reminder | Points |
|---|---|
| Schedule/service overdue | 10, plus 2 per full 30 days late or per full 500 miles over (whichever is larger), at most 20 |
| Schedule/service due today | 5 |
| Schedule/service due soon (14 days / 500 miles) | 2 |
| Registration/inspection expired | 15 |
| Registration/inspection expires today | 5 |
| Registration/inspection expires soon | 2 |
| Warranty ends today or soon | 2 (an already expired warranty costs nothing) |
| Asset or vehicle flagged "Needs attention" | 10 |

Bonus: each log linked to the item and dated in the last 90 days adds 2, at most 6 per item.

- Item score = 100 - its deductions + its bonus, clamped to 0-100.
- Home score = 100 - every deduction + the items' bonuses (at most 10 in total), clamped to 0-100.
- Bands: 85 and up "In good shape" (success color), 60-84 "Needs some care" (warning), below 60 "Needs attention" (destructive).

The Overview dial uses `NumberTicker` and a ring that sweeps to the value in 550 ms (value only, never blocking); under reduced motion both show the final value at once. Every ring is `role="img"` with a full text equivalent ("Home health 79 out of 100, needs some care."). The "Why this score" list shows the five largest deductions ("−12 Main HVAC System: Replace air filter is 3 months overdue"), with "Show N more", and each row opens its item.

## Data Model and Persistence

- Assets: `households/{householdId}/home-assets/{assetId}`.
- Vehicles: `households/{householdId}/vehicles/{vehicleId}`.
- Logs: `households/{householdId}/maintenance/{logId}`.
- Attachment metadata: `households/{householdId}/maintenance-attachments/{attachmentId}`.
- Op ledger: `households/{householdId}/maintenance-ops/{opId}` (server only; see "Server operations"). `uploadedByUid` and `uploadedByName` record the uploader; the name is the profile's display name through `publicDisplayName` ("Member" when it is missing or an email), never the email. `scripts/ops/scrub-remaining-emails.mjs` rewrites older names that are an email (see [Household Governance](../household-governance/README.md), "Email copies").
- Reminder notifications: `households/{householdId}/notifications/{notificationId}`.
- Attachment objects: `households/{householdId}/maintenance/{targetType}/{targetId}/{attachmentId}-{sanitizedFileName}`.

Shared types include `HomeAsset`, `HomeAssetSchedule`, `MaintenanceScheduleMode`, `MaintenanceLastCompletion`, `Vehicle`, `VehicleServiceSchedule`, `MaintenanceLog`, and `MaintenanceAttachment`. Schedules carry a stable `id` (assigned on first server touch or by the backfill; see "Schedule identity") and `mode` of `scheduled` or `checklist`; the server stores `lastCompletion: { opId, byUid, byName, completedDate, at, cycle }` on a completed schedule. Vehicles gain `mileageUpdatedAt` (household day of the reading that set `currentMileage`). Logs written by the server add `doneByUid`, `doneByName`, `createdAtServer` (Timestamp) and `opId`, and a completion's log adds `completion` (see "Server operations"); older logs have none of these. Legacy name-only schedules normalize to checklist presentation without a destructive migration. Legacy logs fall back from missing `title` to `item`, infer target type from IDs, and default missing log type to `other`.

When an asset or vehicle is deleted, linked historical logs are preserved as general maintenance records. The original relationship is retained in optional `formerTargetType` and `formerTargetName` fields while the live `assetId` or `vehicleId` is removed.

Deep links use `/maintenance?asset={id}`, `?vehicle={id}`, `?log={id}`, or `?tab={overview|assets|vehicles|reminders|logs}`. Choosing a tab replaces the URL with `?tab=` (Overview has no parameter), so refresh and Back keep the tab. A deep link is applied once per URL after every listener has answered once (`deepLinkPlan` in `src/lib/maintenance/deep-link.ts`), so a collection arriving late never yields a false "no longer available"; later changes (Mark done, another device's edit) do not pull the page back to the linked record. Schedule reminders add a URL-encoded `schedule={ownerId}:{scheduleId}`. Valid links select the matching tab/detail and highlight schedules/logs; missing IDs select the relevant list tab and show a non-destructive status message. Legacy `targetType`/`targetId` links remain accepted.

## Authentication, Roles, and Security

Clients derive household paths from the authenticated user. Pending `newuser` members are denied and legacy members remain supported.

Attachment metadata creation validates matching `householdId`, target/category values, path prefix, `uploadedByUid == request.auth.uid`, and that the target exists (`maintenance_log` → `maintenance/{id}`, `home_asset` → `home-assets/{id}`, `vehicle` → `vehicles/{id}`). Storage allows approved members, limits files to under 15 MiB, and accepts images, PDF, plain text, Word, and OpenXML documents.

Firestore rules enforce the granular permissions (with per-member overrides): reads need `maintenance.view`; asset/vehicle create and update and attachment metadata create need `maintenance.edit`; log create needs `maintenance.createLogs` and log update `maintenance.edit`; deletes need `maintenance.delete`; resolving a maintenance notification needs `maintenance.edit` and may only change `resolvedAt`/`resolvedBy` (the page's record deletion still uses it; completion and snooze resolve on the server). The page hides controls a member cannot use (see capabilities); the rules remain the authority for client writes. Completion, snooze and log/asset/vehicle saves are server actions that check the same permissions (see "Server operations"); the client write rules stay for record deletion and older clients. `maintenance-ops` is denied to every client (`allow read, write: if false`).

Shape validation (QA-8): logs, home assets and vehicles may only set the fields of `MaintenanceLog`, `HomeAsset` and `Vehicle` in `src/lib/types.ts` (logs include the legacy `item`, `summary`, `receiptUrl`, `formerTargetType` and `formerTargetName`). A `householdId` field, when present, must equal the household in the path, on create and update. Enums are checked (`targetType`, log `type`, asset and vehicle `status`, `formerTargetType`), numbers must be numbers (no range: the forms accept any finite value), short text is bounded at 1000 characters and notes, parts and summaries at 20000; `schedules` and `serviceSchedules` must be lists of at most 100 entries (their entries are not inspected). On update only added or changed keys are checked, so a record with legacy fields stays updatable. The server-owned fields (`doneByUid`, `doneByName`, `opId`, `completion`, `createdAtServer` on logs, `mileageUpdatedAt` on vehicles) are not in the client allowlists, so a client can neither forge nor change them. Schedule entries remain an unvalidated list in the rules (`lastCompletion` inside one could be written by a client with `maintenance.edit`); the server treats it as data and never trusts it for authorization.

## Integrations and Background Processing

- AI summaries use the authenticated `summarizeMaintenanceLog` OpenAI action; generated summaries remain client state and original notes are unchanged.
- The homepage overview uses `maintenance.view` to calculate status, schedule/checklist, date, mileage, warranty, registration, and inspection signals server-side. Only bounded labels/dates/counts reach the optional narrative; serial numbers, VINs, plates, costs, notes, and attachment paths are excluded.
- Date due-soon threshold: 14 days.
- Mileage due-soon threshold: 500 miles.
- The hourly `householdUpkeep` Cloud Function (`functions/src/index.ts` → `functions/src/reminders.ts`) runs the same idempotent step for every household; the Chores page also runs it on demand through `syncHouseholdUpkeep` (`src/app/chores/actions.ts`, authorized by `chores.view`) so a new recurring assignment appears immediately. A notification for something still overdue is re-surfaced within two days of expiry rather than allowed to disappear (so the Firestore TTL policy on `expiresAt` never deletes a still-actionable reminder): its `expiresAt` is extended and its `createdAt` moves to the refresh time, so the 7-day notification lists include it again, while an earlier dismissal still stands. A resolved one is reopened only if upkeep still finds it actionable with the same state (for example the completion never reached the schedule) and it was resolved before that run read its data; it sends no new push. Upkeep writes nothing for a household being deleted.
- The same upkeep run resolves open maintenance reminder notifications (all five maintenance source types, with a string `stateKey`) whose `sourceType`/`sourceId`/`stateKey` is no longer in the current actionable set: a completed, snoozed, edited or removed schedule, or a retired or deleted record. Each is re-checked in a transaction that re-reads the asset or vehicle collection, like the medication pass. This is the durable retry of the server's post-commit cleanup. Tests: `tests/server/household-upkeep.test.ts`.
- New notification documents can trigger FCM through the shared Cloud Function.
- Physical notification expiration requires Firestore TTL on `expiresAt`.

## Cross-Module Dependencies

- [Notifications](../notifications/README.md) displays, dismisses, resolves, and pushes maintenance notices.
- [AI and OpenAI](../ai-genkit/README.md) owns OpenAI configuration, authorization, and deterministic test behavior.
- [Identity and Profile](../identity-profile/README.md) and [Household Governance](../household-governance/README.md) supply user and household context.

The command palette logs maintenance directly after the member confirms a preview card ("oil change 48,200 miles", or the same read by its AI fallback), gated by `maintenance.createLogs`, the permission the rules require to create a log. The log is a `maintenance` document shaped like this module's "Add log" form (`quickMaintenanceLog` in `src/lib/dashboard/quick-add-writes.ts`, written by `logMaintenance` in `src/components/command/quick-add-actions.ts`): an id that is always new (a readable slug plus a Firestore auto-id, `quickCreateId`, so a queued offline write can never overwrite a log), `targetType` vehicle, home_asset or general with its `vehicleId`/`assetId`, `title` and legacy `item`, today's date, `type: 'routine'`, `createdAt`/`updatedAt`, and the odometer reading as `mileage` on vehicle logs only (without a vehicle it goes into `notes` so it is not lost). The preview names the vehicle (nickname, make or model in the sentence; the only active vehicle is preselected), and with a reading but several vehicles none named, Confirm waits until one is chosen; Confirm also waits until the palette has loaded the household's vehicles and assets, so a reading is never saved as a general log just because the vehicles were not known yet; if the vehicles or assets could not be read, Confirm stays disabled with a note and a Try again button; a reading below the vehicle's recorded `currentMileage` gets a warning. It does not change the vehicle's `currentMileage`, schedules or reminders (the page's Add log form, which goes through the server, raises `currentMileage` by the max rule). Offline the write is queued (`awaitWriteUnlessOffline`, "Saved offline"), a rejected write is reported with `toastError` and the preview stays open. Afterwards the palette dispatches `homehub:data-changed` with `module: 'maintenance'`; this page does not need it: its live listeners show a log added from the palette (or another device) while the page is open.

## Invariants and Failure Behavior

- Invalid/missing dates are ignored; mileage reminders require current and next-due mileage.
- Scheduled entries require a date/frequency or mileage due mechanism appropriate to their owner. Explicit checklist entries may contain only a name and never show a Complete action.
- Notification IDs hash stable source type, persisted owner/schedule identity, and a `stateKey` containing due status plus meaningful due date/mileage. Transactions make overlapping synchronization idempotent.
- Read/dismiss history remains on an existing reminder document. A changed due status/date/mileage produces a new identity; completing a schedule resolves the current cycle and advances the embedded schedule.
- Asset and vehicle photos are plain `<img>` elements on the attachment's `downloadUrl` (`alt=""`, the name is printed next to them) inside a fixed 80 px band, so loading never shifts layout; an image that fails to load falls back to the category art. A brand logo reserves its box while the artwork chunk loads, so a badge never flashes before a logo. Only image content types (PNG, JPEG, WebP, GIF, AVIF) are used, preferring the `photo` category; SVG and PDFs are ignored.
- Field problems are shown inline: schedule rows use `FieldError` (focus moves to the first bad schedule name), and the log dialog's missing asset/vehicle uses the form field's message. Failures use `toastError`/`friendlyError`, except AI summary errors, whose messages are already user-safe from the AI module.
- "Summarize with AI" is offered only for notes with at least two statements (split on `. ! ? ;` or line breaks, each two words or more) or at least 40 words.
- Overview labels: "Logged, Last 30 Days" counts logs dated in the last 30 household days (today and the 29 before); the "Latest Logs" list shows the five newest entries regardless of date.
- Home Assets layout: the asset card grid uses `content-start`, so cards keep their content height instead of stretching to the selected asset's detail panel, and the page never scrolls sideways (regression-tested at 1009 px in `tests/e2e/navigation-layout.spec.ts`). The attachment Upload control is a label around a plain visually hidden `<input type="file">` with `relative overflow-hidden`; the shared `Input` component is not used there because its `w-full` overrides `sr-only` and widened the page.
- Asset and vehicle deletion require confirmation and are blocked while direct attachments exist. Embedded schedules/checklists are deleted with the owning registry document.
- Maintenance summary requests require verified household membership plus `maintenance.view`; provider failures are sanitized and non-destructive.
- Registry deletion reads current logs and the record's audience-constrained reminder notifications immediately before committing one batch. Linked logs become general history, and active schedule/warranty/registration/inspection notifications for the deleted record are resolved. Unrelated records are unchanged.
- Log, attachment, and embedded schedule/checklist removal use target-specific confirmation dialogs; attachment Storage cleanup remains best effort under the existing metadata workflow.
- Storage is deleted before metadata; a missing object is tolerated. On upload, if the metadata write is refused (for example the record or a completion's log was deleted meanwhile, which the rules check), the just-uploaded object is deleted again (best effort).
- Loading uses four live listeners; the page waits for all four before its first render of data. A listener failing before that shows the error card (Try again resubscribes); after it, records stay and the listener retries. A refused listener clears every collection and shows the "not shared with you" state.
- A completion writes its log, the schedule, the vehicle's mileage and the ledger in one transaction; a cycle is completed at most once (identified by its due values, so a cycle recreated with the same values by an edit is a new one). Next due is never lost: a completion that would leave no next due is refused.
- New asset, vehicle and log ids are `{slug}-{part of the op id}` and cannot collide; the command palette's quick log keeps its own `quickCreateId`.
- Notification cleanup after a completion or snooze is best effort (`cleanup: 'pending'`); upkeep finishes it within the hour.
- Deleted asset/vehicle deep links remain on the relevant list tab and report that the requested record is unavailable. New schedule notification source IDs use persisted schedule IDs; legacy schedules receive a deterministic compatibility key.
