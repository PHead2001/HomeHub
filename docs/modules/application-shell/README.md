# Application Shell and Dashboard

## Purpose and Scope

This module owns the root layout, global providers and overlays, primary navigation, dashboard, the shared visual shell and design foundation (color tokens and theme presets, surfaces, focus ring, motion system, route loading skeletons, ambient background, sound/haptic feedback API), and global error/toast placement. It does not own feature data or feature-specific authorization rules.

## User-Facing Capabilities

- Signed out, `/` is a landing page: a sample "living house" that assembles as you scroll while each module's tile flies into its room, what HomeHub does, and one "Sign in with Google" button at the end; the header shows no Login button on this page, so that is the only call to action (HP-29). No module navigation and no data reads.
- Signed in, `/` is the dashboard: one subtitle line with the household name, the household's date and time and the current weather (when the member turned it on), the living house hero (rooms with overdue, due and finished chores; pets fed or waiting by the bowl; fridge stock and expiring food; HVAC and car service), a one-line morning brief, and a bento grid of live tiles (today's chores progress ring, shopping with the first three items, pets' fed status, next maintenance countdown, food expiring soon, unread notifications, household streaks, and the weather when the member turned live weather on). Clicking a room opens Chores filtered to that room. An optional, per-user live weather switch in the house's "…" menu draws rain, snow or a sun glint over the house and the shell. Everything comes from exact household data the member can view and is shown by default; nothing needs a "Generate" click. On every wide landscape screen (1024 px and wider, landscape; personal accounts included, no device setting) the dashboard is one screen: the house on the left, the brief and tiles on the right.
- Hover or keyboard-focus a room, a tile, a linked phrase in the morning brief or a notification for 400 ms to see a peek card with its details (fine, hovering pointers only). Without one (touch, pen) the same details are inline: each room lists its top chores, and the tiles list next up, the shopping lists, the later maintenance items and the streak names (HP-13). `PeekLink` makes any link peekable.
- Time-of-day lighting tints the shell and the house (dawn, day, dusk, night; the attic window lights up in the evening). Seasonal touches (snow, a pumpkin, a lit tree) can be switched off in the house's "…" menu. Easter eggs: the Konami code, or five taps on the roof within 2 s, starts a 10 s disco on the house, long-pressing a pet makes it hop, and the roof turns gold after 100 completed chores.
- Open Home Wrapped from the dashboard: a swipeable story of the household's year (chores done, chore champion, most-bought item, pet meals logged, upkeep spend) that can be shared as an image with first names only.
- Ask AI to rewrite the morning brief with the small sparkle button next to it (H04). The rewrite comes from the overview flow's exact, permission-filtered facts, shows with an "AI-written" marker, HomeHub's exact counts and its suggested priorities, and "Show plain brief" goes back. The deterministic brief is always the default; there is no separate AI Overview section or "Generate Overview" button any more.
- Press Ctrl/Cmd+K (or "/", or the header search button) for the command palette: search pages, chores, lists, items, pets and maintenance records, or type a sentence ("milk and eggs to groceries", "fed Maple", "vacuum Friday for Sam", "trash every Tuesday for Sam", "oil change 48,200 miles") to quick-add with a preview and a Confirm step: it adds items, logs feedings, creates one-time and repeating chores (assignee and room) and logs maintenance (odometer reading, for the chosen vehicle). A sentence the parser cannot read can be read by AI on request ("Read … with AI"), with the same preview and Confirm. A mic button adds by voice where the browser supports speech recognition.
- Keyboard shortcuts: `N` new chore, `A` add shopping item, `F` log feeding, `G` then `H`/`C`/`S`/`P`/`M`/`K`/`N` to jump to Home, Chores, Shopping, Pets, Maintenance, Calendar or Notifications, and `?` for the cheat sheet. They never fire while typing. Tooltips on the header search, the header links and the dashboard's quick-add buttons (Add a chore `N`, Add a shopping item `A`, Log a feeding `F`) show the keys from the same map.
- Navigate through permission-filtered desktop header links and avatar actions. The current page's header link is shown in the foreground color with an accent underline that slides between links.
- On phones, navigate with a fixed bottom tab bar (Home, Chores, a raised quick-add button, Shopping, Pets, More) and a More sheet (Maintenance, Calendar, Automation, Household, Barcode Library, Profile). The quick-add button fans out Chore, Item, Feeding and Maintenance; long-press opens voice add.
- The desktop header lists Calendar next to Chores (gated by `chores.view`, like Chores).
- Open profile, household management, barcode library, notifications, and logout controls.
- Receive create/join-household and pending-approval overlays.
- See the chosen theme (Dark, Light or System mode with curated presets) from the first paint, without a flash of the default theme.
- Route changes crossfade page content with an 8 px settle through the View Transitions API (a CSS fade where it is missing), and shared elements morph between pages; the header and tab bar stay put. Tab switches fade the new panel in (opacity only), and lists and tiles enter staggered; every route shows a layout-matched skeleton while it loads, and navigations slower than 150 ms show a thin top progress bar.
- With Reduce motion (system setting or the in-app switch), every transition renders its end state instantly.
- See the HomeHub brand mark in the header, browser tab, and installed app icon; the header mark plays a one-time intro on the first load of a browser session.
- Install HomeHub to a phone or desktop home screen (standalone window, splash from the manifest colors and icon), with long-press shortcuts for "Add to shopping", "Log feeding" and "My chores".
- Share links or text into the installed app (handled by [Shopping Lists](../shopping/README.md)).
- Open the dashboard and shopping lists offline; other routes show a friendly offline page.
- While the browser is offline, a slim pill under the header says "You're offline. Changes you can make offline will sync."; when the connection returns it says "Back online" for 3 seconds.
- When a page crashes, the header and tab bar stay and the page shows "Something went wrong" with **Try again** and **Go home**. If the whole app fails, a minimal HomeHub page offers **Reload**.
- After a new version is deployed, an open tab shows a "HomeHub updated" toast with **Reload**. Nothing reloads on its own.
- Up to three toasts show at once, and Undo toasts stay for the shared 10-second Undo window.
- Signed-in-only pages show their loading skeleton, not the sign-in prompt, until the session is known, and the header shows no Login button before then.

## Architecture and Data Flow

The root layout renders the color tokens for every theme preset and an inline theme script in `<head>`, the ambient background, then installs `AuthProvider`, `AppearanceProvider` (theme, motion preference and feedback settings, wrapping everything in motion's `MotionConfig`), the navigation progress bar, the glow pointer tracker, the cross-route morph listener, the service worker and foreground push (`DeferredPushNotificationManager`, after `load` and idle, loads `PushNotificationManager`, which registers the service worker for every visitor and imports no Firebase, and, only once a member is signed in, `PushForegroundListener`, which imports `@/lib/firebase` and Messaging and shows foreground pushes as toasts; `tests/e2e/dashboard-house.spec.ts` checks that a signed-out visitor downloads neither Firestore nor App Check, even after load and idle; `DeferredToaster` mounts the toast viewport the same way, and toasts raised earlier wait in the toast store), Firebase error reporting, password-change handling, household-state overlays, the header, and global toasts around every route. `src/app/template.tsx` wraps page content in `PageTransition`; because templates remount on navigation and layouts do not, only page content animates. The dashboard (see "Dashboard" below) reads live household facts in the browser and keeps the on-demand `HomeAiOverview`; it makes no overview request on page load and does not persist results. The module carousel (and its arrow that overlapped the first card's title) was replaced by the living house and the tile grid. `CommandCenter` (palette, shortcuts, help) is mounted once after the tab bar and loads `CommandPalette` as its own chunk only for members who can use it; the header loads `NotificationBell` and the motion-driven nav underline the same way (a same-size placeholder first). Signed-out pages therefore load only the app, Auth and the shell, with no Firestore, Storage, Messaging, App Check or animation library before first paint (see [Firebase Platform](../firebase-platform/README.md), "Split initialization"), and `TimeOfDayTint` sits behind the shell next to the ambient background.

Below the `md` breakpoint, `MobileTabBar` renders a fixed bottom navigation bar inside `HouseholdManager`, after `<main>`; at `md` and wider it is hidden. Primary tabs are Home, Chores, Shopping, and Pets; a More tab opens a bottom sheet with Maintenance, Calendar, Automation, Household, Barcode Library, and Profile. A raised "Quick add" button sits in the middle of the bar (`aria-haspopup="menu"`, `aria-expanded`). Tapping it fans out a menu (`role="menu"`, named "Quick add") of the actions the member may use: Add chore (`chores.create`), Add shopping item (`shopping.edit`), Log feeding (`pets.addLogs`) and Log maintenance (`maintenance.createLogs`, opens `/maintenance`). The first three open the command palette pre-filled ("chore ", "add ", "fed "). Escape or a tap outside closes the fan; a 500 ms long-press opens the palette and starts voice input. The active tab icon fills and bounces once. The active tab or sheet item is marked with `aria-current="page"`, and More is highlighted when the current route is one of its items. The bar is not rendered for users without a household, without a loaded member record, or with role `newuser`.

The root `viewport` export sets `viewportFit: 'cover'`. The tab bar pads its bottom by `env(safe-area-inset-bottom)` and the More sheet adds the same inset to its bottom padding, so content clears the home indicator. On phones `<main>` reserves `calc(4rem + env(safe-area-inset-bottom))` of bottom padding (the 4rem bar height plus the inset) so page content is not hidden behind the bar; `md:pb-0` removes it on larger screens. This padding applies whenever `<main>` renders on a phone, including when the bar itself is hidden.

### Brand mark

`Logo` renders the HomeHub mark as inline SVG: a house whose roof is two connection edges meeting at a hub node, with smaller nodes at the eaves, plus two windows. It uses `currentColor`, defaulting to `hsl(var(--primary))`, and fixed `width`/`height` from its `size` prop so it never shifts layout. `variant="wordmark"` adds the HomeHub name; the mark is decorative (`aria-hidden`) unless a `title` is passed. The header renders `<Logo size={24} animated />` next to its own HomeHub text.

With `animated`, CSS keyframes in `logo.module.css` play once: the roof draws on with `stroke-dashoffset` (0-560 ms), the nodes pop in (380-680 ms), and the windows light up (620-900 ms), 900 ms in total. The base styles are the finished mark, so skipping the animation always shows the end state. An inline script rendered right after the SVG records `homehub:logo-intro-played` in `sessionStorage` and, on later loads in the same tab session, marks the SVG `data-intro-played` before it paints, so the intro never replays. The animation is also disabled by `@media (prefers-reduced-motion: reduce)` and by `html[data-motion="reduce"]` (the in-app motion setting contract). Nothing waits on the animation.

All static brand assets come from the same artwork constants in `logo-artwork.ts`. `scripts/brand/render-icons.mjs` rasterizes them with `sharp` into `src/app/icon.svg` and `src/app/favicon.ico` (16/32/48 px, rounded navy tile), `src/app/apple-icon.png` (180 px, full bleed), `public/icons/icon-192.png` and `icon-512.png` (purpose `any`), `public/icons/maskable-512.png` (full bleed, mark inside the 80% safe zone) and `public/icons/shortcut-96.png`. `public/icons/badge-96.png` (the push notification badge: the house glyph alone in white on transparency, used by Android as a monochrome mask) is not produced by that script; it was derived from `icon-192.png` by keeping the accent-colored pixels, so regenerate it the same way if the mark changes (see [Notifications](../notifications/README.md)). Raster icons use the static hex values `BRAND_BACKGROUND_HEX` (`#020817`, the dark `--background`) and `BRAND_ACCENT_HEX` (`#4bb9ae`, the current `--primary`); regenerate and commit them if either token changes. Next's file conventions emit the favicon, SVG icon and apple-touch-icon links.

### Installable app (PWA)

`src/app/manifest.ts` is served at `/manifest.webmanifest` (Next adds the link tag): `id`/`start_url`/`scope` `/`, `display: standalone`, `background_color` and `theme_color` `#020817` (matching the dark default and the `viewport.themeColor`, so the splash blends into the first frame), the icons above, three shortcuts (`/shopping?quickAdd=1`, `/pets?feed=1` (opens the Pets feeding picker, see [Pets](../pets/README.md)), `/chores`), and a GET `share_target` to `/share` with `title`, `text` and `url`. The root `metadata` adds `applicationName` and `appleWebApp` (capable, title, `black-translucent` status bar) for iOS home-screen launches.

### Service worker contract

HomeHub has exactly one service worker, `/api/sw` with scope `/` (`src/lib/service-worker.ts` holds the URL and scope). It combines Firebase Messaging push handling (see [Notifications](../notifications/README.md)) with the offline app shell. Production builds register it from `PushNotificationManager` after the window `load` event; development builds and the Playwright suite (which runs the dev server) do not auto-register, so cached pages never mask code changes or intercept test traffic, though enabling push still registers it.

- Install precaches the page shells for `/`, `/shopping` and `/offline`, every `/_next/static/` asset those pages reference, and the manifest and icons, then calls `skipWaiting`. Activate deletes `homehub-*` caches from other versions and claims open pages. The cache names carry `SHELL_VERSION` in `route.ts`; bump it when the caching logic or precache list changes.
- Navigations are network first. A successful same-origin response for a shell route refreshes its cached copy. If the network fails, the cached shell for that path is returned (query strings are ignored, so `/shopping?quickAdd=1` works offline), otherwise the cached `/offline` page, otherwise a small inline offline page.
- `/_next/static/` assets are cache first (they are content-hashed); the unhashed manifest and icons are served from cache and refreshed in the background. The static cache is trimmed to 300 entries.
- The worker ignores non-GET requests, other origins (Firebase, Google APIs, CDNs), `/api/*` and `/__/*`. React Server Component fetches go to the network; when they fail offline, Next falls back to a full navigation, which the worker serves.
- Cached shells are client-rendered and contain no household data. Offline data comes from Firestore's persistent cache ([Firebase Platform](../firebase-platform/README.md)).

- Build id: `next.config.ts` stamps every build with `NEXT_PUBLIC_HOMEHUB_BUILD_ID` (a fixed-width UTC timestamp such as `20261109T153012345Z`, computed once per `next build` and inlined through `env` into both the client bundle and this route). The worker embeds it, so every deploy ships a byte-different worker that browsers install on their next update check (which also re-precaches the shells). Activate posts `{ type: 'homehub:build', buildId }` to every window client, and a `{ type: 'homehub:build-query' }` message gets the same reply.

`/offline` is a static page with links to Shopping and Home.

### Error recovery, updates and offline status

- Busy state (`src/lib/shell/busy.ts`): `markBusy(reason)` returns a release function, `isShellBusy()` answers, `whileBusy(reason, work)` holds a mark until a save settles, and `useUnsavedInput(dirty)` holds one while a form has unsaved input (wired into Profile's form: an edited name, a chosen or removed photo, or a save in progress). The toast store holds an `undo` mark while any open toast has an action (`holdsAction`, true by default for a toast with an action; the update toast passes false). The shell checks it before any automatic recovery and delays the update prompt while busy.
- Route errors (`src/app/error.tsx`, Next 16 props `error: unknown` and `retry`): only the page is replaced, so the header and tab bar stay. Plain copy ("This page ran into a problem. Anything you already saved is safe."), **Try again** (`retry()`) and **Go home**, each at least 44 px tall. The error is logged with `console.error`. No per-section boundaries: the root one already keeps the shell.
- Root errors (`src/app/global-error.tsx`): it replaces the root layout, so it has its own `<html>`/`<body>`/`<title>`, inline styles in the static brand colors (dark background, accent button, the mark drawn from `logo-artwork.ts`), no providers, globals.css or fonts, and a **Reload** button.
- Crash-loop guard (`src/lib/shell/crash-guard.ts`, `useCrashRecovery`): every crash is recorded in `sessionStorage["homehub.shell.crashes"]` (timestamps inside the last 10 minutes). The only automatic action is one reload for a stale-build chunk failure (`ChunkLoadError`, "Loading chunk ... failed", "Failed to fetch dynamically imported module"), and only when the shell is not busy, the browser is online and the history could be stored. The second crash within 10 minutes never reloads: the page says "This page keeps running into a problem (last at 3:42 PM)" and stays manual. Every other error is manual from the start. No global `error`/`unhandledrejection` reporting is installed.
- Updates (`UpdateWatcher` in `src/components/shell/update-watcher.tsx`, mounted by `DeferredPushNotificationManager` after load and idle, production only): calls `registration.update()` every 45 minutes and when the tab becomes visible again (at most every 5 minutes), asks the controlling worker for its build id and listens for activation messages. `decideUpdatePrompt` (`src/lib/shell/update-check.ts`) offers the toast only when the worker's build is strictly newer than the page's, once per build per tab (`sessionStorage["homehub.shell.update-prompted"]`, memory when storage is blocked), and waits while the shell is busy. The toast ("HomeHub updated", "Reload to use the latest version.", 30 s, **Reload**) reloads only on the member's tap. There is no automatic reload, including idle kiosk reload (deferred).
- Minimum build (member privacy release C): `MINIMUM_SUPPORTED_BUILD_ID` in `src/lib/shell/update-check.ts` (`20261110T203402000Z`, the release A merge time, so every build stamped before it predates release A) is published by the server through the worker: `/api/sw` embeds `minimumBuildId(process.env.HOMEHUB_MINIMUM_BUILD_ID)` (an override can only raise the floor) and sends it as `minimumBuildId` with every `homehub:build` message (on activation and in reply to `homehub:build-query`). `UpdateWatcher` reads it with `buildInfoFromMessage`; when the page's own build is older, `decideRequiredReload` decides: it shows "HomeHub needs to reload" ("This version is out of date. HomeHub will reload as soon as you finish what you're doing.", no timeout, **Reload**) and reloads by itself once the shell is not busy (a save, an Undo toast, unsaved input) and the browser is online, waiting otherwise. It reloads at most once per page build within 10 minutes (`sessionStorage["homehub.shell.forced-reload"]` = `{ buildId, at }`); still too old after that, it only asks ("Reload to keep using HomeHub."). Firestore's persistent cache keeps writes queued before the reload. Only builds that contain this check (release C on) honour it; older pages ignore the field and keep their "HomeHub updated" prompt, which is why chore readers keep their email fallback (see [Chores](../chores/README.md)). Raise the constant in the release that needs every open tab on a newer build. Without a registered worker (development, or a browser without service workers) there is no check.
- Offline status (`ConnectionStatus` in the header): `navigator.onLine` plus the `online`/`offline` events. The pill floats just below the sticky header (absolutely positioned, `pointer-events: none`, not interactive), so it never shifts the layout. Its `role="status"` `aria-live="polite"` region exists only while the pill shows (offline or "Back online"), so an online page has no extra status region; it mounts empty and gets its text a frame later so the change is announced, and going back online swaps the text in the same region (one store holds both states). "Back online" appears only after a real offline-to-online change, never on load. Firestore's pending-writes state is not read (it would load Firestore on first paint).
- Toasts (`src/hooks/use-toast.ts`, `src/lib/shell/toast-queue.ts`): `TOAST_LIMIT` is 3, newest first. When a fourth arrives, the toast dropped is the oldest closed one, then the oldest without a held action, and only when all three hold one, the oldest Undo. A dismissed toast leaves the store 1 s after closing. Undo toasts (shopping tick and remove, notification dismiss, chore complete and calendar move, pet feeding) all use `UNDO_WINDOW_MS` (10 s, `src/lib/undo-window.ts`). Each Undo toast has a `group` (`notification-dismiss`, `shopping-tick`, `shopping-remove`, `chore-complete`, `chore-move`, `pet-feed`): a new toast of the same group removes the previous one from the store at once (no exit animation), so only one Undo of each kind is ever on screen; Undos of different kinds can show together. For notification dismissals this keeps one shared Undo that restores the whole batch.
- Session pending (SL-11, `useSessionKnown`): false only until Auth first answers on the page (`loading` false or a user present), then true for the page's life, so a later sign-in popup does not swap the page for a skeleton. Automation, Calendar, Chores, Barcode Library, Maintenance, Pets, Profile and Shopping render their own `loading.tsx` skeleton until then; Household wraps its page in `SessionGate`. The header holds the Login button's space with an empty placeholder. Signed-out visitors see the sign-in prompt and Login as soon as Auth answers.

### Navigation

The desktop header (`<nav aria-label="Main">`) marks the current link with `aria-current="page"`, the foreground color and an accent underline (a motion `layoutId="header-nav-underline"` element) that slides to the new link on navigation. Links fill the header's 56 px height (the underline sits on its bottom edge), so each is a 56 px tall target that looks like before; their focus ring is inset (`-outline-offset-2`). On touch screens (`coarse:`, `(pointer: coarse)`) the link text is 16 px (SL-4). The header never scrolls sideways (SL-7): `HeaderNav` measures an invisible copy of every label (re-measured by a `ResizeObserver` when the nav or the labels resize: fonts loading, text size changes) and shows as many links as fit, in order, with the rest in a "More" dropdown (`fittingLinkCount` in `src/lib/navigation.ts`); "More" carries the active styling and underline when the current page is one of its links. The logo and the right-hand buttons never shrink, so the avatar stays on screen at 768-1023 px, at 130% text and with touch-sized buttons. At 1440 px with fine pointers and 100% text every link fits, as before; before the first measurement all links render and the nav clips. No side rail is used (D-5). Header and phone tab bar share `isActiveRoute` from `src/lib/navigation.ts`.

Header and phone tab bar navigation derive visibility from effective permissions. The header also has a "Search or quick add" button (opens the palette), the notification bell named "Notifications" (plus the unread count when there is one, which ticks to new values) and the "Account menu" button with the member's shared gradient `MemberAvatar` (photo when there is one; decorative, the button carries the name). The search button and the header links with a `G` shortcut carry `ShortcutHint` tooltips through `LazyShortcutHint` (`src/components/ui/lazy-shortcut-hint.tsx`): the tooltip code loads only once a signed-in header renders them, so the signed-out landing never downloads it before first paint, and until then the control already has its `aria-keyshortcuts`. `ShortcutKeys` lives in its own tooltip-free file (`shortcut-keys.tsx`) for the same reason (the cheat sheet in the first-paint command center uses it).

`tailwind.config.ts` sets `future.hoverOnlyWhenSupported`, so every `hover:` style applies only on devices with a hovering, fine pointer. On phones a tapped button no longer keeps a stuck hover fill (the audit's "Refresh button keeps a filled look after click").

### Dashboard

`src/app/page.tsx` renders, while auth loads, both `Landing` and `DashboardSkeleton` in the server HTML; an inline script picks one before first paint from the device hint `localStorage["homehub.session"]` (`"1"` only, written by `TimeOfDayTint` on every route once auth resolves, removed on sign-out; `src/lib/dashboard/session-hint.ts`). Without the hint the landing hero paints at once instead of waiting for Firebase Auth; a signed-in member on a device without the hint (first visit) sees the landing until auth resolves. It shows `Landing` when signed out (the same element, so nothing remounts), and the dashboard (`src/components/dashboard/dashboard.tsx`, loaded with `next/dynamic` as its own chunk so the landing page never downloads it) otherwise. `useDashboardData` (`src/lib/dashboard/use-dashboard-data.ts`) subscribes read-only, with `onSnapshot`, to the sections the member can view: `chores.view` (rooms, chore templates, completed chores, the household's membership records and the `chore-settings/preferences` leaderboard setting for streaks, open chores, chores with today's `originalDueDate`, and chores completed today found by both completion timestamps: `completedAtServer >=` local midnight as a Timestamp, and the legacy `completedAt >=` its ISO string; single-field range queries, merged by document id, with readiness and failure tracked for each), `shopping.view` (lists, each list's items, pantry), `maintenance.view` (home assets, vehicles, and maintenance logs dated within the last 92 days for the home health score), `pets.view` (pets, and each pet's 12 most recent feedings per date shape) and `notifications.view` (the bell's query, built by `notificationWindowQuery` in `src/app/notifications/notification-query.ts`: the last 7 days, newest 30; for a member who lacks any module view permission it adds `where('category', 'in', ...)` with the categories they may see plus `system` and `general`, so the 30 are counted after that filter). The notification query therefore also depends on `automation.view`. No model is called and the AI quota is not used.

**Context, day and resilience (HP-4, HP-5, GOAL-lastgood).** The store (`dashboard-store.ts`) belongs to one context: household, member (uid and email) and the view permissions (`chores`, `shopping`, `maintenance`, `pets`, `notifications`, `automation`). Any context change stops every listener and starts from an empty store, so the previous household's or member's data never shows, even for a frame; last-good data is kept only within one context. The household day is deliberately not part of the context. Each top-level listener is declared once (`PLAN` in `use-dashboard-data.ts`) as context-scoped or day-scoped; only queries whose filter depends on the day are day-scoped: today's chores (`originalDueDate == today`), today's completions (both timestamps `>=` the household's midnight), the home-health log window and the notification window (the 7 days before the household's midnight, so it moves forward each day). `createListenerSet().sync` restarts a listener only when its key changes (day-scoped keys include the household day and zone), so at household midnight only those three chore queries, the health window and the notifications query resubscribe; rooms, templates, open chores, streaks, shopping, pantry, maintenance and pets keep running. The notification documents are kept in the store (deduplicated as the bell does) and the unread count is computed from them at `now` (`unreadBellCount`: the last 7 days, then the bell's expiry, audience, category, resolved, dismissed and cap rules), so a notice that expires drops out of the count within a minute, with no new snapshot. The store is never reset at midnight: the old day's values stay until the new snapshots replace them (merged by document id as before), so the house, tiles and brief never fall back to skeletons or "Welcome home.". The brief stays mounted once it has been shown (it is keyed by the context), so it is not retyped and an AI rewrite survives midnight; while any section is loading again (a new list whose items have not answered), it keeps speaking from the last complete facts (`briefFacts`), so a missing section is never read as "Nothing needs you". Tiles of a section that is loading again show their skeleton, never an empty state. Every listener, including each list's items and each pet's feedings, goes through `resilientListener` (`resilient-listener.ts`): an error is not final; it subscribes again after 1 s, 2 s, 4 s … capped at 60 s (±20% jitter, reset only by the next server-confirmed answer: a snapshot from the cache never resets the backoff or the permission-denied count, so a refused query that first answers from cache still reaches `unauthorized`), at once on `online` or when the page becomes visible, and stops on unmount. `permission-denied` is retried at most twice, then the listener reports `unauthorized` and stays quiet until the context changes. Listeners use `includeMetadataChanges` so a cached snapshot turning live (or back) is seen.

**Section states (GOAL-states).** Each group of listeners (a section such as `chores`, plus `streaks`, `leaderboard`, `health` and `feed:<petId>`) is exactly one of `loading` (an expected listener has not answered), `ready`, `stale` (data kept while a retry is pending, reason `reconnecting`; or data from Firestore's cache while the browser is offline, reason `offline`), `unauthorized` (the server refused after the retries) or `failed` (an error before any data; retries continue in the background). `sectionStates` decides it with that precedence (unauthorized, failed, loading, stale, ready); `useDashboardData` returns it as `sections`, plus `retry()` (retries every pending listener now), `today`, `timeZone` and `context`. Failed and unauthorized groups are the failure keys passed to `assembleDashboardFacts` (so they are listed in `facts.unavailable`); stale ones keep their facts. Rendering: loading shows the skeletons as before; stale shows the last data with a small hint next to the tile or house title ("Reconnecting…", or "Updated 9:41" offline, in the household zone; `staleHint`); failed shows the dashed "Unavailable right now." tile with a working Retry button (a sibling of the tile link), and the house "Rooms are unavailable right now. Retry · Open Chores"; unauthorized shows "Not shared with you." (house: "Rooms are not shared with you.") with no Retry. A pet whose feeding history (`feed:<petId>`) is stale while the pets collection is fine is marked too (`withFeedingHints` sets `PetState.feedingHint`): the Pets tile's hint covers the displayed pets' feeding groups (`sectionHint`), each stale pet's count reads "0 of 2 today · Reconnecting…", and the house's pet chip carries the hint in its name and title with a dashed outline, so an old count is never taken as current. Missing data is never shown as zero, "all clear" or a celebration.

**Layout (HP-12, HP-15a, HP-20, HP-26).** `dashboard.tsx` renders two blocks in reading order: the subtitle and the house, then the brief, the tiles and the footer (Home Wrapped, keyboard shortcuts). Phones, portrait and narrow screens stack them exactly as before. On `(min-width: 1024px) and (orientation: landscape)` (every account, D-5: no local wall mode and no tablet registration) `.page` in `dashboard.module.css` is a two-column grid, `fit-content(50%)` for the house and the rest for the brief and tiles. The house stage's height is capped at `--house-stage-max-h` (`100dvh`, `100vh` where unsupported, minus the header, page padding, subtitle line and the house card's own rows, all in rem, never below 14rem) and its width is that height times `--house-aspect` (set inline from `layoutHouse`), at most the column. Beside the house the tiles are a 2-column stack with compact padding (min height 7rem, the chores ring 4rem, the "Next:" line hidden; on touch the inline tile details show only the rows the screen height leaves room for: two from 54rem (864 px) tall, one from 50rem (800 px), none below, so 1024x768 keeps them on the module pages), and the "Welcome Home" h1 and the "Your Dashboard" h2 are visually hidden (`.wideSrOnly`; still in the accessibility tree). With the seeded four-room household and up to eight tiles this is meant to fit 1280x800, 1200x800 and 1024x768 at 100% text without vertical scrolling; at 115 or 130% text the rem budgets grow, the house shrinks and the page may scroll, but no pane has a fixed height, so nothing clips or overlaps. No animation was added; the reduced-motion rules are unchanged. The subtitle (`DashboardSubtitle`, `data-testid="dashboard-household-line"`) is the one place the household name is shown (the house's h2 is `sr-only` and only names the section): name, then the household-zone date, time (re-read on each minute boundary and when the page is shown again) and, when live weather has a report, the temperature and condition. It is pane-aware (HP-20c): `.subtitle` in `dashboard.module.css` is an inline-size container, so the formats follow the width the subtitle has (half the page beside the house), not the viewport: the short date and the temperature only below 24rem of subtitle width, the long date from 24rem and the weather condition from 30rem (rem, so the thresholds grow with the text scale). Date, time and weather are whole `nowrap` items, each carrying its leading separator, and the line wraps between them when they still do not fit (large text, a narrow pane), so nothing runs into the brief. Only the name truncates, and only when it alone is wider than the pane. Inline-size containment also means the subtitle never widens the house column.

### Household day

`src/lib/household-day.ts` is the one definition of "today" for shared screens (D-6), meant for reuse by Chores, Shopping and any later module: `householdToday(timeZone, now)` is the `yyyy-MM-dd` day in `household.timeZone`, falling back to the server default (`DEFAULT_TIME_ZONE`, America/Chicago, the same default reminders and recurring chores use) only when the household has none or an invalid one (`householdDayZone`); `householdDayStart(timeZone, day)` is that day's midnight as an instant; `msUntilHouseholdMidnight`; `watchHouseholdDay(timeZone, onDay)` (framework-free, unit tested with fake timers) and the hook `useHouseholdToday(timeZone)`, which keeps the key current with a timer to the next household midnight (capped at an hour) and re-checks on `visibilitychange` to visible, `pageshow` and `online`, so a tablet waking from sleep shows the right day at once. It imports React as a namespace, so server code (server actions) can import the pure functions. Date-only values (`originalDueDate`, `dueDate` keys) stay date-only and are compared as strings with the key. On the dashboard every day-based calculation uses it: the day-scoped queries, chores due today and done today (`computeChoreFacts(..., timeZone)` compares `completionInstant` by its household-zone day), recurring folding, maintenance reminders and home health (also given the resolved zone), the date line under the title, the brief's "typed today" key and the house-rooms snapshot day. `useNow` refreshes every minute and also on `visibilitychange`, `pageshow` and whenever the household day changes (HP-17d). Not yet on the household day: pets' feeding status (`feedingStatus`, the device day; the shared feed work moves it), the pantry's `daysUntilExpiry` (Shopping, SH-10) and streaks (Chores' `computeChoreStats`).

Pure functions in `facts.ts` turn the documents into exact facts by reusing each module's own rules, so the dashboard always agrees with the module's screen:
- Chores: `choreDueKey`, `completionInstant` and day-key math from `src/lib/chores/dates.ts`, in the household day (`householdToday`), and missed instances of a repeating series fold into the latest one with `collapseMissedRecurring` (templates with a valid recurrence), exactly as the Chores To Do list counts them. "Open for you" on the Today tile counts the viewer's chores that are overdue or due today, and a room's "coming up" counts the next 7 days (`UPCOMING_DAYS`). The hourly job generates 30 occurrences of each repeating chore ahead of time, and those are not today's work. A room's up-to-three chores (its peek) are sorted overdue (most overdue first), then today's, then the soonest, undated last, and anything beyond `UPCOMING_DAYS` is left out, so a far-future repeat never pushes out today's work (HP-10). `nextUp` is the household's next five; `mineNext` is the reader's own (overdue, today, then within the week), which the brief's "Your chores" peek lists (HP-11a).
- Maintenance: `buildMaintenanceReminders` (shared with the Maintenance page, the calendar and the hourly reminders) in the household's time zone. Retired assets and retired or sold vehicles are excluded, "due soon" is 14 days or 500 miles, an expired warranty is not a task, and each item links to the module's own deep link (`?asset=…&schedule=…`, `?vehicle=…`). Each item keeps the reminder's own status and its trigger (`reminderTrigger`: the due date, or the mileage threshold when mileage made it due), and every status shown on the dashboard (the Next maintenance tile, its peek, the HVAC and car names, the brief) uses Maintenance's copy helpers: `reminderStatusLabel` ("Overdue", "Due today", "Expires soon") and `reminderDueDetail` ("Due Aug 1, 2026 · 2 weeks ago", "At 49,000 mi · 1,200 mi over"). A pet is fed when today's target is met ("2 of 2 today"); the house and tile use its `feedingCountLabel` and `feedingHeadline` wording.

Loading, failed and empty are kept apart (`assembleDashboardFacts` in `src/lib/dashboard/assemble.ts`). ### Command palette and shortcuts

`CommandCenter` renders only for approved household members. No key handler acts while an input method editor is composing (`isComposingKey`: `isComposing` or `keyCode` 229): the palette input ignores Enter and the arrows, the palette does not close on that Escape, and shortcuts, the Konami listener, peek cards, the tab-bar fan and Wrapped's arrow keys do the same. It handles Ctrl/Cmd+K anywhere, and other shortcut keys only when focus is not in a text field and no dialog or menu is visibly open; closed overlays that stay in the DOM (the tab bar's aria-hidden quick-add fan, or the whole tab bar on desktop) don't count (`matchShortcut` and `isTypingTarget` in `src/lib/dashboard/shortcuts.ts`); `G` waits 1.2 s for its second key. Shortcuts for modules the member cannot use are neither handled nor listed. Other components open the palette or the cheat sheet through window events (`openCommandPalette`, `openShortcutHelp` in `src/components/command/events.ts`).

`CommandPalette` is a dialog titled "Search or quick add" with a combobox input and a listbox (the project has no cmdk dependency). Each open remounts it and loads a read-only index (members, lists, needed items, open chores, pets, assets, vehicles) for permitted sections. Results are grouped: Quick add, Chores, Shopping, Pets, Maintenance, then Go to (pages filtered by permission, with `G` hints), and enter staggered when the palette opens. The index also loads rooms (`chores.view`), active vehicles (not retired or sold) and home assets (not retired) for the parser. A shopping list or item result opens its list (`/shopping?list=<id>`).

`parseQuickAdd` (`src/lib/dashboard/quick-add.ts`) is deterministic and matches whole words only (pet names, list names, service words, "and"). Feedings name a known pet ("fed Maple", "Pixel ate 1 can"); the amount is read with the Pets module's `parsePortion` (".5 cups", "1/2 cup", "1 1/2 cups", "one can"), and a unit with an unreadable amount is rejected rather than guessed; "fed …" that matches no pet falls back to search. Explicit shopping verbs ("add", "buy", "need") and "to <list>" pick items and a list by name or type (default: the Grocery list); items are split on commas and the words "and", "&" and "+", then parsed by Shopping's own quick-add parser (counts, name casing, repeated names). Maintenance needs a service word or a mileage; a vehicle (nickname, make, model or "make model") or, without a mileage, a home asset named in whole words becomes its target ("for the Civic" is removed from the title). A name that fits more than one record (two Hondas, two "Furnace" assets) selects nothing: the intent keeps the name (`targetQuery`), the preview says "which Honda?" and keeps Confirm disabled until the member picks one. Chores need "chore" or "remind me to", a date ("tomorrow", weekdays, "next friday", "in 3 days", "Aug 20", "8/20"), a repeat ("every Tuesday", "daily", "monthly") or an assignee ("for Sam", "for me", "@sam": first name, full name or email name, case-insensitively; the phrase is removed from the task, and a name that fits several members leaves the assignee empty in the preview, which asks who it is for and keeps Confirm disabled until someone is chosen), and may name a room in whole words ("in the kitchen" is removed from the task, a bare "kitchen" stays). Bare words ("milk and eggs") are offered as a shopping suggestion only when search finds nothing. Anything else falls back to search.

AI fallback (H13): when the parser finds nothing, or only a bare-words shopping guess, for something that reads like a sentence (`shouldOfferAiParse`), the palette offers "Read “…” with AI" (first when nothing else matches, otherwise last, so Enter never spends an AI request by surprise). Choosing it calls `parseCommand` (`src/ai/flows/parse-command-flow.ts`) with the sentence, today's date and display names paired with opaque references (`m1`, `r1`, `p1`, `l1`, `v1`; `buildAiParseRequest`), never emails or ids. A name that is or contains an email, or equals the member's email name (display names fall back to emails for members without one), is sent as a neutral label such as "Member 2" (`modelSafeName`, `src/ai/redact.ts`); the reference still maps the answer back. The answer is mapped back to the same intents (`intentFromAiParse`), filtered by the same permissions, and shown in the same preview card with "Read by AI. Check the details before you confirm."; "AI could not read that" otherwise. Each AI read belongs to one input revision (`createRevisionGuard`): editing the sentence (typed or spoken), opening another preview or going back makes it stale, and a stale read's answer, error and status are ignored, so an older, slower answer never replaces what the member is reviewing. Nothing is written until Confirm.

Choosing a quick-add result shows a preview; nothing is written until Confirm. Writes use the owning modules' contracts (`src/components/command/quick-add-actions.ts`): shopping items merge into a still-needed item of the same name with Shopping's `findMergeTarget` and `mergeIntoNeededItem` transaction (online only, as on the list), otherwise they are created with `buildShoppingListItem` and the share target's category resolution (the guarded, quota-counted categorizer; `Other` offline); feedings go through the Pets module's shared feed operation (`feedPet` in `src/lib/pets/feed-operation.ts`, with who logged them and its "Feed again?" check: when someone fed the pet in the last 90 minutes or today is complete, the preview shows "Sam fed Rex 12 min ago. Feed again?" and Confirm becomes **Feed again**; the toast offers Undo for 10 s through `undoFeed`), using the sentence's amount or else the pet's usual portion from `feedingPlan`, and the food type of the pet's latest feeding (`knownFoodType` of the newest entry across both date shapes, read offline-aware; Dry only without history) with no comment, the same fields as the Pets one-tap feed; one-time chores are a temporary task (`sourceType: 'temporary'`, no room) with a deterministic targeted "New chore assigned" notification, skipped when you assign yourself. The assignee follows the Chores rule (`choreCapabilities(...).canAssign`, i.e. `chores.assign`): with it you may pick any approved member; without it the chore is always yours, and a requested other member is replaced by you with a note in the preview. The preview card lets the member change the assignee and the room (one-time and repeating chores) and the vehicle or asset (maintenance); a mileage needs a vehicle, so with vehicles but none named, Confirm waits for one (the vehicle named in the sentence, else the household's only vehicle, is preselected from the loaded records (`defaultMaintenanceTarget`), even if they arrive after the card opens), and a reading below the vehicle's recorded odometer gets a note. Every preview waits for the palette index: no quick-add suggestion is offered until members, rooms, lists, pets, vehicles and assets have loaded, and the preview keeps Confirm disabled with a loading note while they load (`quickAddConfirmBlocker`), so no choice is made against half the records. A section that failed to load is kept apart from one that loaded empty (`CommandIndex.failed`: members, lists, pets, rooms, vehicles, assets). A preview whose kind depends on a failed section (chores: members and rooms; maintenance: vehicles and assets; shopping: lists; feedings: pets; `failedSectionsFor`) keeps Confirm disabled with "Couldn't load your household's vehicles…" and a Try again button that rereads the index and returns to the input, so the suggestion is rebuilt from the full records. A failure never falls back to a different write shape (a reading is never saved as a general log because the vehicles could not be read). Created documents always get a new id, a readable slug plus a Firestore auto-id (`quickCreateId`), so a create (even one queued offline, when the cached list of existing ids may be incomplete) never overwrites an existing template or log. Repeating chores (`chores.create`) are a `chore-templates` document shaped like the Chores "Add Chore" dialog's (`repeatingChoreTemplate`, with Chores' own `buildRecurrence`; monthly days follow the dialog's 1-28 rule: `normalizeRepeat` moves a day after the 28th to the 28th before the preview, which then says so, so the confirmed schedule is exactly the saved one, and `recurrenceFromRepeat` refuses any other day rather than changing it), plus the "new repeating chore" notification for someone else; the household upkeep (`syncHouseholdUpkeep`) then runs so the first instance appears at once. Maintenance logs (`maintenance.createLogs`) are a routine log dated today shaped like the Maintenance "Add log" form's (`quickMaintenanceLog`): `targetType` vehicle/home_asset/general, `vehicleId`/`assetId`, `title`/`item`, the mileage on vehicle logs only (without a vehicle it goes in the notes). The modules keep these writes inline in their screens and export no write helper, so quick add builds the same documents from the modules' own builders; Firestore rules check the same permissions. Shopping items follow Shopping's quick-add contract: every entry is its own write, settled independently and split with `splitSettled` (`shoppingAddOutcome`). Saved entries are confirmed with a toast; failed ones stay in the preview with an inline message (`quickAddRetryMessage` plus the `friendlyError` reason), so Confirm retries only those and never adds the saved ones twice. Each retry re-reads the list's needed items before merging. Chores (one batch with its notification) and feedings (one transaction with the pet's feeding state online; one queued document offline, toast "Saved on this device. Couldn't check whether someone else fed Rex.") are single atomic writes. Every other quick-add write goes through `awaitWriteUnlessOffline`: offline it is queued (it syncs on reconnect), the palette closes with "Saved offline" instead of waiting for a server acknowledgement, and a later rejection is reported with `toastError`; reads use the offline-aware helpers; the tab-bar fan only opens the palette or a page. After every confirmed write the palette dispatches the `homehub:data-changed` window event (`notifyDataChanged` in `src/lib/data-changed.ts`, detail `{ module, householdId, listId?, petId?, choreId? }`). Screens that load with one-time reads subscribe with `onDataChanged` and refresh silently: the Shopping list re-reads the open list (if it is the one changed) and the card counts with its offline-aware reads, and the Chores page re-runs its load. Pets uses live listeners, so it does not need it; maintenance logs dispatch `{ module: 'maintenance' }` for the Maintenance screen to refresh. Saves confirm with a toast and `playFeedback('add')`; failures use `toastError`.

Voice add (H14) uses the Web Speech API (`useVoiceInput`). The mic button is hidden where the API is missing, starts only on a tap, fills the input with the transcript and never submits by itself.

### Design foundation

**Color tokens.** Every color is an HSL channel token defined once per theme preset in `src/lib/theme/tokens.ts` (`PRESETS`) and emitted by `themeCss()` into `<head>` as `:root[data-theme="<preset>"]` rules (Midnight doubles as the `:root` fallback). Tailwind maps them to classes: surfaces `background`/`card`/`popover` (aliases `surface-base`/`surface-raised`/`surface-overlay`), `foreground`, `muted(-foreground)`, `secondary`, `primary` (brand accent for fills, links, selection), `accent` (a subtle hover/selected surface, not the brand color), status `destructive`/`success`/`warning`/`info` (each usable as text on any surface and as a fill with its `-foreground`), `border` (component boundaries, at least 3:1), `border-subtle` (decorative dividers), `input`, `ring`, `scrim` (dialog backdrops), `chart-1..5`, and `member-1..8` with `member-foreground`. Code outside the token files uses these classes or `hsl(var(--token))`, never raw color literals (the Google logo in the login dialog keeps Google's mandated colors). Non-color tokens live in `src/app/globals.css`: one radius scale (`--radius`, Tailwind `rounded-sm` to `rounded-2xl`), elevation shadows (`shadow-raised` for cards, `shadow-overlay` for dialogs, popovers, menus, sheets and toasts, each with a soft shadow and a 1 px inner highlight), the focus ring, and motion durations/easings.

**Presets and contrast.** Dark presets: Midnight (default), Hearth, Forest. Light presets: Light, Sunrise. Each preset sets its own foreground on filled colors (bright accents carry dark text in dark presets; deep accents carry white text in light presets), so filled buttons are AA (D01). In dark presets each elevation level is lighter than the one below. `tests/unit/theme-contrast.test.ts` (`npm run test:unit`) asserts at least 4.5:1 for every text pair (including accent, status and member colors as text on base/raised/overlay/muted surfaces and on their 10-15% tints) and at least 3:1 for borders, inputs, the focus ring and filled controls.

**Theme application.** `<html>` is server-rendered with `data-theme="midnight"` and class `dark` (`suppressHydrationWarning`). The inline script from `themeBootstrapScript()` (every embedded value goes through `scriptLiteral`, which escapes `<`, `>`, U+2028 and U+2029 so nothing can close the `<script>` tag) runs before first paint and before hydration: it reads `localStorage["homehub.appearance"]`, resolves System mode with `prefers-color-scheme`, sets `data-theme`, the `dark`/`light` class (for Tailwind `dark:` variants) and the `theme-color` meta, and sets `data-motion="reduce"` when reduced motion applies, and `data-text-scale` for a 115% or 130% text size. It never throws; invalid stored values fall back to defaults. After hydration `AppearanceProvider` keeps `<html>` in sync (OS theme changes in System mode, other tabs via the `storage` event, profile sync). Switching themes uses the View Transitions API: the new theme is revealed in an expanding circle (`clip-path`, 520 ms) from the control that changed it; where the API is missing or motion is reduced the switch is instant.

**Touch targets (D-2).** Every shared control meets 44 CSS px, and 48 px on touch screens (`(pointer: coarse)`). The token is `--target-min` in `globals.css` (`2.75rem`, `3rem` under coarse pointers; in rem, so it grows with the text size). Tailwind has a `coarse:` variant (`screens.coarse` in `tailwind.config.ts`, emitted after the width breakpoints, so it wins over `md:`). Inputs, select triggers and default-size buttons are `h-[var(--target-min)]` tall (a caller's own `h-*` replaces it); dropdown, checkbox, radio and select menu items have that min height. Compact controls keep their look and get an invisible hit area of at least `--target-min` from the `hit-slop` utility (a centered `::after`; the element must be positioned): every `Button` size (and any caller that shrinks one, e.g. `h-8 w-8`), checkboxes, radio items, switches (now `h-6 w-11` in rem), tab triggers (`TabsList` has no fixed height), toggle-group items and the dialog and sheet close buttons. The focus ring is the element's own outline, so it still hugs the visible control. Inputs and select triggers use 16 px text on touch screens. The phone tab bar labels and quick-add fan use rem sizes and a rem fan radius. Tightly packed grids size the cell itself to the target instead and switch the overflowing area off with `hit-slop-off`, so a tap near an edge never picks the neighbor: the shared date picker (`src/components/ui/calendar.tsx`) uses gapless day cells of `min(--target-min, (100vw - 40px) / 7)` (48/44 px; 50 px on a 390 px phone at 130% text so the month still fits), and the recurrence weekday toggles are `--target-min` square. Single-row toggle groups and tab lists are wider than the target, so their areas only extend vertically and never overlap. The Calendar page's month grid uses its own buttons, without `hit-slop`. Known limit: a hit area can stick out a few pixels past a button flush against a scroll container's edge. Tab triggers are sized to `--target-min` themselves (no overflowing hit area), so tab lists that wrap into several rows on phones never have overlapping targets.

**Text size (D-4 / PR-14).** A per-device setting (Profile > Appearance, see [Identity and Profile](../identity-profile/README.md)) with three steps, 100% (default), 115% and 130%. The pre-paint script sets `data-text-scale="115"` or `"130"` on `<html>` (absent at 100%) and `globals.css` maps it to a percentage root font size, so every rem-based size (text, spacing, control heights, the header and tab bar) scales together while the browser's own font setting still counts; px sizes do not scale, so shared-shell sizes are rem. `parseTextScale` clamps any stored value to 100-130 and snaps it to the nearest step (a tie goes to the smaller); the script mirrors it (`tests/unit/ui-foundations.test.ts`). Media queries do not change with the setting (px breakpoints ignore the root font size), so layouts that must hold at large text use rem-based widths instead of breakpoints: the header measures its links; on phones (< 640 px) at 115%/130% it hides the wordmark (`.header-wordmark`, the logo link keeps its name); the More sheet's tiles are at least 6rem wide (`auto-fill`, two across at 130% on a phone) and grow to fit two-line labels; dialog and alert-dialog footers wrap their buttons from 640 px. Layouts must hold at 130% on a 390 px phone, 1024/1200/1280 px tablets and a 1440 px desktop: avoid fixed heights that clip text and use rem sizes.

**Dialogs (SH-19).** `DialogContent` and `AlertDialogContent` are at most `--dialog-max-h` tall (`100dvh`, falling back to `100vh`, minus the top and bottom safe areas and 2 rem) and scroll inside (`overflow-y-auto`, `overscroll-contain`), so the footer buttons can always be scrolled to, including above an on-screen keyboard that shrinks the viewport. Top and bottom sheets have the same cap. TODO: on phones (< 640 px) render dialogs as bottom sheets with the existing `Sheet` primitive.

**Focus.** One ring for every interactive element: a base `:focus-visible` rule draws a 2 px `--ring` outline with a 2 px offset (`--focus-ring-width`, `--focus-ring-offset`). Components do not add their own focus rings; menu items keep their highlight and inset the ring (`-outline-offset-2`).

**Surfaces and effects.** The header, phone tab bar and sheets use `surface-glass` (frosted overlay surface, mostly opaque so text keeps its contrast). `AmbientBackground` paints two slow aurora blobs (`--ambient-1`/`--ambient-2`) and a faint grain behind the shell at the preset's single intensity token `--ambient-intensity` (5-7%); loops are 32 s and 41 s, paused while the tab is hidden (`<html data-visibility="hidden">`) and static under reduced motion. Any element with the `glow-card` class (or `<GlowCard>`) gets a pointer-following spotlight and brightening border from the single delegated `GlowPointerTracker`; it runs only for `(hover: hover) and (pointer: fine)` and changes only pseudo-element layers, never the element's position. Dashboard cards and Maintenance asset/vehicle cards use it; `hover:bg-accent` is now a subtle surface lift instead of a solid teal fill (D10).

**Per-member colors.** `memberColor(uid)` (`src/lib/member-colors.ts`) returns a deterministic slot 1-8 (FNV-1a hash of the id) with `color`, `foreground`, `soft` tint and static Tailwind classes (`text-member-N`, `bg-member-N`, `ring-member-N`, `chip`, `softChip`). `memberColorMap(uids)` assigns collision-free colors for up to eight members regardless of input order. Member colors are AA as text on every surface and as chip fills in every preset.

**Motion system.** `motion` (pinned `14.3.1`, React 18 compatible) is the only animation library; import from `motion/react`. Budgets and helpers live in `src/lib/motion.ts` (`DURATION`, `EASE`, `SPRING_UI`, `SPRING_SNAPPY`, `STAGGER`, `staggerDelay`, `PAGE_ENTER`). Contract for all motion: when reduced motion is on (system `prefers-reduced-motion` or the in-app switch), `<html>` carries `data-motion="reduce"`; `useReducedMotion()` from `src/hooks/use-reduced-motion.ts` returns the combined value; `MotionConfig` disables transform/layout animation and zeroes default transitions; and global CSS collapses every CSS animation and transition under both `@media (prefers-reduced-motion: reduce)` and `:root[data-motion="reduce"]`. New CSS animations must honor both selectors.

- Route transitions (H12): one engine, `runRouteTransition` in `src/components/motion/route-transition.ts` (no `motion` import, so it is first-paint safe). `MorphRouteListener` (root layout) turns plain internal link clicks into View Transitions: it listens on `window` in the bubble phase, after `next/link` has started the navigation, so it never changes where a link goes; it skips modified clicks, other origins, query-only changes, links inside `data-route-transition="manual"` (use it when a click handler delays navigation, like the house's room zoom) and clicks a handler already turned into a morph. While a transition runs `<html>` carries `data-route-transition`, which names the page wrapper (`[data-page-transition]`, from `PageTransition`) `page-content` (in `globals.css`, not a CSS module, which would rename the ident so the `::view-transition-*(page-content)` rules never matched), the header `site-header` and the phone tab bar `site-tab-bar`: page content fades out in 120 ms and the new page fades in with an 8 px settle over `--duration-ui` (`globals.css`), and the header and tab bar are shown live so they never re-animate. Nothing is named otherwise, so the theme reveal still clips one whole-page snapshot. The old page is held until the URL (path and query) has committed, at most 500 ms after a link click (`ROUTE_TRANSITION`). The 8 px settle moves only the transition's snapshot, which takes no input; the real page never moves. Where the API is missing, and for back/forward, `PageTransition` plays an opacity-only fade in CSS (`page-transition.module.css`, `PAGE_ENTER` values) instead; during a view transition it adds no animation of its own. The first page of a session is not animated, and under reduced motion (system or in-app) nothing is held or animated.
- Tabs (D09): `TabsContent` (`src/components/ui/tabs.tsx`, used by every tab set) fades a panel in, opacity only, in 150 ms (`tabs.module.css`, `TAB_ENTER`) each time it goes from inactive to active. Radix keeps every panel element rendered (inactive ones are `hidden`), so the trigger is activation, not mounting: a MutationObserver on the panel's own `data-state` sets `data-tab-enter` before paint (`tabEnterChange`), which also works for uncontrolled tab sets whose wrapper does not re-render. The panel shown on first render does not animate, it is focusable at once, only opacity changes (its buttons are clickable at once and never move), and reduced motion shows it at once.
- Loading: every route has a `loading.tsx` built from `src/components/route-skeletons.tsx` that mirrors the page's container, header and first content block; skeletons expose a polite `role="status"` label. `NavigationProgress` shows a 2 px top bar only when an internal link navigation has not committed after 150 ms (it gives up after 10 s).
- Overlays: dialogs, alert dialogs, sheets, popovers, dropdown/menubar menus, selects and tooltips enter on a spring curve (`.ease-spring-enter`, `--ease-spring` is motion's `spring(0.25, 0.2)` as a CSS `linear()` easing, 220 ms; kept in sync by a unit test). Sheets open in 220 ms (previously 500 ms).
- `transition-ui` is the one scoped transition utility (color, background, border, opacity, shadow, transform at 150 ms); `transition-all` is no longer used.
- `Stagger` (`import { Stagger } from '@/components/motion/stagger'`) staggers its direct children with CSS keyed by position (`stagger.module.css`): the first at once, then 30 ms apart, items from the 12th on sharing the last delay (330 ms, `staggerItemDelayMs`), each an opacity-only fade-in over `--duration-ui` (items are often click targets, and clickable targets never move, so nothing translates the item box; `tests/e2e/shell-wave4.spec.ts` checks a staggered button's box is identical at the start and end of its entrance). Children keep their own classes (grid spans) and semantics (`as="ul"` with `li` children), the entrance needs no animation library, and children that mount more than `STAGGER_WINDOW_MS` (1.2 s) after it just appear (`data-stagger="settled"`); reduced motion shows everything at once. `StaggerItem` is an optional wrapper: a plain element, or a motion element when given motion props (`layout`, `exit`, `transition`, ...), so rows inside `AnimatePresence` keep their exit and layout animations (Chores' rows); `data-*` and DOM props pass through. `count` from the earlier motion-based API is accepted and ignored (deprecated). Used for the bento tiles, Notification Center rows, palette results and the household member list; `NumberTicker` ticks numbers in 400-700 ms and is safe inside any label: its element is one inline span whose only text node is the final value at every moment, so the surrounding `textContent`, `innerText` and accessible name read exactly ("To Do (1)", "1 of 2 today"), with no screen-reader copy, no hidden width copy and no grid or block box in the label. While a change counts, that text is drawn transparent and an inline `::after` (`content: attr(data-display) / ""`, alt text empty) paints the moving digits over it, padded to the final width with figure and punctuation spaces (`tickerFrame`), so it adds no text, no name and no width; a count that would be wider than the final value (12 → 9) changes at once. `animateOnMount` (default true, for stat tiles) counts up from 0 on first show; labels pass false and tick only on change. Reduced motion, SSR and pre-hydration show the final value. `tests/e2e/shell-wave4.spec.ts` renders the real component in a button, an accordion trigger and running text and checks exact text, `innerText` and accessible names mid-count and after.
- Shared-element morphs between any two elements (card → detail), through the same engine as route transitions. Contract: (1) name both ends with `sharedId(kind, id, part)` from `@/lib/motion` and spread `morphProps(name)` (or `style={morphStyle(name)}`) on each; names must be unique on the page at both ends, so a card that stays mounted beside its open detail passes `null` while the detail is open. (2) In the click handler, `e.preventDefault()` and `useMorphNavigation().navigate(href, { waitFor?, replace? })` (`@/hooks/use-morph-navigation`). It works across routes and for query changes on one route (`/shopping?list=…`, `/maintenance?asset=…`); `waitFor` (a `data-morph` name from `morphProps`, or a predicate) holds the old snapshot until the destination element has rendered, at most 800 ms in total. The page content crossfades around the morph; plain `router.push`/`replace` where the API is missing or motion is reduced. Within one route without a URL change, motion `layoutId={sharedId(...)}` still works. The dashboard room → Chores morph uses this path.

**Feedback.** `playFeedback('complete' | 'add' | 'celebrate')` from `src/lib/feedback.ts` plays a short Web Audio tone (at most 400 ms, no audio files) and/or a `navigator.vibrate` pattern, only when the member enabled Sound or Haptics in Profile > Appearance; it is a silent no-op otherwise and never throws. Overview metrics and generated sections are filtered by verified server-side effective permissions.

**Empty states.** `EmptyState` (`src/components/empty-state.tsx`) is the one empty-state pattern (H24): an inline-SVG illustration, a title, a one-line invitation and an optional primary and secondary action. Props: `kind` (`chores`, `lists`, `pets`, `assets`, `notifications`, `generic`; default `generic`), `title`, `description`, `action` and `secondaryAction` (`{ label, icon?, onClick }` renders a real `<button>`, `{ label, icon?, href }` a real Next `<Link>`; the primary uses the filled button, the secondary the ghost button), `headingLevel` (2-4, default 2, so it fits the page outline), `compact` (smaller art and spacing for cards and popovers), plus any `div` attribute. The illustrations share one style (primary-tinted backdrop, muted line drawing in `currentColor`, one primary accent, a sparkle), use token classes only so every preset recolors them, and are `aria-hidden`; `EmptyStateIllustration` exports the art alone. The content fades in with a 6 px settle (a 200 ms CSS animation, opacity/transform only, fixed illustration size, so nothing shifts). It starts at first paint, so server-rendered empty states never wait for hydration, and the global reduced-motion rules show the end state instantly. Modules adopt it in their own pages.

**Member avatars.** `MemberAvatar` (`src/components/member-avatar.tsx`) takes `uid`, `name`, `photoUrl?`, `size` (`sm` 24 px, `md` 36 px default, `lg` 56 px), `ring?` (a ring in the member color with a background-colored offset), `color?` (override, e.g. from `memberColorMap`), `decorative?` and `className`. It shows the photo once it has loaded; without a photo, or when the photo fails to load, it shows a generated diagonal gradient between the member's color token and a partner member token (`avatarGradientPartner`, three slots around the hue wheel) with one or two initials (`memberInitials`: first and last word, email local part, `?` when there is no letter) in `member-foreground`, which is AA on every member fill. The avatar is announced as the member's name (`role="img"`, `aria-label`); pass `decorative` when the name is printed next to it. `MemberAvatarStack` takes `members` (`{ uid, name, photoUrl? }[]`), `max` (circles including the `+k` chip, default 4), `size`, `ring`, `label?` and `className`; it colors members with `memberColorMap` so no two share a color, overlaps them with a background ring, and is one image to assistive tech named after the members ("Sam, Alex and 3 others"). Pure helpers live in `src/lib/member-avatar.ts`.

**Toasts.** The toast viewport sits at the top: top-center (at most 420 px wide) on phones and top-right from the `md` breakpoint, below the status bar/notch (`env(safe-area-inset-top)`) and inside the left/right safe areas. Dialogs, alert dialogs and sheets keep their primary buttons in a bottom footer and the phone tab bar is at the bottom, so a toast never covers them (previously toasts sat bottom-right on top of dialog buttons). Toasts slide in from the top and can be swiped right; the viewport ignores pointer events so its empty padding never blocks the header, and it stays above dialogs (`z-[100]`). Radix renders the viewport as a dismissable-layer branch, so pressing a toast does not close an open dialog. Toasts are compact on phones (16 px padding).

**Validation and errors.** One rule for every form: a problem with a field is shown inline under that field and never as a toast; toasts are only for outcomes (saved, deleted, could not reach the server). Use `FieldError` (`src/components/field-error.tsx`; props `id` of the field and the message as children, renders nothing when empty, `role="alert"`) with `fieldErrorProps(fieldId, error)` on the input (`aria-invalid` and `aria-describedby` to `${id}-error`), or `FormMessage` inside react-hook-form forms, which now has the same look (destructive text with an alert icon). `friendlyError(error)` (`src/lib/friendly-error.ts`) turns any thrown value into a plain-language message so raw Firebase or network text never reaches users: Firestore/Functions/Storage/Auth codes with or without a service prefix, HTTP statuses, `FirestorePermissionError`, fetch failures and timeouts map to `permission-denied`, `unavailable`, `deadline-exceeded`, `not-found`, `resource-exhausted`, `unauthenticated`, `offline` (network-shaped or unknown errors while `navigator.onLine` is false) or `unknown`; `friendlyErrorKind` returns the kind and `FRIENDLY_ERROR_TITLES` a short title. `toastError(error, title?, options?)` from `src/hooks/use-toast.ts` (also returned by `useToast()`) shows a destructive toast whose description always comes from `friendlyError`; the global `FirebaseErrorListener` uses it for permission errors. Existing call sites that build their own error toasts are migrated by their modules.

## Data Model and Persistence

The shell owns no Firestore documents. It consumes `User`, `Household`, `HouseholdMember`, and permission data supplied by auth/household context. Overview facts are transient and the optional model narrative remains component state. Dashboard facts are derived in memory from live listeners; the only part written anywhere is the last-known house rooms (`ChoreFacts`), saved on the device for the next visit's first frame (below). Quick add writes only documents owned by Shopping, Pets and Chores, in their existing shapes (no new fields). Tab-local keys (sessionStorage): `homehub.shell.crashes` (crash timestamps inside the last 10 minutes, for the crash-loop guard) and `homehub.shell.update-prompted` (the newest build this tab offered) and `homehub.shell.forced-reload` (the last minimum-build reload: `{ buildId, at }`). Device-local keys: `homehub.session` (`1` while someone is signed in on this device; a pre-paint hint only, never used for access), `homehub.dashboard.seasonal` (`on`/`off`), `homehub.dashboard.briefTyped` (the last household day the brief typed itself) `homehub.dashboard.weather` (the last weather report, 30 minutes) and `homehub.house-rooms` (`{ v: 1, uid, householdId, day, chores }`: the last live `ChoreFacts`, shown only for the same user, household and household day until the live listeners deliver, never after a chores failure; deleted at the start of logout and whenever Auth resolves with nobody signed in). The dashboard writes one profile field: `users/{email}.preferences.weather` (`{ enabled: boolean, city: string }`, the member's own profile, allowed by the existing self-update allowlist; written with `awaitWriteUnlessOffline`). Missing or malformed means off.

Appearance preferences are stored in `localStorage["homehub.appearance"]` (mode, dark and light preset, reduce motion, sound, haptics, text size) for pre-paint use, and the synced part is persisted by the identity/profile module in the existing `users/{email}.theme` field (see [Identity and Profile](../identity-profile/README.md)). Sound, haptics and text size stay device-local.

## Authentication, Roles, and Security

- Logged-out users see the landing page at `/` (no module navigation, no data reads) and normal route content plus login controls elsewhere.
- Signed-in users without a household receive the create/join overlay.
- Members with role `newuser` receive a waiting-for-approval overlay on routes other than `/profile`.
- Desktop navigation uses `hasPermission`. The phone tab bar and More sheet omit (rather than disable) items whose view permission the member lacks: `chores.view`, `shopping.view`, `pets.view`, `maintenance.view`, `automation.view`, and `household.view`; Home, Barcode Library, and Profile are always listed. Calendar is gated by `chores.view` in both. Dashboard tiles, house sections, palette results and pages, shortcut keys and quick-add actions are each filtered by the matching view or write permission (`chores.create`, `shopping.edit`, `pets.addLogs`, `maintenance.createLogs`); Firestore rules remain the authority for every read and write. Direct routes still depend on feature code and Firebase rules.

While either overlay is shown, `HouseholdManager` renders only the overlay: the header and child routes are not mounted, so their effects do not run. Children do render while household state is still loading and for logged-out users. Security must be enforced by rules and module code, not by the overlay alone.

## Integrations and Background Processing

The shell mounts foreground Firebase Cloud Messaging handling and links to the notification bell. The overview calls the authenticated AI server module only after the brief's "Rewrite the brief with AI" sparkle is pressed; provider failure leaves the plain brief and the exact facts visible. The dashboard makes no AI calls on its own. Live weather (opt-in) calls Open-Meteo from the browser with only the member's city name and the coordinates it returns; it has no key and no proxy. Quick add calls the `parseCommand` AI action only when the member picks "Read … with AI" (one daily AI request), and otherwise an AI action only when confirming shopping items, through the existing guarded, quota-counted `categorizeGroceryItem` (as the share target does), falling back to `Other` on any failure, timeout or when offline. Fonts (Inter for body text, Space Grotesk for headings, exposed as `--font-inter` and `--font-space-grotesk`) are bundled at build time from the `@fontsource-variable/inter` and `@fontsource-variable/space-grotesk` packages (Latin subset, variable weights) through `next/font/local` in `src/app/layout.tsx`; builds never download fonts, so a Google Fonts outage cannot fail a build or a deploy. No shell-owned Cloud Function or scheduled job exists.

Authenticated emulator tests enter through `/e2e-login`; that route is rendered only for an explicit demo-project emulator configuration and delegates custom-token sign-in to the identity module.

## Invariants and Failure Behavior

- Global providers must remain ordered so all consumers are inside `AuthProvider`.
- Dashboard tiles and house links appear only for sections the member can view; destination security is authoritative.
- The dashboard never calls a model on load; exact counts come from HomeHub data. The brief's AI rewrite stays on demand, and the plain brief is the default. The palette calls the AI reader only when the member picks "Read … with AI", and never writes before Confirm.
- Quick add never writes without an explicit Confirm and never writes repeating chores or maintenance logs.
- Attention animations never loop forever: the overdue glow, pet walk and pantry pulse run three cycles and hold until their state changes (HP-16a).
- Every dashboard control is at least 44 px, and 48 px on coarse pointers (D-2); nothing on the dashboard needs hover to be read.
- Under reduced motion the living house, brief, bell and tab bar render end states (no wandering, glow, typing, disco, ring or fan animation, no room zoom, no tile tilt), the shell tint and weather are not rendered, and the landing shows the finished house.
- Live weather never sends anything but the city name (and the coordinates geocoded from it) and never shows an error outside its own tile: a failed refresh keeps the last good report, marked stale, and with no good report only the weather tile says "Unavailable right now." while the house and shell effects stay off.
- A dashboard listener error is never final (backoff to 60 s, retry on `online` and visible); stale, unauthorized and failed sections are told apart from loading and empty, and last-good data never crosses a household, member or permission change. Midnight in the household zone resubscribes only the day-scoped queries and blanks nothing.
- Firebase permission errors are converted to global toasts through the error emitter/listener pair, using `toastError`, so the text is the plain-language `friendlyError` message.
- Toasts never cover dialog or sheet footers or the phone tab bar: the viewport stays at the top of the screen inside the safe areas. Bottom placement for action toasts on phones is not used: Radix has one viewport per provider, so action toasts cannot be placed apart from the others.
- Nothing reloads the page on its own except the crash guard's single reload for a stale-build chunk failure, which never fires while the shell is busy (a save, an open Undo toast, unsaved input), offline or without a stored crash history, and never twice within 10 minutes. The update prompt never reloads by itself. The one other automatic reload is the minimum-build check's (a page older than the published minimum build, only when idle and online, at most once per build within 10 minutes).
- The offline pill claims only that changes which can be made offline will sync; it never names a feature or a write.
- Undo windows come from `UNDO_WINDOW_MS` only; a feature must not set its own Undo duration.
- An Undo toast is never displaced by a newer toast while a toast without a held action can go instead, and at most one Undo per group shows.
- The offline pill adds no `role="status"` region while online.
- The household overlays replace child routes, but shell loading does not: children mount while household state is resolving, so loading must not be treated as proof that route effects did not run.
- At 360 px and wider supported mobile viewports, the dashboard (house yard strip, tiles, peeks) must not make the document scroll horizontally.
- The theme script must stay dependency-free and must not throw; if storage is blocked the default dark theme applies and in-session changes still work.
- No animation delays content, errors or focus: the first page is not animated, loading skeletons match page layout, overlays remain interactive during their enter animation, and the progress bar is decorative (`aria-hidden`).
- `html { scrollbar-gutter: stable }` keeps pages from shifting sideways when a scrollbar appears.
- Ambient and glow effects are decorative (`aria-hidden`, `pointer-events: none`) and must keep text contrast; new ambient effects get one intensity token per preset. Guardrail G5: every ambient effect (aurora, shell tint, weather, pet blink and idle sway, house window lights and fridge pulse) scales with the same `--ambient-intensity`, so they turn down together and 0 means still.
- Only one service worker may control the origin. Any new worker behavior goes into `/api/sw`; registering a different script at scope `/` would replace it and break push or offline support.
- The logo intro must stay at or under 900 ms, show the finished mark under reduced motion (system or `data-motion="reduce"`), and never replay within a tab session.
- The manifest, favicon and app icons must be regenerated from `logo-artwork.ts` with `npm run brand:icons`, never edited by hand.
