# Household Governance

## Purpose and Scope

This module owns household creation and recovery, membership, roles, permission presets/overrides, temporary invites, pending approval, member departure/removal, ownership transfer, and audit activity. It does not own feature records except compatibility cleanup such as unassigning chores when a member leaves.

## User-Facing Capabilities

- Create or join a household from the global onboarding overlay. Enter submits either form; an empty or over-long name and an incomplete invite code are explained inline under the field (`FieldError`). Invite codes are re-formatted as `XXXX-XXXX-XX` while typing or pasting, with or without dashes. The pending-approval overlay names the household the person asked to join.
- Manage Overview, Invite Codes, Members, Roles, Activity, and Danger Zone at `/household`. Tabs a viewer cannot use are not shown: Invite Codes needs `household.manageInvites`, Activity needs `household.manageMembers` (the rules let only its holders read audit entries). The page itself needs `household.view` (see View household below); without it an approved member sees a short "Household settings are not available to you" card (a pending person sees "Waiting for approval") and the page opens no listener. Signed-out visitors get a Sign in prompt. What a person may do on this page comes from the management policy below (their effective permissions and rank), not from their role name.
- All Household Manager data is live (Firestore listeners); there is no Refresh button.
- Overview: four stat cards (2x2 on phones, four across from `md`); long household names, even without spaces, wrap inside their card (at most three lines; `tests/e2e/shell-wave4.spec.ts` checks that the longest allowed name (80 characters) with no spaces stays inside its card and never overlaps the next one). The member and active-invite counts tick in with `NumberTicker` (the card's title keeps the plain text). Rename the household inline (Enter saves; errors inline). The household ID is plain small text, not an input.
- Generate, copy, revoke, and use temporary invite codes. An empty invite list shows an `EmptyState` with a Create invite code action. Create invite code (both the button and the empty-state action) stays disabled until the household, the signed-in member and `household.manageInvites` have loaded, so a tap is never silently ignored; `EmptyState` actions accept `disabled` for this. The list is a live client read (`inviteCodes` where `householdId ==`) for everyone who holds `household.manageInvites`, including a member granted it by an override; a code created or revoked through the server action appears or changes through that listener.
- Members (D13/H25): the member cards enter staggered (`Stagger`; instant under reduced motion). Each member is a card with `MemberAvatar` (photo or gradient) in a collision-free member color (`memberColorMap`), a color stripe, a role chip, "(you)", the email only on your own card and for viewers who may manage members (`canSeeMemberEmails`: owner and `household.manageMembers`), join time, a custom-permission count and, with `chores.view`, chores done this week and still open. Role changes, Customize permissions, Make owner and Remove live in a "..." menu that lists only actions the policy lets the viewer perform on that person (the role list holds only roles below the viewer's own); with none, no menu is rendered. Choosing a role asks for confirmation and says that custom permissions are reset. A household with only you shows an `EmptyState` inviting others.
- Pending people (`newuser`, shown as "Pending"; the internal name never appears) appear to people with `household.manageMembers` as an "Approve Jamie?" banner at the top of Members (slides in; instant under reduced motion) with a role picker (default Member; only roles below the viewer's own), Approve (the role-change server action) and Deny (the remove server action, after confirmation). A badge on the Members tab counts pending people.
- Roles (D12/H25): a permission matrix with permissions as rows (grouped by module) and the five role presets as columns; the viewer's role column is highlighted. Choosing a member in "Show" (or Customize permissions from their card) shows that member's role default beside their own permissions. When the policy lets the viewer edit that member (`household.manageRoles`, not themselves, not someone at or above their own rank; otherwise the description says why), the member column is editable: each cell is a real `role="checkbox"` button `--target-min` square (44 px, 48 px on touch screens; locked and read-only cells keep the same size) (Tab to move, Space/Enter to toggle), checkmarks draw in within 150 ms (instant under reduced motion), cells that differ from the default carry a dot, and a bar shows unsaved changes with Use role defaults, Discard and Save permissions. Locked cells (lock icon, reason as tooltip and for screen readers, and a short note under the matrix): the owner-only permissions (`household.delete`, `household.transferOwnership`), and any permission the viewer does not hold that the member does not already have. A permission the member already has can always be taken away. If a draft would grant something the viewer does not hold (for example through Use role defaults), the bar says so and Save is disabled. Save permissions asks for confirmation. Only differences from the role default are stored.
- Activity: plain-language entries ("Created an invite code", "Changed a role to Admin") with relative times; an empty log shows an `EmptyState`.
- Remove, Deny, Make owner, Leave, role changes and permission saves ask for confirmation in an alert dialog (no `window.confirm`). Approve does not: it is already a deliberate choice of role plus a button.
- Owners can permanently delete the household from Danger Zone after typing its name to confirm; the delete control is shown only to the owner, and Leave only to non-owners.
- Failures use `toastError` (plain language via `friendlyError`); raw Firebase text is never shown. Successful outcomes use short toasts.

## Architecture and Data Flow

`AuthProvider.createHousehold` checks for an empty name, then calls the `createHouseholdAction` server action with a fresh ID token and the browser's time zone (see Household Creation); it shows the returned message as a "Creation Failed" toast when one is refused, and a `friendlyError` toast when the server cannot be reached. `AuthProvider` also keeps the caller's own membership live (see Live membership below; a profile pointing at a household without a record is cleared, with no legacy recovery). Joining calls the `joinHouseholdWithInvite` server action (`src/app/household/actions.ts` → `src/lib/server/household-membership.ts`), which verifies the ID token and, in one Admin SDK transaction, checks the invite (exists, not revoked, not expired, uses left), refuses existing members and people still in another household, creates the pending record (no email; with `householdName`, the household's name at that moment, and a display name only when the token's name is not an email; photo by the same rule as creation, `membershipAvatarUrl`), increments `useCount`, records the redemption, writes the person's contact document (`members/{uid}/private/contact`, see Member contact), updates the profile cache (including `uid`), writes an audit entry, and sends a targeted approval notification to each active owner/admin. It writes no email to the household document or the record (see Email copies). Codes are accepted in any case, with or without dashes or spaces. The action returns only `{ household: { id, name }, member: { uid, role, status, joinedAt, householdName } }` (`JoinResult`): never the household document, which only approved members may read. While pending, `AuthProvider` shows the waiting screen from that result and, from then on, from `householdName` on the person's own record (`pendingHouseholdView`, `src/lib/member-directory.ts`); it never reads or listens to the household document for a pending person. An older pending record without `householdName` gets an empty name, so the waiting screen shows its generic text ("An owner or admin will choose your role...") and Profile shows the role without a household name. Leaving and deleting also run through server actions (`leaveHouseholdAction` and `deleteHouseholdAction` in the same files), each verifying the ID token first. Leaving (any non-owner) runs in one transaction that re-reads the caller's membership, so a concurrent ownership transfer makes it retry and be refused; it deletes the caller's member record and contact document, removes their email from a legacy `memberEmails` list when the household still has one (and writes no list otherwise), clears their profile cache, unassigns their incomplete chores and recurring templates, writes a `member.left` audit entry, and sends a targeted notification to each other active owner/admin. Deleting requires an active `owner` member record and an exact (trimmed) household-name match; it first, in one transaction, marks the household `deletingAt`, sets every membership to status `deleting` (rules grant non-active members nothing but a read of the household document, kept for members who were approved so a deletion that failed can be seen and finished; all other client reads, writes and uploads stop) and revokes every invite; joining and leaving are refused from then on. It then deletes Storage objects under `households/{householdId}/` (when a bucket is configured) and recursively deletes every subcollection except `members`; then, in one batch, it clears the profile cache of every member whose profile still points at the household, deletes the household's `inviteCodes`, the member records with their contact documents, and the household document. If cleanup fails partway, the household stays frozen: members see that it is being deleted, and the owner gets a Finish deleting action that resumes it. `HouseholdManager` chooses the onboarding or pending-approval overlay. `HouseholdManagementClient` performs no management writes itself: rename, invite create/revoke/revoke-all, approve and role change, permission edits, removal and ownership transfer each call a server action (see Management Operations) with a fresh ID token and show the returned plain-language message when one is refused. The daily pending-member reminder is a server action too (`remindPendingMembersAction`), so the component writes nothing to Firestore. `useHouseholdLiveData` opens its listeners only for viewers holding `household.view` (`householdManagerReads` in `household-model.ts` decides each one from the effective permissions, mirroring the rules; without `household.view` it opens none): `onSnapshot` listeners on `members` (the live household name comes from `AuthProvider`'s household listener); for holders of `household.manageMembers` (`canSeeMemberEmails`) one listener per member on their contact document (see Member contact); on `inviteCodes` (`householdId ==`) for holders of `household.manageInvites`; on `auditLogs` for holders of `household.manageMembers`; and two single-field chore queries for member stats (`isCompleted == false`, and `completedAt >=` the start of this week, Monday local time). Each listener fails independently, so a denied invite or audit read never blanks the member list.

`households/{householdId}/members/{uid}` is the only authority for household access in Firestore rules, Storage rules and server actions. `households.memberEmails`, `households.ownerEmail` and `users/{email}.{householdId,role,permissions}` are display/compatibility data and never grant access; a user listed only in `memberEmails` has no access and cannot create their own membership record. `households.ownerUid` points at the active `owner` membership record; only the server changes it (`transferOwnership` updates it, `ownerEmail` and both membership records in one transaction), and clients cannot write it, `ownerEmail`, `name` or `memberEmails`.

### Live membership

`AuthProvider` (`src/contexts/auth-context.tsx`) keeps one listener per document for the whole app and shares the results through `useAuth()`; no screen opens its own listener on these documents, and navigation adds no reads:

- `users/{email}` (from the server-confirmed profile on): its `householdId` chooses the household to watch;
- `households/{householdId}/members/{uid}`, the person's own record (`includeMetadataChanges`, so a server answer that matches the cache still confirms it): the only authority for access, role and permission overrides;
- `households/{householdId}`, only while that record is approved and active (the rules let only approved members read it; pending people use `householdName` on their record).

These are exactly the reads the rules allow: a person may always `get` their own record (also while pending; a deleted record reads as missing), approved members may `get` the household document, and only the person may read their profile. The pure state machine is `src/lib/auth/live-membership.ts` (`membershipReducer`, `membershipView`, `liveProfile`); each listener runs under `keepListening` (`src/lib/shopping/live-state.ts`: reopened after an error with backoff up to 30 s, and at once when the browser comes back online or a page hidden for 30 s or more is shown again).

- A role change or a permission override change reaches `currentMember`, `permissions` and `currentUser.role`/`permissions` at once; every screen gated by `permissions` updates in the open page. `permissions` keeps its object identity while the values are the same, and the record object stays the same while its data is unchanged, so effects that depend on them do not re-run for an unrelated snapshot. A toast says "Your role changed" when the person's role changes.
- Approval: the waiting screen stays until the record is approved; then the household document is loaded (screens show their loading states meanwhile) and the app opens, with a "You're in" toast naming the role.
- Removal, a declined join request, or the final step of a household deletion: when the server says the record is gone, or a read of it is denied, access ends. Everything of that household is dropped from the auth context, its listeners stop, the profile pointer is cleared if the server has not already done so, and `HouseholdManager` shows "You're no longer in this household" (removed), "Your request to join was declined" or "This household was deleted", each saying nothing from it is shown on this device any more, with Continue (onboarding, on `/`) and Sign Out. The notice names no household. A removal whose profile pointer change arrives before the record's own snapshot is handled the same way. Leaving or deleting from this device shows no notice (the request is marked as ending here before the server action runs, and unmarked if it is refused).
- Household deletion in progress (the record's status is `deleting`): the "This household is being deleted" screen, with Finish deleting for the owner; an owner who started the deletion from the household page keeps that page until the server action answers. The rules let nobody read a frozen household's document, and Finish deleting must send its exact name (the server checks it), so the owner always gets an editable Household name field. It is prefilled with a suggestion (`deletionNameSuggestion`): the name this session last saw or this device's cached copy of the household document (`getDocFromCache`, no network) first, the `householdName` on a record joined by invite last (that is the name at invitation time; a rename does not update it), empty when nothing is known. Finish deleting is enabled only while the field holds a name; when it is refused (a name that does not match, or no connection) the field stays as typed and says "That didn't finish. The name must match the household's name exactly; check it and try again." next to the server's own message.
- Offline is never removal: a cached "does not exist" is ignored; a network or other non-denial error keeps the last known state and retries; once access ended, a cached copy of the record never brings it back (only the server can). With nothing known (offline with nothing cached, or no answer within 15 s, `MEMBERSHIP_LOAD_TIMEOUT_MS`), the "Couldn't load your household" screen offers Try again, which reopens the listeners; a late answer opens the app by itself.
- A profile pointer is cleared as stale only for the signed-in account's own, server-confirmed profile and only when the membership state belongs to that same account (`stalePointerFor`); the write is re-checked against the signed-in account just before it runs and dropped if another account signed in meanwhile. A switch to another account un-confirms the previous profile at once, so the previous account's ended membership never clears the new account's pointer, and the legacy invite cleanup and the name/photo sync run only for the signed-in account's own membership.
- The watch is keyed by the signed-in account as well as the household: when another account signs in on an open page (for example a sign-in synchronised from another tab), even one in the same household, the previous account's membership, notice, listeners and device data are dropped, the screens remount, and the new account starts from its own record; nothing of the previous person's role or permissions is shown meanwhile.
- A denied or missing household document never ends access by itself; the record decides, and the household listener keeps retrying.
- Household data on this device: when this device stops showing a household (access ended, the person left or deleted it, the profile pointed somewhere else, or sign-out), `forgetHouseholdOnDevice` (`src/lib/auth/household-scope.ts`) removes the dashboard's last-known house (`homehub.house-rooms`), that household's shopping Undo journal entries (all of them at sign-out), its per-household keys (`HOUSEHOLD_KEY_PREFIXES`: the pantry's last-used list `homehub.pantry.recentList.<householdId>`; all of them at sign-out), its entries in the shopping device maps (`HOUSEHOLD_MAP_KEYS`: the last-used Grocery list `homehub.shopping.recentGrocery.v1`, keyed by household, and this device's own recent ticks `homehub.shopping.localTicks.v1`, keyed `<householdId>/<listId>`; an emptied or unreadable map is removed, both maps at sign-out), and, through the `homehub:household-forgotten` event, in-memory memories such as the shopping category memory (`src/lib/shopping/add-items.ts`). `HouseholdManager` keys the screens by account and household, so moving to another household, or another person signing in, remounts every screen and no screen state, listener, Undo offer or pending-write tracker of the previous household survives; access ending replaces the screens with the notice. Firestore's own offline cache belongs to the signed-in person and is cleared at sign-out (see [Identity and Profile](../identity-profile/README.md)); no screen reads another household from it.
- Server actions still authorize every operation on the server from the verified token and the stored membership; the live client state only decides what is shown.

### Member privacy (public member data)

What housemates see of each other is the membership record's public fields: `uid`, `displayName`, `avatarUrl`, `role`, `status`, `joinedAt` (and the record's permissions, used by the policy). `src/lib/member-directory.ts` turns records into `HouseholdPerson` values (`approvedPeople`, `personFromMemberRecord`); `publicDisplayName` never returns an email (an empty or email-like name becomes "Member"). The Chores page, the calendar, the command palette, the streaks tile and the member cards use only these fields; no screen reads a housemate's `users/{email}` profile, and the rules let only the person read it. Membership records carry no email (older records keep one until `scripts/ops/scrub-member-emails.mjs` removes it; the app uses such a leftover only as a matching key for chores written before `assignedToUid` existed). Emails are shown only to the person themself (their own card uses the email of their sign-in) and to member managers, from the protected contact document (member cards, the approval banner, which only managers see, and the invite and activity lists, which only holders of `household.manageInvites` and `household.manageMembers` respectively can read). The untargeted pending-member reminder and the household-wide ownership-transfer notice name people by display name only ("A member" when there is none).

### Member contact

Each member's email has a document of its own, `households/{householdId}/members/{uid}/private/contact` (`{ email, updatedAt }`, ISO time). Only the server writes it (Admin SDK, in the same transaction as the membership change): joining (`redeemInvite`) and household creation (`createHousehold`, the owner's). Approval and role changes leave it as it is. Leaving, removal (including denying a request) and household deletion delete it. Members who joined before it existed get it from `scripts/ops/backfill-member-contact.mjs` (dry run by default; `--apply`, `--project`, `--firebase-tools-login`; counts only; creates a missing document, updates one that holds another email, skips records without an email; each write in its own transaction that re-reads the record, so it is safe to stop and re-run; pure planner `scripts/ops/member-contact.mjs`).

The rules let the person (`request.auth.uid == uid`, also while pending) and holders of `household.manageMembers` who also hold `household.view` (`hasHouseholdManagerPermission`, so an override counts both ways) `get` it; nobody lists the `private` collection, reads any other document in it, or writes from a client.

It is the only place the household keeps a member's email. Every reader uses it and nothing else (`functions/src/shared/member-contact.ts`: `memberEmailOf`, `withContactEmails`, which drops any legacy `email` from a record): the server actions read it inside their transactions for the profile cache (`users/{email}`), removal's assignee queries, activity-log `targetEmail` and household deletion; the push trigger and the hourly upkeep read it with the Admin SDK (Notifications and Chores modules). A member without one has no email for these purposes (no profile-cache update, no push, no reminder) until `scripts/ops/backfill-member-contact.mjs` creates it. In the Household Manager only member managers subscribe to the contact documents (`useHouseholdLiveData`, `canManageMembers`); a missing or unreadable document is simply absent, so the card shows no email. Everyone else never requests it.

### Email copies

No email is written into a document housemates can read. Household creation writes no `ownerEmail` or `memberEmails`, joining adds nothing to `memberEmails`, leaving and removal only remove the departing person's email from a legacy list that still exists (`legacyMemberEmailRemoval`), and an ownership transfer deletes a legacy `ownerEmail` instead of rewriting it. Membership records are written without `email` (creation, joining). Chores and templates are assigned by uid only and completions record `completedByUid` and `completedByName`, never `completedByEmail` (see [Chores](../chores/README.md), "Assignee identity"); yearly chore stats are keyed by uid; notifications are targeted by uid only (see [Notifications](../notifications/README.md)). The rules refuse client writes that would add an email to a household document, a membership record or a notification (see Authentication, Roles, and Security). No document ID or label is derived from an email either: generated chore IDs are `templateId-roomId-day` (`occurrenceChoreId`, see Chores), every name stored for housemates to read falls back to "Member" ("Someone" on pet logs) rather than to the email (`publicDisplayName`, `loggerName`), and a photo URL in an email-named Storage folder (`users/{email}/avatars/...`, `isEmailFolderPhotoUrl` in `src/lib/member-directory.ts`) is never copied to a membership record: joining, household creation and `syncMemberProfile` leave the record without a photo instead, and `memberProfileDiffers` does not count it as drift.

`scripts/ops/scrub-shared-emails.mjs` (dry run by default; `--apply`, `--project`, `--firebase-tools-login`; counts only; update-time preconditions; re-runnable; pure decisions in `scripts/ops/shared-email-scrub.mjs`) removes the stored copies: `memberEmails` and `ownerEmail` from households (`ownerEmail` is kept on a household without `ownerUid`), assignee emails from chores and templates, `targetUserEmail` and action-entry emails from live notifications, and email labels from the yearly chore stats' `names`. Members' emails come from their contact documents, then their records. `scripts/ops/rekey-chore-stats.mjs` then moves the yearly chore stats from email-derived keys to uid keys (see [Chores](../chores/README.md), "Yearly stats"), and `scripts/ops/scrub-member-emails.mjs` (same conventions; pure decisions in `scripts/ops/member-email-scrub.mjs`) removes what is left: `email` from each membership record whose contact document holds the same email (decided and written in one transaction that re-reads both; a record without a contact document, or with a different email in it, keeps its email and is counted), `memberEmails`/`ownerEmail` from households, every assignee email from chores and templates (completed ones included, after setting `assignedToUid` where missing or stale) and `completedByEmail` from chores (after setting `completedByUid` where missing). A completion credited to someone who has left and named only by that email is credited to that person's identity instead (their uid when the chores pair the email with exactly one, else an opaque former-member key), settled once and stored in `households/{id}/ops/former-members`, which only the server can read; the scrub first moves the yearly stats' legacy keys to the same identities (see [Chores](../chores/README.md), "Assignee identity"); counts are reported. Run the re-key and the scrub, in either order, after the app, functions and rules of that release are live.

`scripts/ops/rekey-chore-ids.mjs` moves chores generated under IDs that carried the assignee (see [Chores](../chores/README.md), "Assignee identity"), and `scripts/ops/scrub-remaining-emails.mjs` (same conventions; pure decisions in `scripts/ops/remaining-email-scrub.mjs`) rewrites the remaining copies: any assignee or completer email still on a chore or template (as above, with the same stored identities of people who have left and the stats re-keyed to them first, so a completion credited to one of them keeps that credit), and labels that are an email (chores' `assignedToDisplayName` and `completedByName`, templates' `assignedToDisplayName`, maintenance attachments' `uploadedByName`, pet logs' `loggedByName`, the feeding state's `lastFedByName`), which become the current public name of the uid next to them, else "Member" ("Someone" for pet labels, "Unassigned" for an assignee label without a uid). On membership records it removes a `displayName` that is an email and an `avatarUrl` in an email-named photo folder, and counts records that still hold `email` (run `backfill-member-contact.mjs`, then `scrub-member-emails.mjs`). Run both after the app and functions of that release are live.

Invites keep `createdByEmail` and activity-log entries keep `actorEmail`/`targetEmail`; both are readable only by people who manage invites or members (owners and admins by role).

Members cannot write their own record under the rules, so a change of name or photo reaches it through `syncMyMemberProfileAction` (below): `updateUser` calls it after a profile save that touches `displayName` or `avatarUrl`, and `AuthProvider` calls it whenever a confirmed load finds the record's name or photo differing from the profile (`memberProfileDiffers`), which heals older drift. Both are best effort: a failure is logged and retried on the next load.

## Management Policy

`src/lib/household-policy.ts` is a pure module (no Firebase, no React). The server actions enforce it inside their transactions and the Household Manager uses the same functions to decide what to show.

- Rank: owner > admin > member > child = guest > pending (`newuser`). Unknown or legacy role names rank lowest and grant nothing.
- Effective permission: the role preset merged with the member's overrides (`getMemberPermissions`); the owner always has the full preset; pending or non-active people have nothing; `household.delete` and `household.transferOwnership` are forced off for everyone but the owner, whatever an override says.
- `household.view` and `household.manageInvites` work as ticked. A member granted `household.manageInvites` can create and revoke invite codes; an admin with it unticked cannot.
- View household (`household.view`, every approved role by default; the owner always): shows the Household Manager (page, header and phone navigation, command palette) and the AI home overview. In the rules (`hasHouseholdManagerPermission`), the reads that only the Household Manager makes need it as well as their own permission: invite codes (`household.manageInvites`), the activity log and other members' contact documents (`household.manageMembers`). With it unticked a member reads none of these, whatever else they hold. What stays readable by every approved member regardless: the household document and the member list (public member data: the names, photos and roles other features use for attribution, assignee pickers, the calendar, the dashboard and the palette; the rules grant whole documents, so they cannot be split by screen), the person's own record and own contact document, and each feature's data under its own permission (the member chore counts use `chores.view`). The management server actions check their own permissions (above), not `household.view`.
- `household.manageMembers`: approve pending people, change roles, remove members and rename the household. Member operations apply only to people whose rank is strictly lower than the actor's (never the owner, never an equal, never yourself), and the assigned role must be strictly below the actor's own rank (`owner` and `newuser` are never assignable).
- `household.manageRoles`: edit another member's permission overrides, with three guards: not your own; not someone at or above your rank; no granting (setting to true) a permission you do not effectively hold. Revoking is allowed under the same rank guard. Pending people have no permissions to edit.
- `household.delete` and `household.transferOwnership`: the owner only, always. They cannot be granted through overrides (the server refuses the save; no client can write a membership record at all).
- Each refusal has a typed reason (`PolicyRefusal`: `not_member`, `missing_permission`, `self`, `rank`, `role_not_assignable`, `role_too_high`, `owner_only`, `grant_unheld`, `target_pending`) and a plain-language sentence (`refusalMessage`).

## Household Creation

`createHouseholdAction` (`src/app/household/actions.ts` → `createHousehold` in `src/lib/server/household-membership.ts`) verifies the ID token, then runs one Admin SDK transaction:

- the name is trimmed and must be 1-80 characters (`readHouseholdName`: "Give your household a name." / "Keep it under 80 characters.", code `invalid_input`); the time zone is kept only when it is a valid IANA name of at most 64 characters (`normalizeTimeZone`), otherwise left out so upkeep uses its default;
- refused with `conflict` ("Leave your current household before creating a new one.") when the caller's profile points at a household where they have a membership record (active or pending); a stale pointer without a record does not block;
- the ID is the slugified name plus a four-character suffix from a cryptographic random source (`householdIdFor`); up to 8 candidates are read and the first free one is used, and the household and membership are written with `create`, so an existing household can never be overwritten;
- writes the household (`name`, `ownerUid`, `createdAt`, `updatedAt`, `timeZone`; no emails since release C), the owner membership (no email; `role: owner`, `status: active`, `approvedAt`, `approvedByUid`, and the name and photo from the HomeHub profile, falling back to the token, with an email-like name left out (`publicMemberName`); a photo removed in Profile (`avatarUrl: null`) stays removed, and the token photo is used only when the profile has no `avatarUrl` field; `membershipAvatarUrl`), the profile pointer (`householdId`, `role: owner`, `permissions: {}`) the owner's contact document (`{ email, updatedAt }`) and the `household.created` activity entry (`details.householdName`) together.

Identity comes only from the token. Two creations at once by the same person both read the profile, so one is retried and then refused; the person ends up in exactly one household. The action returns the household and membership, which `AuthProvider` puts into context without another read.

## Management Operations

All are server actions in `src/app/household/actions.ts` backed by `src/lib/server/household-membership.ts`. Each verifies the ID token (identity never comes from caller-supplied fields), then runs one Admin SDK transaction that re-reads the household and the actor's membership (and the target's), refuses when the household is being deleted or the policy says no, performs the writes and adds the activity-log entry in the same transaction. Results are `{ ok: true, data }` or `{ ok: false, code, message }`, where `code` is a policy reason or one of `unauthenticated`, `invalid_input`, `not_found`, `household_deleting`, `conflict`, `failed`.

|---|---|---|---|
| `renameHouseholdAction` | `household.manageMembers` | household `name`, `updatedAt` (name trimmed, 1-80 characters; an unchanged name writes nothing) | `household.renamed` (`householdName`, `previousName`) |

Activity-log entries written by these actions take `actorUid` and `actorEmail` from the verified token, `actorName` from the actor's membership record (falling back to the token), `targetUid`/`targetName` from the target's membership record and `targetEmail` from their contact document (`null` when there is none), both read in the transaction, and `createdAt` from the server clock (an ISO string, as before). Joining, leaving, creation and these operations are therefore all authored by the server; the current app writes no activity-log entry from the browser.

Removal keeps completed chores assigned to the person who did them (the earlier client batch unassigned those too, erasing history) and clears recurring templates the same way leaving does. Leaving and removal (not a denied request) also fold the person's email-derived yearly chore stats key into their uid key in every `stats/chores-{year}` document, in the same transaction (the email from their contact document; nothing derived from it is written to a document a client can read) and record their uid under the former-member key of that email in `households/{id}/ops/former-members` (server-only; no email), so the ops scripts credit their email-only completions to the same uid, so their completions stay one score after they leave (see [Chores](../chores/README.md), "Assignee identity"). Leaving and removal find assigned chores and templates by email (older records) and by uid; a record found by uid whose email now names someone else (an older build reassigned it by email only) is left alone. Open chores become `assignedToUid: ''`, `assignedToDisplayName: 'Unassigned'` (`unassignedChoreFields`) and lose `assignedToEmail`; templates lose `assignedToUid`, `assignedToEmail` and `recurrence.assignedToEmail`.

### Household writes are server-only in the rules

Every household-management write is made by the server actions above (Admin SDK, which the rules do not apply to), and `firestore.rules` denies the same writes from a browser:

- `households/{id}`: no client `create` or `delete`. The only client update is an owner or admin setting `timeZone` (with `updatedAt`; a string of at most 64 characters). `name`, `ownerUid`, `ownerEmail`, `memberEmails` and the legacy `inviteCode` field are server-written.
- `households/{id}/members/{uid}`: no client `create`, `update` or `delete`, including of your own record. Joining, approval, role and permission changes, removal, leaving, transfer and the name/photo sync all go through the server.
- `inviteCodes/{code}`: no client writes. Holders of `household.manageInvites` and `household.view` (role default or override) may read and list their own household's codes; nobody else may read one, and a `get` of a code that does not exist is denied.
- `households/{id}/auditLogs/{auditId}`: no client writes. Holders of `household.manageMembers` and `household.view` (role default or override) may read the log.
- `users/{email}` household fields (`householdId`, `role`, `permissions`): only the server writes them. The one client write is a person clearing their own stale pointer (`householdId: null`, `role: 'member'`, `permissions` empty or untouched), which `AuthProvider` does when the membership record is gone.

`tests/rules/firestore.rules.test.ts` checks that each of these client writes is denied, that records written with the rules disabled (as the server writes them) are readable by the people who need them, and that the remaining client writes (the time zone, clearing your own pointer, notifications) still pass.




The emulator E2E seed creates Household A and Household B, matching fake identities, a limited member, and a legacy profile-elevation fixture. Cross-household and conservative fallback checks complement the primary owner/admin/member/pending membership path.

## Data Model and Persistence

- Household: `households/{householdId}` with `name`, `ownerUid`, timestamps, and `timeZone` (older households may still carry the legacy `ownerEmail` and `memberEmails` until the scrub removes them). `timeZone` is captured from the creator's browser when the household is created (the rules let owners/admins change it, bounded to a 64-character string; no screen does so today). Server-side upkeep uses it for recurring-chore dates and maintenance due days; households without one use `America/Chicago`.
- Membership: `households/{householdId}/members/{uid}`: `uid`, `displayName`, `avatarUrl`, `role`, `status`, `permissions`, `joinedAt`, approval fields, and for people who joined by invite `inviteCode` and `householdName` (server-written; read by the person while pending). No `email` (older records keep one until the scrub removes it).
- Member contact: `households/{householdId}/members/{uid}/private/contact`: `email`, `updatedAt` (server-written; see Member contact).
- Temporary invite: `inviteCodes/{code}`.
- Audit: `households/{householdId}/auditLogs/{auditId}`.
- System events: `households/{householdId}/notifications/{notificationId}`.
- Legacy compatibility: `users/{email}.{householdId,role,permissions}`, and on older households `households.memberEmails` and `households.ownerEmail` (no longer written; scrubbed by `scripts/ops/scrub-shared-emails.mjs` and `scripts/ops/scrub-member-emails.mjs`).

Household IDs are slugified names plus a four-character suffix. Invite codes use readable `XXXX-XXXX-XX` values, expire after 30 minutes, and default to one use. The legacy optional `Household.inviteCode` field remains in the type for compatibility; it is not generated, grants nothing (joining uses only `inviteCodes`), and clients cannot remove it (the app used to delete it when an owner loaded the household).

## Authentication, Roles, and Security

Roles are `owner`, `admin`, `member`, `child`, `guest`, and system-only `newuser`. Legacy `super-admin` normalizes to owner and `user` to member. Permission presets and per-member overrides are defined in `src/lib/permissions.ts`.

Who may manage what is decided by the management policy above and enforced by the server actions. The rules deny every household-management write from a client (see Household writes are server-only in the rules), so no client can add an email to a household or a membership record, and keep the reads the app needs: approved members get and list memberships and read the household document; during a deletion (every membership set to status `deleting`), anyone whose record was approved (any role but `newuser`) can still get the household document and nothing else of the household (`isDeletingMember`), so a deletion that failed partway shows its frozen screen and the owner can Finish deleting (the server action checks owner and deleting state itself); pending users can read their own membership and their own contact document but not the household document or any household subcollection. A `users/{email}` profile is readable only by that person (the server reads profiles with the Admin SDK). A member's contact document is readable only by that person and by holders of `household.manageMembers` with `household.view`, and never writable from a client. Deleting your own record removes access; it never grants it.

Ownership invariants are kept by the server: a membership has role `owner` exactly when `households.ownerUid` points at it, the owner cannot be demoted, removed or leave, and ownership moves only through `transferOwnership`, one transaction that updates `ownerUid` and both membership records. No client can write either document.

Invite codes are created and revoked only through the server actions, for anyone holding `household.manageInvites`, and redemption (expiry, revocation, use count) is a server operation; the rules let only holders of `household.manageInvites` with `household.view` read their household's codes. No client may change another person's profile; the server keeps the household fields of profiles in step with membership changes.



## Integrations and Background Processing

- Governance events create system notifications and therefore can trigger FCM delivery.
- Pending-member reminders run on the server (`remindPendingMembersAction`) when someone with `household.manageMembers` opens Household Manager and a pending person is due; the page checks `pendingReminderDue` first so it calls the action only when there is something to send. Each person's daily state uses a deterministic notification identity, so overlapping visits, and an older browser doing the same, write one document. It is a page-triggered server action rather than part of the hourly `householdUpkeep` function so the reminder keeps its existing timing (a manager visiting), ships with the web app instead of a separate Functions deploy, and reuses the household transaction, policy and tests. No daily scheduled function exists.
- Household deletion is a server action (`deleteHouseholdAction`) that uses the Admin SDK's recursive delete; there is no Cloud Function or scheduled cleanup for it.

## Invariants and Failure Behavior

- `newuser` is not manually assignable and has no preset permissions.
- Owners cannot leave before transferring ownership or deleting the household; only the owner can delete, and the delete dialog stays disabled until the typed name matches. The server rechecks ownership and the name.
- Nobody but the owner can act on the owner; the owner's role changes only through an ownership transfer, and the owner cannot be removed.
- Every management operation re-reads the actor and the target inside its transaction, so a concurrent demotion, role change or removal is seen before anything is written.
- A role change resets the member's permission overrides.
- Household creation picks a free candidate ID inside its transaction and writes with `create`, so it never overwrites an existing household.
- Membership is live (see Live membership): an approval, a role or permission change, a removal and a household deletion take effect in an open session without a reload; permission denied on the person's own record ends access, a network error never does.
- Manager listeners fail independently, so denied invite/audit/chore queries never blank member data; a failed members listener shows a notice and the caller's own record.
- Member chore stats count chores assigned to the member (chores do not record who ticked them), keyed by member uid (`choreStatsByMember`: the chore's `assignedToUid`, or for older chores the member with that email). This week's completions are read with two single-field queries, the same pair of timestamps the Chores module's `completionInstant` reads: `completedAtServer >= weekStart` (the authoritative server stamp, a Timestamp) and the legacy `completedAt >= weekStart` (ISO string); documents missing one field are covered by the other. Open chores come from `isCompleted == false`. The three results are merged by document id (`mergeChoresById`), so a chore found twice counts once, and bucketed with `completionInstant` (`choreStatsByEmail`). Counts are shown only once all three listeners have answered for the current household and week and none failed (`choreStatsReady`); before that, or after a failure, member cards show no chore counts rather than 0. The week boundary moves at local Monday midnight (and is re-checked when the tab regains focus or visibility), which re-subscribes all three queries.
- An unsaved permission draft is tied to the member, their role and their stored overrides; a live role or permission change discards it.
- The permission editor never sends owner-only overrides; the server refuses them (`owner_only`), and the rules deny every client membership write.
- `/household?tab=<tab>` opens that tab, including when the page is already open (`overview`, `invites`, `members`, `roles`, `activity`, or `danger`); unknown values, and tabs the viewer cannot see, fall back to Overview. Choosing a tab replaces the URL with `?tab=<tab>` (Overview has no parameter), so refresh and Back keep it. Approval notifications deep-link to `/household?tab=members`.
- Join-request and member-left notifications are written server-side with one notification per event and active owner/admin (a rejoin creates new documents, so push fires again), each targeted by `targetUserUid`. The daily pending-member reminder, written by the `remindPendingMembersAction` server action, is household-wide (`audience: ['*']`), so it is shown and pushed to every approved member with `notifications.view` and names the person by display name only (never the email); push recipients come only from approved member records, never from `memberEmails`.
